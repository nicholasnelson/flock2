# Intrinsic motivation and homeostatic drives for RL agents (FLOCK 2.0 research notes)

Scope: survey of (a) homeostatic / drive-based reward formulations and (b) curiosity / intrinsic-motivation methods, their known pathologies and remedies, plus multi-agent curiosity, emergent circadian behaviour, long-horizon stability and animal-behaviour grounding. Every literature claim carries a link. Items marked **[speculation]** or listed under "Inferences" are my own reasoning, not sourced claims. The FLOCK 2.0 fit assessment is in the clearly labelled final section.

Research date: 2026-10-22. Note: several arXiv abstract pages returned only abstracts; where I could only read the abstract I say so.

## Q1. Homeostatic RL: drive-reduction reward, HRRL, allostasis, and avoiding single-drive gaming

### Takeaway
Keramati & Gutkin's HRRL defines reward as the *reduction* in a drive function D(H) (distance of internal state H from a set-point) and proves that maximising discounted reward is equivalent to minimising discounted drive; the formulation naturally yields risk aversion, anticipatory responses and avoidance of extreme states. Later work (Laurençon, Yoshida) adds continuous internal-state decay (allostasis) and fatigue/rest, and shows "difference of drive" is the best-performing reward variant in deep RL. Gaming one drive is countered by (i) a concave/norm drive function where deficits in one dimension cannot be bought off by surplus in another, (ii) inverted-U (over-satiation) penalties, and (iii) thresholded-lexicographic priority for safety-like drives.

### Cited Findings
- Core definition: drive is the distance between the current internal state and the set-point, computed as a sum of deviations across physiological variables raised to exponents n and m; reward is the reduction in drive caused by an outcome, r(H_t, K_t) = D(H_t) − D(H_t + K_t). For γ < 1, policies that minimise the sum of discounted drives are the same as those that maximise the sum of discounted rewards ("we mathematically prove that seeking rewards is equivalent to the fundamental objective of physiological stability"), via the identity SDR = D(H0) + (γ − 1)·SDD — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811) (also [PMC4270100](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4270100)); original NeurIPS 2011 version: [A Reinforcement Learning Theory for Homeostatic Regulation](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2011/file/9778d5d219c5080b9a6a17bef029331c-Paper.pdf)
- Discounting is necessary: with γ = 1 the value of a policy depends only on start and end internal state, not the path, so discounting is what makes the agent prefer the shortest path back to the set-point — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- Risk aversion emerges because reward is a concave function of outcome magnitude: the agent prefers a certain outcome over a risky one with equal expectation, because "frequent small deviations from the setpoint are preferable to rare drastic deviations" — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- Anticipatory (allostatic) responses: the model explains conditioned tolerance (cue-triggered pre-emptive warming offsets a predicted temperature drop), and the authors predict instrumental anticipatory responses, not only Pavlovian ones — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- "Stepping back from danger": from tabula rasa, 1-D simulated agents never visited states beyond a boundary, which the authors argue holds without the agent ever experiencing the extreme states — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- Known pathology (reward gaming by a drive-independent term): adding a drive-independent palatability term T makes "the behavior look like as if the subject is hungrier than what they really are"; the agent defends a set-point above the true one and over-consumes — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- The multi-dimensional case is stated to generalise ("can be generalized to multi-dimensional homeostatic spaces") but the paper's simulations are one-dimensional — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- Limitation of discrete HRRL and the allostasis fix: in the original theory "the internal state of the agent is assumed to be fixed when the agent does not take action, thus, the agent only has to reach its homeostatic set point once and take no more action"; Laurençon et al. add continuous internal-state dynamics so the agent "has a constant need to regulate itself", explicitly framed as allostasis ("living beings learn to act to predicatively ensure homeostasis (allostasis)"). They also add "the agent's internal energy levels, which need to be replenished by periods of rest that are caused by states of excessive fatigue (during which actions cannot be performed)" — [Laurençon, Ségerie, Lussange & Gutkin 2021, arXiv:2109.06580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06580)
- Same paper notes the earlier Konidaris & Barto approach: a reward that is "a sum of Hullian drive weighted by time-dependent coefficients indicating the priority of the drive" — [Laurençon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06580)
- Continuous-time/space extension (CTCS-HRRL) "Towards Biological Self-Autonomous Agent" — [arXiv:2401.08999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08999)
- Reward-definition comparison in deep RL motor control: "homeostatic rewards using the difference of the drive function performed best among the reward definitions compared" — [Yoshida et al. 2021, "Embodiment Perspective of Reward Definition for Behavioural Homeostasis", NeurIPS 2021 workshop](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2021/yoshida2021neuripsw-embodiment) (summary via search; I did not read the full text)
- Earlier Yoshida work reframes Ashby's homeostats as maximising multi-step survival probability — [Yoshida 2018, J. Artificial General Intelligence](https://reference-global.com/article/10.1515/jagi-2017-0001)
- 2025 perspective: "HRRL inherently produces multiple behaviors such as risk aversion, anticipatory regulation" and adaptive movement; the deep-RL extension "enables autonomous exploration, hierarchical behavior" — [Yoshida, Sprekeler & Gutkin 2025, arXiv:2507.04998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04998) (abstract only)
- Deep HRRL with two nutrient drives in MuJoCo: "The homeostasis reward is defined using the temporal difference of the error" between set-point and current state, scaled by α = 100 with small posture/torque costs; episodes end ("death") "if the agent's internal state s exceeded the unit hypercube boundaries". Pathologies observed: many agents died before the test horizon; trained agents "continued to collect blue food balls while experiencing an excess of red nutrients" (habit carry-over under train/test mismatch); one variant "approached the food nearly deterministically and periodically before stopping" (stereotypy); agents "do not learn new food conditions online" — [Yoshida et al. 2024, PNAS Nexus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635831/)
- Same paper: re-weighting the two drives moves emergent foraging between the Geometric-Framework "rules of compromise" (CD → NI), i.e. the relative weights of drives shape macro-behaviour — [Yoshida et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635831/); earlier short paper: [Arikawa et al., AMAM 2023](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/92317/P62-AMAM2023-revised.pdf)
- Avoiding single-drive gaming, benchmark perspective: homeostatic objectives should penalise "both deficiency and excess" (inverted-U), "excess in one resource dimension does not compensate for a deficit in another", "eating food does not properly compensate for the lack of drink, and vice versa", and naive linear summation "would be adequate only if the goods were 'perfect substitutes'". Pitfalls illustrated: "unboundedly maximizing a homeostatic objective, overoptimizing one objective at the expense of others". They cite Vamplew et al.'s thresholded lexicographic ordering: "first maximize the value of thresholded (safety) objectives, and then secondarily maximize the unthresholded (performance) objectives" — [Pihlakas & Pyykkö 2024/25, arXiv:2410.00081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00081)
- Lexicographic MORL with convergence proof: policies first optimise the top-priority signal, then lower ones only within the set of top-optimal behaviours; applied to safety constraints — [Skalse, Hammond, Griffin & Abate, IJCAI 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/476)
- Multi-agent homeostats: each agent is "a vulnerable homeostat charged with maintaining its own well-being"; prosocial behaviour arises only when a partner's internal state is coupled to one's own; agents trained with PPO + LSTM on exteroceptive plus interoceptive inputs — [Yoshida & Man 2024, arXiv:2412.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12103); [2025 version, arXiv:2506.12894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12894)

### Inferences
- Because HRRL reward is a *difference* of drive, an agent that sits at the set-point gets zero reward; the only sustained source of positive reward is decay-then-restore. With Laurençon-style continuous decay this gives a natural behavioural rhythm (graze until sated, wander, graze again) rather than a terminal "done" state. **[inference]**
- The Keramati palatability pathology is the generic warning for FLOCK: any additive reward term that is not routed through a drive (e.g. a raw curiosity bonus) will shift the effective set-point and can produce over-consumption-like behaviour (e.g. endless wandering). Routing extrinsic/intrinsic bonuses *through* drives, or capping them, is the structural fix. **[inference]**
- A Euclidean/L2-style drive with exponents > 1 (Keramati's m, n) is itself a mild anti-gaming device: the marginal reward for fixing the largest deficit exceeds that for topping up an already-small one, so a greedy agent naturally attends to its worst drive. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that combines explicit lexicographic ordering with HRRL drives; the closest are Pihlakas (benchmarks, cites Vamplew) and Skalse (lexicographic MORL, safety constraints), neither of which uses drive-reduction reward.
- Yoshida 2021 workshop paper: I only have a search-engine summary of the "difference of drive is best" finding; the full PDF could not be parsed.
- No source found giving concrete guidance on *fear* as a homeostatic drive (fear has no obvious set-point); SMiRL (Q3) is the nearest analogue.

## Q2. Curiosity methods (ICM, RND, Plan2Explore, BYOL-Explore, episodic, count-based), the noisy-TV problem, and what works at tiny scale

### Takeaway
Prediction-error curiosity (ICM) is attracted to irreducible stochasticity (noisy TV); RND, ensemble disagreement, aleatoric-variance subtraction and episodic/count bonuses are the documented remedies. Random features already work surprisingly well, and in gridworlds the empirical driver of success is a count-based episodic term — strong evidence that for 16×16 inputs and tiny models, simple hashed/episodic counts or RND are the right tier, not learned world-model curiosity.

### Cited Findings
- ICM: curiosity is "the error in an agent's ability to predict the consequence of its own actions" in "a visual feature space learned by a self-supervised inverse dynamics model", which "bypasses the difficulties of directly predicting pixels" and "ignores the aspects of the environment that cannot affect the agent"; evaluated in VizDoom and Super Mario Bros — [Pathak et al. 2017, arXiv:1705.05363](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05363)
- Large-scale study (54 environments, no extrinsic reward): "random features (RF) perform quite well across tasks" (beat a random agent in ~67% of Atari games); inverse-dynamics features beat RF in ~55% of games and generalised better to novel Mario levels; VAE features were "good but somewhat unstable"; raw pixels performed poorly — [Burda et al. 2019 (ICLR), arXiv:1808.04355](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04355) / [HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04355)
- Noisy TV: with stochastic transitions even a perfect model yields reward equal to the transition entropy, so "the agent will seek out transitions with the highest entropy"; a TV with a channel-change action added to a 3-D maze: "the presence of the TV drastically slows down learning", though "if you run the experiment for long enough the agents do sometimes converge". Other sources of the same problem: "a poor learning algorithm, a limited model class, or partial observability". Curiosity sometimes does worse than random (Atlantis, IceHockey) — [Burda et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04355)
- RND: bonus is the error of a predictor network trained to match a *fixed, randomly initialised* target network; because the target is deterministic the bonus does not stay high on unpredictable noise. In Montezuma's Revenge a forward-dynamics agent "oscillates between two rooms due to the non-determinism of sticky actions, resulting in an irreducibly high prediction error"; RND scored 8,152 vs 400 for the dynamics-based method — [Burda et al. 2018, Exploration by Random Network Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894) ([HTML](https://ar5iv.org/html/1810.12894)). Caveat: using identical predictor/target architectures and state-only novelty "can result in substantial inconsistencies in reward bonuses" — [arXiv:2401.09750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09750)
- Plan2Explore: novelty "estimated as ensemble disagreement in the latent space of the world model"; actions chosen to maximise expected information gain by *planning* in imagination; matches a task-reward oracle on 20 DM Control tasks from pixels — [Sekar et al. 2020, arXiv:2005.05960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05960); [BAIR blog](https://bair.berkeley.edu/blog/2020/10/06/plan2explore)
- BYOL-Explore: learns representation, dynamics and exploration policy from "a single prediction loss in the latent space with no additional auxiliary objective"; solves most DM-HARD-8 tasks and reaches superhuman on the ten hardest Atari exploration games — [Guo et al. 2022, arXiv:2206.08332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08332)
- Episodic curiosity through reachability: bonus if the current observation is more than k environment steps away from everything in an episodic memory; memory cleared at episode end; beats ICM in VizDoom/DMLab navigation — [Savinov et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.02274](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02274)
- Count-based with hashing: bonus β/√n(φ(s)) over hashed state buckets (SimHash as one LSH option); "results depend heavily on the hash function's granularity" — [Tang et al. 2017, arXiv:1611.04717](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04717). Pseudo-counts from a density model (PixelCNN) — [Ostrovski et al. 2017, arXiv:1703.01310](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.01310)
- Gridworld-scale evidence: BeBold uses a regulated difference of inverse visitation counts and solves the 12 hardest MiniGrid tasks within 120M steps — [Zhang et al. 2020, arXiv:2012.08621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08621); NovelD uses RND-based novelty difference at the explored/unexplored boundary — [NovelD, NeurIPS 2021](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/d428d070622e0f4363fceae11f4a3576-Paper.pdf); Henaff et al. show that removing the count-based episodic term from NovelD/RIDE/AGAC on MiniGrid "results in a failure to learn or makes learning much slower" — [Exploration via Elliptical Episodic Bonuses, arXiv:2210.05805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05805)
- Aleatoric Mapping Agents: predict next-state mean and diagonal aleatoric variance; intrinsic reward = squared prediction error minus scaled predicted variance ("To avoid stochastic traps we subtract the aleatoric uncertainty"). In MiniGrid an action-dependent noisy TV sharply reduced coverage for MSE-curiosity and RND, while "AMA agents are almost unaffected"; ensemble disagreement also held up. Limitations: "no guarantee that AMAs are able to surgically subtract those errors", unreliable out-of-distribution, "our method generates non-stationary rewards", hyperparameter-sensitive — [Mavor-Parker et al. ICML 2022, arXiv:2102.04399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04399)
- Surprise-novelty hybrid that "reduces unwanted attraction to unpredictable or noisy observations", tested on Noisy-TV, navigation and Atari — [Le et al., AAMAS 2024](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p1084.pdf)
- Surveys organising these methods (surprise, novelty, skill learning) under an information-theoretic taxonomy — [Aubret, Matignon & Hassas 2019, arXiv:1908.06976](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06976); [Entropy 2023 / arXiv:2209.08890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08890)

### Inferences
- On a 128×128 tile meadow with a 16×16 local view, the state space is small enough that *exact or hashed counts* over (tile-type pattern, time-of-day bucket) are feasible; the MiniGrid literature's dependence on an episodic count term suggests counts will outperform any learned-model curiosity at this scale. **[inference]**
- RND with a tiny MLP target is cheap and noise-robust and is the natural second choice if a learned signal is wanted; ensemble disagreement (Plan2Explore-style, but with 3–5 tiny MLPs) is the robust choice if a world model is already being trained for other reasons. **[inference]**
- Pure forward-prediction error (ICM-style) on raw 16×16 pixels is the one option the literature argues *against* for this setting: other sheep and the fox are action-independent stochastic sources, i.e. built-in noisy TVs. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No paper found that directly benchmarks curiosity methods on 16×16-pixel inputs with very small (few-thousand-parameter) models; the MiniGrid results are the nearest proxy (symbolic 7×7 partial views).
- Burda's "sometimes converge" statement for the noisy TV carries no numbers.

## Q3. Empowerment and surprise minimisation (SMiRL) as alternatives for prey-like agents

### Takeaway
SMiRL rewards the agent for making its observations *likely* under a running density model; it produces stabilising, danger-avoiding behaviour (dodging, bracing, escaping) without task reward, but only in environments that inject entropy; in static environments it "may trivially find low entropy policies", and it needs an exploration bonus to find distant safe states. Empowerment (channel capacity actions→sensors) is a task-independent "keep your options open" objective; primary sources on its behavioural consequences were only available as abstracts.

### Cited Findings
- SMiRL "alternates between learning a density model to evaluate the surprise of a stimulus, and improving the policy to seek more predictable stimuli"; it "seeks out stable and repeatable situations that counteract the environment's prevailing sources of entropy"; demonstrated on Tetris, Doom, humanoid balance and "avoiding other hostile agents" — [Berseth et al. ICLR 2021, arXiv:1912.05510](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05510)
- Density models used: per-cell Bernoulli (Tetris), 20-D Gaussian on downscaled images (VizDoom), Gaussian over pose/velocity (humanoid), VAE + diagonal Gaussian on latents for high-dimensional observations; the policy is "conditioned on the parameters of this distribution to construct a stationary MDP" — [SMiRL HTML](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.05510)
- Key caveat: in stable environments "SMiRL may trivially find low entropy policies", whereas in unstable ones "a novelty seeking agent is satisfied with watching the environment change around it" while the surprise-minimiser must acquire skills — [SMiRL HTML](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.05510)
- Failure modes: without an added counts-based exploration bonus the agent does not reach the safe room in HauntedHouse (a gym-minigrid task) and does not learn a gait on Treadmill; the agent may terminate episodes early to return to familiar states (authors used fixed-length episodes / "god mode"); "estimating surprise across episodes or over long sequences remains an open challenge"; results depend heavily on state representation — [SMiRL HTML](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.05510)
- Contrast with ICM/RND: in Tetris they "seek novelty by creating more and more distinct patterns of blocks rather than clearing them", and on humanoid tasks they jump off cliffs/pedestals; SMiRL reaches near-perfect Tetris with no deaths — [SMiRL HTML](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.05510)
- Empowerment: "an information-theoretic, task independent utility function ... defined as the channel capacity between an agent's actions and an agent's sensors"; the chapter gives "a fast approximation for empowerment in the continuous domain" and shows "the same formalism can lead to different observed behaviors" — [Salge, Glackin & Polani 2013, "Empowerment – an introduction", arXiv:1310.1863](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1863) (abstract only)

### Inferences
- A FLOCK meadow is mostly *stable* (the main entropy sources are the nightly fox, weather/day-night and other sheep). Per SMiRL's own analysis, a pure surprise-minimiser risks the trivial low-entropy policy — standing in a corner — which is exactly the degenerate behaviour the brief wants to avoid. SMiRL is therefore a candidate *fear/vigilance* component, not the sole intrinsic reward. **[inference]**
- SMiRL's density model over a tiny observation (e.g. counts of fox/sheep/tree pixels in the 16×16 view, or a 20-D projection as in their VizDoom setup) is cheap enough for tens of agents. **[inference]**
- Empowerment evaluated by enumeration over a small discrete action set and a few-step horizon on a tile grid would be tractable, but there is no published evidence it yields lifelike prey behaviour; treat as optional. **[speculation]**

### Gaps
- Could not access the empowerment chapter body; no citation available here for the "avoids death/traps" behavioural claims commonly attributed to it.
- No paper found that applies SMiRL to prey/predator ecology specifically; the "avoiding other hostile agents" result is in VizDoom-style settings.

## Q4. Curiosity in multi-agent settings: does other agents' behaviour cause pathological curiosity?

### Takeaway
The literature agrees that other agents are a source of irreducible unpredictability for prediction-error curiosity (the same mechanism as the noisy TV), and Burda's two-curious-agents Pong experiment shows curious agents will actively *prolong interaction* with each other. Published remedies are mostly cooperative-MARL (joint/collective novelty, context-calibrated curiosity) rather than ecological; the single-agent noisy-TV remedies (RND, aleatoric subtraction, counts) are the transferable fixes.

### Cited Findings
- Two curiosity-driven agents controlling both Pong paddles (no extrinsic reward): rallies grew longer over training until "the rallies eventually became so long that they broke the Atari emulator"; the appendix notes the agent maximises ball bounces — [Burda et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04355)
- Partial observability and limited model class produce the same high-entropy attraction as true stochasticity — [Burda et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04355)
- Survey: an agent's own prediction error "quantifies only one agent's knowledge gap and ignores what other agents may already know", proposing collective novelty (minimum of individual errors, or a joint curiosity module) — [Exploration in Deep RL: From Single-Agent to Multiagent Domain, arXiv:2109.06668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06668)
- Mixed-objective curiosity: a two-headed module predicts the agent's next observation and the next joint observation, reward = sum of both errors — [arXiv:2210.16468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16468)
- CERMIC: "Existing curiosity mechanisms exhibit a uniform novelty bias, treating all unexpected observations equally"; it calibrates intrinsic curiosity "with inferred multi-agent context" in decentralised, no-communication settings, with gains on VMAS, Meltingpot, SMACv2 — [Wonder Wins Ways, arXiv:2509.20648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20648)
- Non-stationarity of multi-agent systems "makes it impracticable to apply existing single-agent methods directly" (fuzzy partition of next joint state) — [NWPU fuzzy curiosity paper](https://pure.nwpu.edu.cn/en/publications/a-fuzzy-curiosity-driven-mechanism-for-multi-agent-reinforcement-/) (abstract-level)
- Aleatoric subtraction explicitly targets transitions that are unpredictable "even if those dynamics are induced by the actions of the agent" — [Mavor-Parker et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04399)

### Inferences
- In FLOCK, a forward-model curiosity sheep would be rewarded for staring at / following other sheep and the fox, because their moves are unpredictable from its 16×16 view (sheep-chasing-sheep is the Pong-rally pathology in ecological clothing). Remedies, in order of simplicity: (1) compute curiosity on a *masked* observation that excludes other agents (an ICM-inverse-dynamics-like "controllable only" view by construction); (2) use count/RND novelty over static-world features only; (3) aleatoric-variance subtraction if a learned predictor is kept. **[inference]**
- The social drive should be the *only* channel through which other sheep affect reward; curiosity should be blind to them. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- I found no paper that explicitly reports "agents chase other agents because of curiosity" as a named pathology in an ecological/prey setting; the Pong rally result is the closest published evidence.

## Q5. Emergent circadian / sleep behaviour from drives

### Takeaway
There is one directly relevant ICML paper (Labash et al. 2023) showing deep-RL foragers develop an endogenous, entrainable day/night rhythm — but the rhythm is induced by an explicit night-time penalty for leaving home, not by a warmth/energy drive. Laurençon et al. put fatigue-driven forced rest into continuous HRRL, and the ethology literature gives the target pattern (dawn/dusk grazing peaks, rumination after midnight).

### Cited Findings
- Setup: 5×5 gridworld (APES) with a binary daylight signal; "at nighttime, we impose a negative reward if the agent leaves a specific safe location ... the home location", chosen so "the optimal strategy ... is to forage in the food area at daytime and to stay at home at night". Result: "the emergence of a rhythm that is endogenous and entrainable", which "adapts to shifts in the phase of the environmental signal without any re-training", mechanistically "the emergence of a stable periodic orbit in the neuron dynamics" — [Labash, Stelzer, Majoral & Vicente, ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/labash23a.html) ([PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/labash23a/labash23a.pdf))
- Continuous HRRL explicitly models "internal energy levels, which need to be replenished by periods of rest that are caused by states of excessive fatigue (during which actions cannot be performed)" — [Laurençon et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06580)
- Keramati & Gutkin's conditioned-tolerance simulation shows HRRL agents learn cue-triggered *anticipatory* thermoregulation — [Keramati & Gutkin 2014](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)
- Real sheep (stall-fed): eating concentrated in daylight "especially after sunrise and before sunset", rumination peaking after midnight; ~7 h/day eating, rumination bouts of 14 min (lying) / 9 min (standing) recurring every 2–3 h — [PMC6205656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6205656)
- Merino sheep on pasture: alternating rest and grazing periods of about two hours during the night; grazing stops at ~18–20 °C in the morning and resumes at ~36–40 °C in the afternoon in July/August — [Observaciones sobre el efecto de la luz y la temperatura ... merinos](https://helvia.uco.es/handle/10396/3286)
- Ruminants tend to fill the rumen in the evening because little grazing occurs overnight — [Circadian Patterns of Ingestion and Rumination in Ruminants: review](https://www.pms.lifescienceglobal.com/index.php/JBS/article/view/5950)

### Inferences
- Nobody has published "sleep emerges purely from a warmth/energy drive"; Labash needed an explicit night penalty. For FLOCK the equivalent is a *warmth* drive whose decay rate rises at night and away from treeline/huddle, plus an *energy/fatigue* variable restored only while resting — this makes rest the drive-reducing action at night without hard-coding it. **[inference]**
- The ethological target is not "sleep all night" but ~2-hour rest/graze alternation with dawn/dusk grazing peaks; a decaying hunger drive with a nightly fear/vigilance term reproduces this shape naturally. **[speculation]**

### Gaps
- No work found where resting/sleeping behaviour emerged from an energy drive alone in an RL agent (Laurençon's rest is enforced by a fatigue state in which actions cannot be performed).

## Q6. Long-horizon stability: non-stationary drive rewards, loss of plasticity, continual single-agent learning over months

### Takeaway
Deep networks trained continually lose plasticity (Dohare et al., Nature 2024: performance decays toward a shallow/linear network over thousands of tasks); Adam, dropout and normalisation can worsen it. Remedies with published support: continual backprop (reinitialise low-utility units), L2 / weight decay, shrink-and-perturb, periodic resets of final layers (Nikishin), CReLU, LayerNorm + weight decay in combination. Mavor-Parker and Keramati both flag that drive/curiosity rewards are intrinsically non-stationary.

### Cited Findings
- Standard deep-learning methods in continual settings "slowly become less able to learn, eventually performing about like a shallow network"; continual backpropagation reinitialises "a small fraction of less-used units"; the authors conclude gradient descent alone is insufficient and "sustained learning needs a random, non-gradient component" — [Dohare et al. 2024, Nature, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7) (summaries: [Amii](https://www.amii.ca/research/loss-of-plasticity-in-deep-continual-learning)); preprint numbers: ImageNet binary-classification accuracy fell from 89% on early tasks to 77% (≈ linear network) by the 2000th task; L2 regularisation substantially eased the loss, especially with weight perturbation — [arXiv:2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812)
- Same line of work found dropout and normalisation "worsen the loss of plasticity", and Adam showed "a dramatic loss of plasticity" — reported in the plasticity search results citing Dohare et al. ([arXiv:2306.13812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812))
- Continual deep RL (Atari games in sequence): value-based agents lose the ability to learn good policies; mechanism is activations becoming sparser, shrinking gradients; CReLU helps — [Abbas, Zhao, Modayil, White & Machado, CoLLAs 2023, arXiv:2303.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07507)
- Primacy bias: deep RL agents "rely on early interactions and ignore useful evidence encountered later"; remedy is periodically reinitialising the last few layers while preserving the replay buffer; SPR+resets IQM 0.478 vs 0.380 on Atari 100k — [Nikishin et al. ICML 2022, arXiv:2205.07802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07802)
- On-policy RL: a continual version of shrink+perturb "consistently resolved plasticity loss in gridworld tasks and in ProcGen and ALE"; LayerNorm fixes training plasticity but is "inconsistent in its effect on generalization"; CReLU and plasticity injection "less effective" — [A Study of Plasticity Loss in On-Policy Deep RL, arXiv:2405.19153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19153)
- "Intervening on any single mechanism is insufficient ... intervening on multiple mechanisms in conjunction results in highly robust learning algorithms"; best combination layer normalisation + weight decay — [Lyle et al. 2024, Disentangling the Causes of Plasticity Loss, arXiv:2402.18762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18762)
- Intrinsic rewards are non-stationary by construction: "our method generates non-stationary rewards, which is known to make learning difficult for RL agents" — [Mavor-Parker et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04399); count/RND bonuses decay as states are visited (by definition, [Tang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04717), [RND](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12894))
- HRRL agents in the PNAS Nexus study "do not learn new food conditions online" and showed habit carry-over when conditions changed — [Yoshida et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635831/)

### Inferences
- Drive rewards themselves are *stationary* in the HRRL sense once the internal state is part of the observation (Keramati's reward is a deterministic function of (H, outcome)); what is non-stationary is the *curiosity* bonus and the agent's own data distribution. Keeping the curiosity term small and decaying (or episodic/count-based, which self-decays) bounds the non-stationarity. **[inference]**
- For a months-long run with tiny networks, the cheapest robust recipe from this literature: SGD or Adam with weight decay + LayerNorm, continual-backprop-style reinitialisation of dormant units, and periodic reset of the policy head with a retained replay buffer. Alternatively (and simpler for an art piece): freeze learning after a convergence phase, or run learning in slow epochs with checkpoint/rollback on behavioural-metric regression. **[inference]**
- Tabular or linear-on-hashed-features learners sidestep plasticity loss entirely (the phenomenon is a deep-network pathology); given a 16×16 view and tile world this is a real option. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No published continual-RL study at the "single agent, months of wall-clock, tiny network" scale; all evidence is from task-sequence benchmarks.
- Dohare et al.'s exact Nature figures were not read from the article itself (press summaries and preprint used).

## Q7. Animal-behaviour grounding: sheep/herbivore foraging and vigilance

### Takeaway
Sheep show a robust group-size effect: individual vigilance falls and foraging rises with group size, mediated by strong imitation of scanning (ε_S ≈ 0.92) and weaker imitation of foraging (ε_F ≈ 0.47), with spontaneous switching rates of order 0.002–0.05 s⁻¹; cortisol also falls with group size. Sheep alternate long dispersed grazing phases with fast "packing" events, which an allelomimetic agent-based model reproduces. These give concrete parameters for a social/fear drive and for vigilance-as-contagion.

### Cited Findings
- Michelena & Deneubourg 2011 (Merino sheep, groups of 2–8, 25 m arena): individual scanning fell with group size, "driven by longer foraging bouts and shorter scanning bouts"; imitation stronger for scanning (ε_S = 0.92) than foraging (ε_F = 0.47), r² = 0.86; spontaneous switching k_S ≈ 0.016–0.051 s⁻¹, k_F ≈ 0.002–0.025 s⁻¹, k_F ≈ 0.46·k_S; "for high imitation (ε_S + ε_F > 1), the level of collective vigilance decreases in large groups"; strong imitation of scanning "produces rare but long periods with no one scanning"; group "tempo" does not depend on group size — [PLoS ONE 2011, PMC3078120](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3078120)
- Sheep in groups of 2–100: "Both vigilance and average cortisol concentration decreased as group size increased", with the hypothesis that "group size on its own acts as a stressor" — [Group size elicits specific physiological response in herbivores, INRAE HAL](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-02646841)
- Meta-analysis across mammals: "a moderate negative effect of group size (r = −0.44), but 43% of the effect sizes were compatible with a null effect" — [A meta-analysis of the group-size effect on vigilance in mammals](https://commons.emich.edu/fac_sch2021/4)
- Bighorn sheep: vigilance declines with group size up to ~6 — [CDFW Bighorn natural history](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Mammals/Bighorn-Sheep/Desert/Natural-History/Behavior); larger groups "used areas with more forage biomass and greater predation risk" — [Behav Ecol Sociobiol 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-009-0840-4)
- Merino flocks show "long dispersion phases while grazing, interrupted by fast packing events triggered by individual behavioural shifts"; an agent-based model reproduces this "when allelomimetic parameters are large enough" — [Ginelli et al. PNAS 2015](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/files/58184052/PNAS_2015_Ginelli_12729_34.pdf)
- Penned sheep grazed less alone or in very small groups, but increasing from 4 to 5, 6 or 10 did not further increase grazing time — reported in [PMC6205656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6205656)
- Three-state (grazing/flocking/moving) agent-based flock model with close-range repulsion and longer-range alignment causing spread and sub-group splitting — [Welch et al., MODSIM 2025](https://mssanz.org.au/modsim2025/files/B09.welch.pdf)
- Friction-based social-force model in which a sheep moves only when social + food-attraction forces exceed the "friction" of its current patch — [Li & Jiang, Ecological Modelling 2014 (SEU PDF)](https://cs.seu.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/55/b3/f8c39a1347f684f5c3fbd4067cd8/3dba0746-c597-4148-aa32-644a807b3b0b.pdf)
- Homeostatic RL reproduces animal foraging "rules of compromise" between nutrients — [Yoshida et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635831/); [Arikawa et al. AMAM 2023](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/92317/P62-AMAM2023-revised.pdf)

### Inferences
- The social drive's set-point can be grounded directly: fear/stress is a decreasing function of visible-conspecific count with saturation around ~6–10 (bighorn and penned-sheep data), and vigilance should be *contagious* (ε_S ≈ 0.9) more than grazing is (ε_F ≈ 0.5). **[inference]**
- Ginelli's dispersion-then-packing dynamic is what a viewer reads as "a flock"; it arises from allelomimesis, which drives alone will not produce unless the social drive responds to *neighbour state changes* (a neighbour startling), not just neighbour distance. **[inference]**

### Gaps
- No published RL or drive-based model of *sheep* specifically; the computational ethology models are stochastic switching or social-force models, not learning agents.

## FLOCK 2.0 FIT ASSESSMENT (own analysis — speculation, not sourced unless linked)

**Recommended minimal combination (simplest likely to be lifelike):**

1. **Reward = HRRL drive difference, nothing additive outside it.** r_t = D(H_t) − D(H_{t+1}) with D the L2 (or m,n > 1) norm of deviations of (hunger, thirst, warmth, social, fear) from set-points, per [Keramati & Gutkin](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811) and the deep-RL evidence that difference-of-drive performs best ([Yoshida 2021](https://mlanthology.org/neuripsw/2021/yoshida2021neuripsw-embodiment), [Yoshida 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11635831/)). Give every drive continuous decay (allostasis, [Laurençon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06580)) so a sated sheep is never "done". Make warmth decay faster at night/away from treeline and recover only while resting, which is the closest the literature gets to drive-driven rest ([Laurençon fatigue](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06580); explicit night penalty in [Labash](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/labash23a.html)).
2. **Anti-gaming structure, not weights.** Use the inverted-U / non-substitutable form ([Pihlakas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00081)): a norm with exponent > 1 makes the worst deficit dominate; add over-satiation penalties for hunger/thirst (prevents a sheep parking on a patch). Treat *fear* as a thresholded top-priority objective ([Vamplew-style thresholded lexicographic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00081), [Skalse](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/476)): when fear exceeds θ, only fear-reducing actions are scored; below θ it drops out. Keep *social* set-point grounded in the ethology (satiates around 6–10 visible conspecifics).
3. **Curiosity: count-based, blind to other agents.** The 16×16/tile scale argues for episodic hashed counts over static-world features (tile types, time-of-day bucket, position), the term that the MiniGrid literature shows is the one that matters ([Henaff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05805)). Exclude other sheep and the fox from the curiosity observation entirely to avoid the Pong-rally / noisy-TV pathology ([Burda](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04355)); route the bonus into an "interest" drive (a decaying variable that curiosity satisfies) rather than adding it to reward directly, so it cannot shift effective set-points (the palatability pathology, [Keramati](https://elifesciences.org/articles/04811)). If a learned curiosity term is wanted later, RND on a tiny MLP is the noise-robust choice; avoid ICM-style forward error on raw pixels.
4. **Vigilance/fear as surprise-minimisation, locally.** SMiRL's "make observations likely" objective with a tiny Gaussian/Bernoulli density over the fox/motion channels of the 16×16 view is a principled fear term ([Berseth](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1912.05510)), but must be *bounded to those channels* and gated by the lexicographic threshold; as the sole reward it would collapse to standing still (SMiRL's own stable-environment caveat). Add allelomimetic coupling (a startled neighbour raises fear, ε ≈ 0.9 for scanning per [Michelena](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3078120)) to get Ginelli-style packing events.
5. **Months-long stability.** Prefer the smallest learner that works: tabular/linear over hashed features avoids plasticity loss altogether. If a small network is used, adopt weight decay + LayerNorm, dormant-unit reinitialisation ([Dohare](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7)), periodic head resets with buffer retained ([Nikishin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07802)), and shrink-and-perturb ([on-policy study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19153)); plus a behavioural watchdog (time-fraction standing still, mean path entropy, drive-violation rate) with checkpoint rollback. Consider freezing learning after a convergence phase and running only slow, bounded online adaptation.

**What to avoid, with the published reason:** pure ICM/forward-error curiosity (noisy-TV / other-agent chasing); pure SMiRL (corner-standing in a stable meadow); linear sum of drives with hand weights (perfect-substitutes gaming); additive uncapped curiosity (set-point drift); Adam + dropout + normalisation without a plasticity remedy over months (plasticity loss).

**Degenerate-behaviour map:** standing still forever → over-satiation penalty + continuous drive decay + count bonus; chasing noise/other sheep → agent-blind curiosity + RND/counts; fleeing endlessly → fear threshold with hysteresis and decay; ignoring night → warmth decay tied to night/exposure and restored by rest; forgetting how to graze after months → plasticity remedies or frozen core policy.
