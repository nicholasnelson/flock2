# Iterated Learning, Cultural Transmission and Generational Learning in Agent Populations (survey for FLOCK 2.0)

Scope note: this survey covers (1) the classic iterated-learning model (ILM) and human experiments, (2) neural/computational iterated learning, (3) iconicity-to-symbol drift in graphical/gestural/acoustic systems, (4) cultural transmission of *behaviour* in RL, (5) inheritance mechanisms in neuroevolution (Lamarckian/Darwinian/Baldwin), (6) practical generational turnover, (7) watchability/metrics, and (8) a clearly separated FLOCK 2.0 fit assessment. Every literature claim carries a DOI/arXiv/repository link. Where I rely on an abstract or a secondary summary rather than the full text, I say so. Items marked **[speculation]** or **[inference]** are my own.

Research date: 2026-10-22. Sources were located via web search; full texts fetched for Ren et al. 2020, Li & Bowling 2019 and Bhoopchand et al. 2023. Other papers are cited from abstracts/repository records unless stated.

## Key Question 1: What exactly produces structure in Kirby-style iterated learning — the bottleneck, expressivity pressure, or both?

### Takeaway
The transmission (learning) bottleneck alone produces *compressible* languages, but left to itself it collapses them toward underspecified/degenerate systems; *compositional* structure requires both a compressibility pressure (from learning through a bottleneck) and an expressivity pressure (from communication). This is the central empirical result of Kirby, Tamariz, Cornish & Smith (2015) and is now the consensus framing.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition of iterated learning:** "the process by which the output of one individual's learning becomes the input to other individuals' learning". The models show that compositionality emerges because learners must generalise from sparse input; "only a language which is generalizable from sparse input data is stable" through the bottleneck. — [Smith, Kirby & Brighton 2003, *Artificial Life* 9:371–386 (PDF)](https://cse.iitk.ac.in/users/hk/cs784/papers/iteratedLearningFrameworkForEmergenceOfLang.pdf); companion paper [Advances in Complex Systems 6(4), 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wsi/acsxxx/v06y2003i04ns0219525903001055.html)
- **Kirby 2001 (IEEE Trans. Evol. Comp.)** is the original computational ILM in which a bottleneck on transmission causes regular (compositional) mappings to be preferentially preserved and irregularity to persist only for frequent meanings. — [doi:10.1109/4235.918430](https://doi.org/10.1109/4235.918430) *(DOI from memory; not fetched this session — see Gaps)*
- **Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008 (PNAS):** first human iterated-learning experiment. Artificial languages passed down chains of learners "became easier to learn and more structured over generations", with structure arising from transmission itself, not from any learner's intent ("apparent design without a designer"). — [doi:10.1073/pnas.0707835105 / PMC2504810](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2504810). In the 2008 study the languages in the no-filter condition became highly *underspecified* (many meanings sharing one string); only when homonyms were filtered out of the training set did compositional structure appear (this filtering acted as a proxy for an expressivity pressure). *(Detail of the filter condition from the PNAS paper as I recall it; the PMC record confirms the headline result but I did not re-read the filtered-condition section — see Gaps.)*
- **Kirby, Tamariz, Cornish & Smith 2015 (Cognition 141:87–102):** "structure arises from a trade-off between pressures for compressibility (imposed during learning) and expressivity (imposed during communication)", shown with both simulations of rational (Bayesian) learners and lab experiments. A learning-only chain condition produced degenerate languages; conditions where pairs learned the language and then used it to communicate (taking turns describing pictures and identifying the partner's pictures) produced structured languages. — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2015.03.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.03.016); experimental-condition description via [Heriot-Watt record](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/compression-and-communication-in-the-cultural-evolution-of-lingui/) and [secondary summary (cognitionandculture.net)](https://cognitionandculture.net/webinars/speaking-our-minds-book-club/communication-culture-and-biology-in-the-evolution-of-language). The three outcome classes I recall from the paper are: **learning-only chains → degenerate/underspecified**, **communication-only closed groups (no new learners) → holistic (expressive but not compressible)**, **chains with both learning and communication → compositional**. *(The three-way outcome is from my reading of the paper; the search sources confirm the first and third but did not quote the holistic/closed-group result verbatim.)*
- **Bayesian account:** Griffiths & Kalish show that with Bayesian learners the stationary distribution of iterated learning converges to the learners' *prior*, i.e. transmission chains reveal and amplify inductive biases. — [Griffiths & Kalish 2007, *Cognitive Science* (PDF)](https://cogsci.syr.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/GriffithsK2007.pdf)
- **Replication at scale:** Beckner, Pierrehumbert & Hay reran the 2008 paradigm with 24 independent chains × 10 generations on Mechanical Turk; compositionality rises under iterated learning but "the trend levels off before the 10th generation"; the apparent decline in the original 4-chain data is attributed to small-sample chance. Systematic units emerge along some meaning dimensions before others. — [doi:10.1093/jole/lzx001](https://doi.org/10.1093/jole/lzx001); [Oxford repository](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:48356b4a-41c9-43c9-95b2-d231b197f3d5)
- **Community size (no chain, interaction only):** larger groups of interacting participants developed more systematic languages, faster and more consistently; small groups varied more, "suggesting that small communities are more vulnerable to drift". — [Raviv, Meyer & Lev-Ari 2019, Proc. R. Soc. B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2019.1262](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6661353). Companion study found no effect of network structure. — [MPI record](https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item3138714/larger-communities-create-more-systematic-languages)
- **Grounded computational version:** Vogt (2005) combined ILM, language games and Talking Heads; "a transmission bottleneck serves as a pressure mechanism for the emergence of compositionality", emerging languages "reflect the structure of the world to a large extent", and co-developing semantics with competitive selection sped up compositionality versus predefined semantics. — [Vogt 2005, *Artificial Intelligence* 167:206–242](https://datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0004-3702/volumes-and-issues/298/paper-detail/1828)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** For FLOCK, the two pressures map onto two mechanisms: lambs seeing only a *sample* of elder bleats/drawings (bottleneck → compressibility) and sheep needing signals to actually *discriminate* referents in a task (communication → expressivity). Omit the second and the language should degrade toward a few all-purpose bleats; omit the first and you get an expressive but holistic, unlearnable "code" that resets with every lamb.
- **[inference]** Beckner et al.'s plateau suggests structure saturates within ~5–10 generations; a months-long installation will spend most of its life in a *maintained* regime, so mechanisms for continued drift/innovation matter as much as initial emergence.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the full text of Kirby et al. 2015 (ScienceDirect 403) so the exact MDL/compressibility and expressivity measures, stimulus space (I recall 3 shapes × 3 colours × 3 motions = 27 meanings in 2008) and chain lengths are not quoted here. The paper is CC-BY; the writer should pull numbers from the DOI.
- Kirby 2002 ("Learning, bottlenecks and the evolution of recursive syntax", in Briscoe ed.) has no DOI I could verify; omitted from cited findings.

## Key Question 2: Neural iterated learning — mechanisms and results

### Takeaway
Every neural variant implements the same skeleton: periodically re-initialise (or replace) some agents; have the newcomer *imitate* the incumbent's object→message data for a *limited* number of steps (the bottleneck); then let agents *interact* on the task; then sample the new agents' outputs as the next generation's training data. Limited imitation amplifies the network's inductive bias for simpler (more compositional, higher topographic-similarity) mappings; results consistently show higher topographic similarity, faster teaching and better generalisation than no-reset baselines, with sensitivity to the imitation budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Ren, Guo, Labeau, Cohen & Kirby 2020 (ICLR), "Compositional languages emerge in a neural iterated learning model".** Setup: referential game, speaker (Alice) → fixed-length message → listener (Bob) picks target from candidates. Each generation: (1) *learning phase* — Alice and Bob re-initialised; Alice pre-trained with cross-entropy on pairs sampled from the previous generation's dataset D_i for I_a iterations; Bob pre-trained with REINFORCE against the pre-trained Alice for I_b iterations; (2) *interacting phase* — both play I_g rounds and update with REINFORCE (filters out ambiguous languages); (3) *transmitting phase* — Alice samples messages for objects to form D_{i+1}. 80–100 generations. Results: compositional (topographic similarity ρ=1) languages learned faster by both agents than ρ=0.21 languages; resetting both agents gave converged ρ≈0.93 vs 0.23 with no reset; validation accuracy on held-out objects 0.847 (reset both) vs 0.136 (no reset); validation performance correlated with ρ at r=0.928; the beneficial pre-training window was ~1000–2000 iterations for Alice and ~100–200 for Bob — outside that range both ρ and success fall. Probabilistic explanation: under a uniform prior high-ρ languages are rare; because they are learned *faster*, a weakly pre-trained Alice's distribution favours them more than D_i does; the interacting phase removes ambiguous languages without changing expected ρ; sampling D_{i+1} carries the higher expected ρ forward, so expected ρ is non-decreasing across generations. "The limited pre-training acts as a bottleneck, analogous to the bottleneck in human iterated-learning studies." — [arXiv:2002.01365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01365); [ICLR 2020 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2020/poster/1771)
- **Li & Bowling 2019 (NeurIPS), "Ease-of-teaching and language structure from emergent communication".** 32 objects (8 colours × 4 shapes), 2-symbol messages from an 8-symbol vocabulary, listener picks from 5. Only *listeners* are reset; single listener replaced every 6k iterations for 50 resets; staggered populations of N=2 (one replaced every 3k) and N=10 (one every 600). Metrics: ease-of-teaching (train 30 fresh listeners on the frozen speaker) and topographic similarity (negative Spearman correlation between meaning distances and message edit distances). Results: both reset and no-reset reach ~97% success, but reset languages are easier to teach and topographic similarity rises to ~0.59 (vs ~0.51–0.53 no-reset). Ease-of-teaching ranking: sequential N=1 > staggered N=2 > staggered N=10 > no-reset; *larger staggered populations smooth the training change and reduce the pressure*, behaving closer to no-reset; simultaneous reset of all N=10 restores the benefit (ρ≈0.59). Proposed mechanism: abrupt listener replacement spikes the speaker's policy entropy, which pushes the speaker toward more teachable languages (presented as plausible, not proven). Failure modes: success drops to ~20% at each full reset (55% with N=2); languages plateau at ρ≈0.6 vs 1.0 for a hand-built compositional language; with higher listener-entropy weight no-reset training occasionally collapsed to 20%. — [arXiv:1906.02403](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02403); [NeurIPS 2019 proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/b0cf188d74589db9b23d5d277238a929-Abstract.html)
- **Cogswell, Lu, Lee, Parikh & Batra 2019/ICLR 2020, "Emergence of compositional language with deep generational transmission".** No explicit teacher→student imitation: agents in a population are periodically replaced (re-initialised), creating a knowledge gap that "implicitly induc[es] cultural transmission of language"; this yields better compositional generalisation; explicit transmission models are flagged as future work. — [arXiv:1904.09067](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09067)
- **Guo, Ren, Havrylov, Frank, Titov & Smith 2019, "The emergence of compositional languages for numeric concepts through iterated learning in neural agents"** (iterated learning applied to a numeric-concept referential game; found via the search snippet only). — [arXiv:1910.05291](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05291) *(abstract-level; not fetched)*
- **Lu, Singhal, Strub, Courville & Pietquin 2020 (ICML), "Countering language drift with seeded iterated learning" (SIL).** Problem: agents fine-tuned for task success drift, "slowly losing syntactic and semantic properties of language". Method: periodically copy the student to make a *teacher*, fine-tune the teacher on the task for a while, then have a fresh/pretrained student *imitate data sampled from the teacher*; "requires no external syntactic constraint or semantic knowledge". Tested in the Lewis game and a natural-language translation game; counters drift and improves task completion over baselines. — [arXiv:2003.12694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12694); [PMLR v119](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/lu20c.html)
- **Vani, Schwarzer, Lu, Dhekane & Courville 2021 (ICLR oral), "Iterated learning for emergent systematicity in VQA".** Treats neural-module-network layouts as an emergent language and applies iterated learning (with regularisation) so that compositional program structure emerges without gold layouts; outperforms non-IL baselines on SHAPES-SyGeT and CLOSURE and recovers ground-truth program structure with limited supervision. — [arXiv:2105.01119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01119); [ICLR 2021](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/oral/3425)
- **Rita, Tallec, Michel, Grill, Pietquin, Dupoux & Strub 2022 (NeurIPS), "Emergent communication: generalization and overfitting in Lewis games".** Decomposes the Lewis-game objective into a *co-adaptation loss* and an *information loss*; identifies two sources of overfitting that "may undermine the emergence of a structured communication protocol"; controlling overfitting in the co-adaptation loss yields more compositional, better-generalising languages. — [arXiv:2209.15342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15342); [NeurIPS 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/093b08a7ad6e6dd8d34b9cc86bb5f07c-Abstract.html)
- **Population size and heterogeneity.** Rita et al. 2022 (ICLR) note the sociolinguistic claim "the larger the population, the more structured the language" "has not been consistently reproduced in computer simulations with neural agents"; they vary population size and then challenge the homogeneity assumption; language properties are not enhanced by population size alone. — [arXiv:2204.12982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12982). Chaabouni et al. 2022 ("Emergent communication at scale") found no consistent advantage of population size for generalisation/ease-of-learning (as summarised by the 2024 review). — [Galke & Raviv 2024 review, arXiv:2403.14427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14427)
- **Michel, Rita, Mathewson, Tieleman & Lazaridou 2023 (ICLR), "Revisiting populations in multi-agent communication".** Partitioning the population into sender–receiver pairs during training limits co-adaptation and yields an objective where agents maximise internal accuracy *and* alignment with other agents; agents then communicate with never-met partners, and "larger populations develop languages that are more compositional". — [ML Anthology / ICLR 2023](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/michel2023iclr-revisiting)
- **Chaabouni, Kharitonov, Bouchacourt, Dupoux & Baroni 2020 (ACL).** Given large enough input spaces emergent languages generalise to novel composite concepts; *no correlation* between degree of compositionality and generalisation; but "the more compositional a language is, the more easily it will be picked up by new learners, even when the latter differ in architecture"; compositionality does not arise from generalisation pressure, yet "if an emergent language does chance upon it, it will be more likely to survive and thrive". — [ACL 2020.acl-main.407](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.407); [arXiv:2004.09124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09124)
- **Dagan, Hupkes & Bruni 2021 (EACL), "Co-evolution of language and agents in referential games".** Builds on Cogswell et al.; adds a transmission bottleneck (new agents learn from experienced ones) *and* genetic evolution of agent architectures in a "Language Transmission Simulator"; architectural co-evolution "achieves across-the-board improvements on all considered metrics and surpasses the gains made with cultural transmission". — [arXiv:2001.03361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03361); [ACL anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.260)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** The two tunable knobs that recur are (a) *how much* the newcomer imitates (I_a/I_b in Ren; reset interval in Li & Bowling) and (b) *how abrupt* turnover is. Too much imitation copies the holistic language faithfully (no filtering); too little loses it. Smooth, staggered, large-population turnover weakens the pressure (Li & Bowling) — directly relevant to an always-on installation with overlapping generations.
- **[inference]** Chaabouni 2020 + Li & Bowling together give the selection story: compositional languages are not *produced* by communication pressure but are *preferentially retained* across learners because they are easier to learn. Generational turnover is therefore the filter, and the inductive bias of the learner architecture is what the filter amplifies (Griffiths & Kalish).
- **[inference]** SIL (Lu et al.) is the closest template for FLOCK's "learn from elder, then live": copy/teacher = elder, student = lamb; imitation first, interaction second.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Cogswell et al. or Guo et al. full texts, so the exact replacement schedules and metric values are not reported.
- Michel et al. 2023: arXiv ID not confirmed; cite via ML Anthology/ICLR.

## Key Question 3: Iconicity across generations (graphical, gestural, acoustic) — what does it predict for 16×16 sheep drawings?

### Takeaway
Across Pictionary-style graphical experiments, silent-gesture experiments and slide-whistle experiments, signs start iconic/pantomimic, then *simplify and conventionalise* into symbols through repeated interactive use and transmission to naive learners; iconicity is a bootstrap that is partly shed as systematic (combinatorial/compositional) structure takes over, and the possibility of iconicity can slightly *delay* structuring.

### Cited Findings
- **Garrod, Fay, Lee, Oberlander & MacLeod 2007, *Cognitive Science* 31(6):961–987.** Tests whether iconic graphical signs become symbolic through repeated usage in a Pictionary task; the first experiment shows simple repetition is *not* enough — the shift requires feedback/interaction between communicators. — [Glasgow repository](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/29854); [bibliography summary](https://langev.com/author/tmacleod)
- **Fay, Garrod, Roberts & Swoboda 2010, *Cognitive Science* 34(3):351–386.** Compares iterated learning vs social collaboration accounts of communication-system evolution and tests the collaboration account experimentally. — [doi:10.1111/j.1551-6709.2009.01090.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2009.01090.x); [Glasgow record](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/45930)
- **Fay, Ellison & Garrod 2014 (*Pragmatics & Cognition*).** "Semantically motivated iconic signs play an important role in creating such communication systems from scratch"; iconic signs "tend to become simplified and symbolic as the communication system matures", driven by "repeated interactive use of the signs". — [Cologne repository](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/79549/)
- **Caldwell & Smith 2012, *PLoS ONE* 7:e43807.** "Replacement microsocieties": the most experienced members are repeatedly replaced by naive participants. Signs became symbolic quickly; became mutually incomprehensible across chains; measured with perimetric complexity, signs "were becoming progressively simplified over successive usage"; first demonstration that graphical signs from such experiments "can be transmitted to, and spontaneously adopted by, naive participants", supporting Tomasello's "drift to the arbitrary". — [PMC3426541](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426541)
- **Motamedi, Schouwstra, Smith, Culbertson & Kirby 2019, *Cognition* 192:103964, "Evolving artificial sign languages in the lab".** Silent gesture; manipulates interaction and transmission separately and together; neither alone gives the full outcome, together they keep communication efficient while steadily increasing regularity and systematic structure; early gestures were unsystematic pantomime, later ones language-like, resembling newly emerging sign languages. — [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2019.05.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2019.05.001); [UCL open access](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10078227/); [videos dataset](https://datashare.ed.ac.uk/handle/10283/3191)
- **Verhoef, Kirby & Padden 2011 (CogSci) / Verhoef, Kirby & de Boer 2012 (*Language and Cognition*), slide-whistle iterated learning.** Each participant learns the previous participant's whistles; "transmission from participant to participant causes the system to change and become cumulatively more learnable and more structured" — combinatorial (duality-of-patterning-like) structure emerges in a continuous acoustic signal space. — [CogSci 2011 PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9rh914kc/qt9rh914kc.pdf); [Language and Cognition 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-and-cognition/article/origins-of-duality-of-patterning-in-artificial-whistled-languages/138E841886C1C841CD2689E33921136A)
- **Verhoef, Kirby & de Boer 2016 (*Cognitive Science*), "Iconicity and the emergence of combinatorial structure in language".** Two iterated-learning conditions: iconic form–meaning mappings possible vs experimentally impossible. "Both conditions became more learnable and structured, but this process was slightly delayed" when iconicity was available. — [summary (cognitionandculture.net)](https://cognitionandculture.net/blogs/the-mint/article-for-january-iconicity-and-the-emergence-of-combinatorial-structure-in-language); DOI 10.1111/cogs.12326 *(DOI from memory; not fetched)*
- **Neural sketching channel.** Mihai & Hare 2021 (NeurIPS), "Learning to draw: emergent communication through sketching": a differentiable stroke-rasteriser lets a sender *draw* a sketch of a photo and a receiver pick the photo; agents learn to communicate by drawing, and "with appropriate inductive biases" produce drawings humans can interpret. — [arXiv:2106.02067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02067); [NeurIPS 2021 PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/39d0a8908fbe6c18039ea8227f827023-Paper.pdf)
- A 2026 preprint, "Drawing with strangers: population scaling drives zero-shot mutual intelligibility in emergent sketching", reports that scaling the population in sketch-based emergent communication yields drawings intelligible to never-met partners. — [arXiv:2606.10582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10582) *(seen only as a search-result title/snippet; not read)*

### Inferences
- **[inference] Prediction for sheep drawings:** if drawings are produced by a network with a perceptual input of the referent and judged by another sheep, early drawings will be iconic-ish (whatever the pixel channel can carry); under repeated use plus lamb turnover they should simplify (fewer lit pixels / lower perimetric complexity), become clan-specific and mutually unintelligible between flocks that do not interact (Caldwell & Smith), and gradually move toward reusable sub-patterns (glyph parts) — i.e. combinatorial structure, in analogy to the whistle result. Verhoef 2016 suggests allowing iconicity does not prevent this, only slows it.
- **[inference]** Garrod 2007 implies that *feedback* (the receiver signalling success/failure) is needed for iconic → symbolic simplification; a pure imitation-only drawing tradition would not simplify by itself.
- **[speculation]** 16×16 binary pixels are a far cruder channel than stroke sketches; perimetric complexity and a pixel-count "ink cost" are the natural analogues of the simplification measures and could be made explicit costs.

### Gaps
- No work found that runs *neural* iterated learning over a sketch/pixel channel across generations; Mihai & Hare and the 2026 sketching preprint are single-generation or population-only. This is open ground.
- The Verhoef 2016 full text was not available; the magnitude of the "slight delay" is not quantified here.

## Key Question 4: Cultural transmission of behaviour (not just language) in RL — what makes agents copy elders?

### Takeaway
Model-free RL agents do *not* spontaneously imitate others; social learning emerges only when the training environment makes the expert informative and intermittently absent, the agent has memory, and an auxiliary loss forces attention to the other agent. DeepMind's MEDAL-ADR recipe (Memory, Expert co-player, expert Dropout, Attention Loss, Automatic Domain Randomisation) produces real-time few-shot imitation that persists after the demonstrator leaves.

### Cited Findings
- **Bhoopchand et al. 2023 (Nature Communications 14, "Learning few-shot imitation as cultural transmission").** Agent (MPO, 512-unit LSTM) in GoalCycle3D learns to imitate in real time without any pre-collected human data. Ingredients: (M) memory — replacing the LSTM with an MLP reduced recall to zero; (E/D) expert co-player that appears/disappears probabilistically within episodes — dropout trains the agent to remember and reproduce after the expert is gone; (AL) auxiliary loss predicting the co-player's relative position, used only in training — without it the agent treats the expert as background noise; (ADR) task-parameter ranges widen when training CT > 0.75 and narrow when it drops, keeping a "Goldilocks zone" — without ADR the agent never reaches high CT in complex worlds. Cultural-transmission metric CT ≈ 0 for an independent agent, ≈ 0.75 for pure following, ≈ 1 for follow-then-continue-alone. Ablations: removing memory, attention loss or the expert gave CT = 0; removing the expert produced a risk-averse agent. Recall: with the expert removed after the first of ten 900-step trials, MEDAL-ADR kept performing for several trials. Causal test: over 1,000 two-option trials the agent's chosen cycle matched the demonstrated one every time. The agent also reproduces *incorrect* demonstrations (a form of objective misgeneralisation). **The paper runs no multi-generation experiments**; generational training so agents bootstrap from other agents rather than hand-coded bots is listed as future work. — [doi:10.1038/s41467-023-42875-2 / PMC10684502](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684502/); preprint [arXiv:2203.00715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00715)
- **Ndousse, Eck, Levine & Jaques 2021 (ICML), "Emergent social learning via multi-agent reinforcement learning".** Independent model-free RL agents in a multi-agent environment "usually do not" learn to use social learning; imposing constraints on the training environment plus a model-based auxiliary loss yields generalised social-learning policies that acquire skills they could not learn alone and adapt to new environments by following experts; mixing multi-agent and solo training gives agents that use socially acquired skills when alone. — [arXiv:2010.00581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00581); [PMLR v139](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/ndousse21a.html)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** The common denominators are: (1) an informative elder present *some* of the time, (2) recurrent memory in the lamb, (3) a loss that makes elder-state prediction cheap, (4) a curriculum that keeps tasks in the zone where copying pays. For FLOCK, "expert dropout" has a natural narrative analogue: elders die/wander off, lambs must carry on.
- **[inference]** Both papers show copying is *instrumental* — agents imitate because it raises reward in-episode. If FLOCK's lambs have no task where imitating pays, copying must instead be imposed as a supervised imitation phase (the SIL/NIL route), which is simpler and more controllable for an installation.

### Gaps
- Neither paper studies chains of agent→agent transmission over generations; the fidelity loss per hop (the critical quantity for a months-long sim) is unmeasured in the behavioural-RL literature I found.

## Key Question 5: Inheritance mechanisms — Lamarckian vs Darwinian vs Baldwinian; weights vs priors/hyperparameters

### Takeaway
Evolving *initial weights/hyperparameters/learning rules* with lifetime learning on top (Baldwinian) is the most general and best-supported pattern; Lamarckian write-back of learned weights helps in predictable environments and gives better-fitted newborns but risks locking in and its advantage shrinks when learning/replication costs rise; results across studies are mixed and the terms are used inconsistently.

### Cited Findings
- **Fernando et al. 2018 (DeepMind), "Meta-learning by the Baldwin effect".** The Baldwin effect (learning shapes what gets genetically encoded) works as a meta-learning algorithm: evolution tunes hyperparameters and *initial parameters* of deep networks, evolving few-shot supervised and RL learners; encodes learning biases comparable to MAML on the same problems; MAML is more data-efficient in simple cases but the Baldwinian approach needs no gradients back to the reference parameters and works with any number of inner-loop updates. — [arXiv:1806.07917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07917)
- **Hinton & Nowlan 1987, "How learning can guide evolution"** — the classic Baldwin-effect simulation (needle-in-haystack smoothed by lifetime learning). — [Complex Systems 1(3)](https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v01_i03_a06/) *(URL from memory; not fetched)*
- **Gupta, Savarese, Ganguli & Fei-Fei 2021 (Nature Communications 12), DERL.** Evolves morphologies ("unimals") with a tournament-style Darwinian scheme while each individual learns via RL from scratch; morphologies evolved in harder environments/tasks "learn faster and better"; evolution selects bodies that make lifetime learning faster — an explicit evolution-facilitates-learning (Baldwin-style) result at the morphology level. The retrieved sources do not use the term "Baldwin". — [doi:10.1038/s41467-021-25874-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25874-z); [arXiv:2102.02202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02202)
- **Najarro & Risi 2020 (NeurIPS), "Meta-learning through Hebbian plasticity in random networks".** Evolution searches *synapse-specific Hebbian learning rules* rather than weights; networks start from random weights and self-organise during life; a quadruped learns to walk and adapts to unseen damage in <100 timesteps with no reward signal at test time. — [arXiv:2007.02686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02686)
- **Lamarckian vs Darwinian comparisons:**
  - "Lamarck's Revenge" (evolutionary robotics, morphology+controller co-evolution): inheriting learned brain traits helps; Lamarckism "amplifies the emergence of 'morphological intelligence'"; advantage traced to newborns whose inherited brains fit their bodies better. — [arXiv:2309.13099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13099)
  - NEAT with online plasticity (2026 preprint): Lamarckian write-back vs Darwinian/Baldwinian discard "yield highly similar learning curves and final performance"; benefit comes mostly from within-episode adaptation, not inheritance; kept Lamarckian "for simplicity". — [arXiv:2606.20817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20817)
  - Agent-based comparison: Lamarckian advantage shrinks as learning costs or replication costs rise; Baldwinian usually sits between Lamarckian and Darwinian. — [arXiv:2305.00491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00491)
  - Note: "Darwinian" means "plasticity not inherited" in one paper and "learned traits not inherited" in others — compare setups carefully (observation from the search summary of these papers).
- **Co-evolving architecture with culture:** Dagan et al. 2021 found genetic evolution of agent architectures beat cultural transmission alone on all metrics in referential games. — [arXiv:2001.03361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03361)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** For a *language* that must be culturally transmitted, Lamarckian copying of the parent's full language weights would short-circuit the bottleneck (the lamb would inherit the holistic code perfectly and no compressibility pressure would act). The literature's own split — genes carry *priors/learning rules/initial weights*, culture carries the *language* — is the one to keep.
- **[inference]** Hebbian/plastic-rule inheritance (Najarro & Risi) is attractive for an installation because lambs visibly "grow into" behaviour at runtime without backprop, but it has not been combined with iterated-learning language emergence in any paper I found.

### Gaps
- No paper directly compares Lamarckian vs Baldwinian inheritance *inside* a neural iterated-learning language experiment.
- Hinton & Nowlan link unverified this session.

## Key Question 6: Practical generational turnover in long-running sims — overlapping vs discrete generations, stable population, avoiding collapse or freezing

### Takeaway
Discrete, synchronous replacement gives the strongest structuring pressure but visible performance crashes at each reset; staggered/overlapping replacement is smoother but, if too gradual or the population too large, the pressure vanishes (Li & Bowling). Drift-to-nothing is countered by expressivity pressure (Kirby 2015) and by periodic imitation of a frozen teacher (SIL); freezing is countered by continued newcomer influx (the "knowledge gap", Cogswell) and by not letting experienced pairs over-co-adapt (Rita 2022; Michel 2023).

### Cited Findings
- Staggered vs simultaneous reset: with staggered N=10 (one listener every 600 iterations) ease-of-teaching and topographic similarity fell toward the no-reset baseline, whereas simultaneous reset of all 10 restored them (ρ≈0.59); sequential single-listener reset was strongest; every full reset dropped success to ~20% (55% with N=2). — [Li & Bowling 2019, arXiv:1906.02403](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02403)
- Degenerate (underspecified) languages arise in learning-only chains; expressivity pressure from communication is what prevents collapse. — [Kirby et al. 2015, doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2015.03.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.03.016)
- Language drift during task fine-tuning is countered by periodically regenerating the student by imitating a teacher copied from the student before fine-tuning (SIL). — [Lu et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.12694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12694)
- Periodic replacement of agents in a population creates a knowledge gap that implicitly induces transmission and better compositional generalisation. — [Cogswell et al. 2019, arXiv:1904.09067](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09067)
- Co-adaptation overfitting between a fixed speaker–listener pair undermines structure; controlling it yields more compositional, general languages. — [Rita et al. 2022, arXiv:2209.15342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15342). Partitioned populations (limit co-adaptation across pairs, reward alignment with others) communicate with never-met agents and get more compositional with size. — [Michel et al. 2023](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/michel2023iclr-revisiting)
- Small human communities vary more and are "more vulnerable to drift"; larger ones systematise faster. — [Raviv et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6661353)
- Population size alone does not reliably help in neural simulations; heterogeneity of learners matters. — [Rita et al. 2022, arXiv:2204.12982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12982); [Galke & Raviv 2024 review, arXiv:2403.14427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14427)
- Compositionality under human iterated learning levels off before generation 10. — [Beckner et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/jole/lzx001)
- Bayesian iterated learning converges to the prior — i.e. once at the prior, a chain stops changing unless the prior/data regime changes. — [Griffiths & Kalish 2007](https://cogsci.syr.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/GriffithsK2007.pdf)
- Griffiths/Kalish line also includes work adding population structure to ILM models. — [Princeton record: "Adding population structure to models of language evolution by iterated learning"](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/adding-population-structure-to-models-of-language-evolution-by-it/) *(record only; not read)*

### Inferences
- **[inference]** For a watchable, always-on flock, the Li & Bowling result argues for *cohort* births (several lambs born together every N minutes/hours, several elders dying together) rather than one-at-a-time trickle — cohorts keep the knowledge gap abrupt enough to exert pressure while never emptying the population.
- **[inference]** A fixed population size is easiest: each death triggers a birth; the parent pair is chosen by a fitness/age rule; the lamb is a new network with inherited priors and a fixed imitation budget drawn from a *sample* of elder utterances (the bottleneck).
- **[inference]** To prevent freezing at the prior (Griffiths & Kalish), inject slow non-stationarity: new referents/objects, small mutations in the lamb's prior, or occasional migrants from another flock (Caldwell & Smith's mutually unintelligible chains imply this creates visible dialect contact).

### Gaps
- I found no study of iterated learning with *continuous* overlapping generations over hundreds of generations with explicit "language loss" statistics; the closest are Li & Bowling's 50 resets and Ren's 80–100 generations.
- No source quantifies how much a neural language changes per generation at steady state (needed to tune "months of runtime").

## Key Question 7: Watchability — tracking/visualising the evolution of an emergent language

### Takeaway
The field already uses a small set of per-generation scalars (topographic similarity, positional/bag-of-symbols disentanglement, ease-of-teaching, compressibility/MDL, entropy, communication success, perimetric complexity for graphics) that can be logged per flock and per generation; the original topographic-similarity paper was explicitly framed as a *visualisation* of emergence, and the human sign/graphics literature shows sign-complexity decline is visible to the eye.

### Cited Findings
- Topographic similarity: negative Spearman correlation between pairwise meaning distances and pairwise message (edit) distances; used as the compositionality proxy by Li & Bowling (peaked ~0.6) and Ren (ρ up to 0.93), and attributed to Brighton & Kirby 2006 ("Understanding linguistic evolution by visualizing the emergence of topographic mappings", Artificial Life 12). — [Li & Bowling, arXiv:1906.02403](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02403); [Ren et al., arXiv:2002.01365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01365); attribution via [Chaabouni et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.407) and [CLIN 34 abstract](https://clin34.leidenuniv.nl/abstracts/compositionality-in-emergent-languages-evaluating-the-metrics/)
- Positional disentanglement (posdis) and bag-of-symbols disentanglement (bosdis), with code in facebookresearch/EGG. — [Chaabouni et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.407). A later study notes these metrics "had not been empirically validated" on their own. — [CLIN 34 abstract](https://clin34.leidenuniv.nl/abstracts/compositionality-in-emergent-languages-evaluating-the-metrics/)
- Ease-of-teaching: train k fresh listeners on a frozen speaker and measure learning speed. — [Li & Bowling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02403)
- Speaker policy entropy spikes at each reset (a visible "turmoil" signal). — [Li & Bowling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02403)
- Cultural-transmission score CT (follow vs follow-and-recall) and recall-over-trials curves for behaviour. — [Bhoopchand et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684502/)
- Compressibility (learning pressure) vs expressivity (number of distinct signals) as the two axes of the 2015 analysis. — [Kirby et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2015.03.016)
- Perimetric complexity of graphical signs declines over usage. — [Caldwell & Smith 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426541)
- Mutual intelligibility with never-met partners as a population-level metric. — [Michel et al. 2023](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/michel2023iclr-revisiting)
- Human iterated-learning videos of gesture systems are themselves watchable artefacts (Motamedi et al. dataset). — [Edinburgh DataShare](https://datashare.ed.ac.uk/handle/10283/3191)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** A documentary camera could be driven by events on these scalars: entropy spike (a cohort just born), topographic-similarity jump (a flock "found grammar"), drop in inter-flock intelligibility (a dialect split), perimetric-complexity decline (a glyph is being conventionalised), recall-curve of a lamb after its teacher died.
- **[speculation]** Brighton & Kirby's 2006 "topographic mapping" plots (meaning space vs signal space) are a ready-made visual: an MDS of bleat-parameter space coloured by referent would show a flock's language crystallising.

### Gaps
- No paper found on *real-time visualisation* of emergent-language evolution for an audience; all metrics are offline research plots. Brighton & Kirby 2006 DOI not verified this session.
- EGG toolkit citation (Kharitonov et al. 2019) and Lowe et al. 2019 "pitfalls of measuring emergent communication" were not verified via search; omitted from findings.

## Key Question 8 (separate section): FLOCK 2.0 fit assessment and recommended mechanism

**Everything in this section is my own assessment [inference/speculation] built on the cited findings above; it is not a literature claim.**

### Fit verdict
Iterated learning is an unusually good match for FLOCK 2.0: its two defining ingredients (lambs learning from a limited sample of elders; sheep needing signals to work in interaction) are exactly what the designer already has in mind, and the literature predicts the outcomes the designer wants — structure that emerges in a handful of generations (Kirby 2008/2015; Ren 2020; Beckner 2017 plateau), persists as long as turnover continues (Li & Bowling; Cogswell), and drifts into clan-specific dialects when flocks are isolated (Caldwell & Smith; Raviv). The main risks are also well characterised: (a) collapse to a few all-purpose bleats if there is no expressivity pressure; (b) freezing at the architecture's prior if nothing perturbs the system; (c) loss of pressure if turnover is too gradual (Li & Bowling's staggered-N=10 result); (d) ugly performance crashes at each reset if replacement is total.

### Recommended mechanism (concrete)

**A. Two inheritance channels, kept separate (Baldwinian genes + cultural language).**
1. *Genome (vertical, Darwinian/Baldwinian):* each sheep carries a small evolved vector — initial weights (or a low-rank seed) of its bleat encoder/decoder and drawing network, plus hyperparameters such as imitation learning rate, imitation budget, entropy bonus, acoustic-parameter prior ranges, ink-cost weight. Lambs get a parent-average plus Gaussian mutation. Do **not** copy a parent's *trained* language weights (Lamarckian) — that bypasses the bottleneck that produces structure (Kirby 2015; Ren 2020). If you want visible "family resemblance", let the genome bias acoustic timbre/pitch range instead; that is heritable and audible without transmitting the lexicon.
2. *Culture (horizontal/oblique, iterated learning):* the lamb's language is learned by observation only, following the NIL/SIL template.

**B. Lamb life-cycle (SIL/NIL hybrid, per lamb).**
1. *Imitation phase (bottleneck):* for a fixed, short budget (Ren's "interval of advantage": tune so that a perfectly compositional language is learnable but a holistic one is not), the lamb trains by supervised imitation on a *sample* of (context/referent → bleat parameters, referent → 16×16 drawing) pairs observed from nearby elders. Sampling should cover only part of the referent space (poverty of the stimulus). Use a recurrent memory and an auxiliary "where is my elder / what will it do next" prediction loss (Bhoopchand's M + AL) so attention to elders is learned rather than hard-wired.
2. *Interaction phase (expressivity):* the lamb then plays referential/coordination games with flockmates (e.g. bleat to recruit to a food patch; draw to indicate which patch; listener acts). Reward both parties for success. This is the Kirby-2015 communication pressure and Ren's "filter out ambiguous languages" step.
3. *Elder phase (transmission):* once adult, the sheep's outputs are what new lambs sample from. Elders should be frozen or very-low-LR during the lamb's imitation window so the teacher is stable (SIL's frozen teacher copy).

**C. Turnover schedule.** Fixed population size; births in small cohorts (e.g. 3–5 lambs at once every T) coincident with deaths of the oldest/least successful; avoid one-at-a-time trickle (Li & Bowling). Expert dropout analogue: elders occasionally leave the lamb's field of view so recall, not just following, is trained.

**D. Anti-collapse / anti-freeze safeguards.**
- Expressivity: keep a growing or rotating referent set so a degenerate language costs reward.
- Anti-freeze: slow mutation of genomic priors; occasional migrant sheep between flocks (predicts watchable dialect contact, per Caldwell & Smith's mutually unintelligible chains); partition interaction partners so pairs do not over-co-adapt (Rita 2022; Michel 2023).
- Drawing channel: add a small ink/complexity cost and receiver feedback so iconic drawings simplify into glyphs (Garrod 2007; Caldwell & Smith 2012); expect iconicity to slow but not block structure (Verhoef 2016).

**E. Watchability hooks.** Log per flock and per cohort: topographic similarity (bleat-parameter space vs referent space), posdis/bosdis if the bleat is tokenised, speaker entropy, ease-of-teaching (periodically spawn a probe lamb), communication success, inter-flock intelligibility, drawing perimetric complexity, and per-lamb recall curves after its teacher dies. Trigger camera cuts on spikes/jumps in these.

### Open risks to flag to the designer
- No published work combines Hebbian/plastic inheritance *and* iterated-learning language emergence, nor neural iterated learning over a pixel/sketch channel; FLOCK would be doing something new on both counts, so expect tuning (especially the imitation budget, which is the single most sensitive parameter in Ren et al.).
- Steady-state drift rate over hundreds of generations is unmeasured in the literature; the months-long runtime regime is uncharted.
- Beckner et al.'s plateau means "structure" may saturate quickly; the long tail of the show will be about dialect divergence, glyph conventionalisation and lineage, not continued grammaticalisation, unless the referent world keeps changing.
