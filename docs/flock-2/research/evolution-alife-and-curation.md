## KQ1. Neuroevolution: where it beats or complements gradient RL for small agents (NEAT/HyperNEAT, ES, Deep GA, indirect encodings, evolved plasticity, tiny pixel policies, EvoJAX/evosax, CPU practicality)

### Takeaway
For agents with ~10^3–10^5 parameters, evolution strategies and simple GAs match policy-gradient RL in final performance on standard benchmarks while being invariant to action frequency, delayed/sparse reward and non-differentiable components, at a data-efficiency cost of roughly 3–10× on hard continuous-control tasks; the practical CPU crossover is "a few hundred to a thousand evaluations per generation on tens of cores", with one 18-core box solving 3D Humanoid in ~11 h and 1,440 cores in 10 min, and JAX libraries (evosax/EvoJAX) collapsing this to minutes-to-hours on one GPU for networks below ~100k parameters.

### Cited Findings

**OpenAI ES (Salimans et al. 2017)**
- ES is "invariant to action frequency and delayed rewards, tolerant of extremely long horizons, and does not need temporal discounting or value function approximation"; it can "incorporate non-differentiable elements" and avoids exploding gradients with RNNs by smoothing in parameter space — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864) (full text extracted via PDF).
- Data efficiency: ES matched TRPO on MuJoCo tasks using "between 3x and 10x as much data" on hard environments (Hopper, Walker2d) but "up to 3x better sample complexity than TRPO" on simple ones; Table 1 ratios of ES-to-TRPO timesteps to reach 100% of TRPO's 5M-step progress: HalfCheetah 0.58, Hopper 6.94, InvertedDoublePendulum 1.23, InvertedPendulum 0.88, Swimmer 0.30, Walker2d 7.88 — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).
- Policy architecture in MuJoCo: MLP with two 64-unit tanh hidden layers (identical for ES and TRPO) — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).
- CPU scaling: "Solving 3D Humanoid with ES on one 18-core machine takes about 11 hours" (median 657 min); with 1,440 cores, 10 minutes, with near-linear speedup in cores; Atari on 720 EC2 CPUs took "about one hour per game" for 1B frames, and ES beat published A3C on 23 of 51 games and lost on 28 — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).
- Communication: workers exchange only scalar returns using shared random seeds (common random numbers), plus antithetic sampling and fitness shaping — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).
- ES "has been able to learn a very wide variety of gaits" on Humanoid versus TRPO, "which suggests a qualitatively different exploration behavior" — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).

**Deep GA (Such et al. 2017)**
- A simple truncation-selection GA evolves Atari policies with "over four million free parameters", trains Atari "in ∼4 hours on one desktop [4 GPUs, 48 CPU cores] or ∼1 hour distributed on 720 cores" — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567) (PDF text extracted).
- Hyperparameters (Table 2): Atari population 1,000+1, mutation power σ = 0.002, truncation T = 20; Humanoid population 12,500+1, σ = 0.00224, T = 625; Image Hard Maze population 20,000+1, σ = 0.005, T = 61 — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567).
- On 13 games with 1B frames, "DQN, ES and the GA produced the best score on 3 games each, while A3C produced the best score on 4"; GA beat ES on several games within 1–29 generations; random search with the same budget outperformed DQN on 3 games, ES on 3, and A3C on 6, which the authors read as evidence that the local structure near random init is highly exploitable — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567).
- Compact encoding stores a genome as a list of mutation seeds ("up to 10,000-fold compression"); GA + novelty search (archive probability p = 0.01, k = 25 nearest neighbours) solved the deceptive Image Hard Maze where DQN, A3C, ES and reward-GA failed — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567).

**Tiny pixel policies via neuroevolution (Tang, Nguyen & Ha 2020)**
- A self-attention patch-selection agent with 3,667 learnable parameters (vs 4,733,485 for World Models and 445,955 for PPO, Table 2) reaches 914 ± 15 on CarRacing and 1125 ± 589 on DoomTakeCover ("at least 1000x fewer parameters"); trained with pycma CMA-ES, population 256, initial σ 0.1, fitness = mean of 16 rollouts (CarRacing) / 5 (Doom); 7×7 patches, stride 4, N = 529 patches, K = 10 attended patches, 16-neuron LSTM controller — [arXiv:2003.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08165) (PDF text extracted).
- Generalisation under task-irrelevant perturbations (Table 4): Ours 866 ± 112 / 900 ± 35 / 898 ± 53 versus World Models 655 / 166 / 446, GA 442 / 675 / 833, PPO 505 / 615 / 855 — [arXiv:2003.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08165). Hardware/time not stated in the paper text extracted (gap).

**Evolved plasticity (Hebbian meta-learning)**
- Najarro & Risi 2020: evolve only per-synapse ABCD Hebbian coefficients (5 per synapse) with an ES adapted from Salimans et al.; Car Racing net 92,690 weights / 456,640 Hebbian coefficients, population 200; quadruped net [128, 64, 8] with 12,288 synapses / 61,440 coefficients, population 500; α = 0.2 (decay 0.995), σ = 0.1 (decay 0.999). Results: Car Racing Hebbian 872 ± 11 vs static 711 ± 16; quadruped unseen left-front-leg damage Hebbian 452 ± 95 (solved) vs static 68 ± 56 (not solved), while static was better on seen morphologies (1604 vs 1051 undamaged) — [arXiv:2007.02686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02686).
- Pedersen & Risi 2021 "Evolving and Merging Hebbian Learning Rules": K-means merging of rules cut trainable parameters from 61,440 to 1,920 while agents optimised on one quadruped generalised to 30 unseen morphologies; framed as a "genomic bottleneck" regulariser — [arXiv:2104.07959](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2104.07959).

**NEAT / HyperNEAT / indirect encodings**
- NEAT (Stanley & Miikkulainen 2002) is a direct encoding that evolves arbitrary topologies by complexification; HyperNEAT (Stanley et al. 2009) uses NEAT to evolve CPPNs that describe "network connectivity as a function of neural geometry", searching a compressed space so difficulty shifts "away from dimensionality to the underlying problem structure" — [arXiv:1312.5355 (Generative NeuroEvolution for Deep Learning)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5355); [arXiv:1604.07806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.07806).
- A 2014 study found "HyperNEAT alone has difficulty in classifying" MNIST but "is effective at training a feature extractor for backward propagation learning" — [ar5iv 1312.5355](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1312.5355).
- HyperNEAT appears as a baseline in the ES paper's Atari table (e.g. Berzerk 1394.0, Star Gunner 2720.0 vs ES 1470.0) — [arXiv:1703.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864).

**JAX ES libraries and throughput**
- evosax (Lange 2022): 30+ strategies (OpenAI-ES, CMA-ES, Sep-CMA-ES, xNES, SNES, PGPE, ARS, LES, DiscoveredES, Simple GA, DE, PSO…), ask-eval-tell API, works with jit/vmap/lax.scan, v0.3.2 requires Python ≥ 3.10 — [GitHub RobertTLange/evosax](https://github.com/RobertTLange/evosax). The paper's Brax experiments used population 256, 2,000 generations, and "a single run takes between 30 minutes and 4 hours of training time on a single V100S"; the limitations section notes "current ES struggle beyond roughly 100k parameters" — [ar5iv 2212.04180](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.04180). No CPU-vs-GPU throughput table is reported (gap).
- EvoJAX (Google, archived read-only since Aug 2025): claims experiments "within minutes on a single accelerator, compared to hours or days when using CPUs"; MNIST ConvNet >98% in 5 min on one GPU; Slime Volleyball under 5 min on one GPU vs hours on multiple CPUs; WaterWorld and Brax locomotion "tens of minutes"; first release focused on PGPE; examples run on TPUs / V100 — [GitHub google/evojax](https://github.com/google/evojax).
- NeuroEvoBench provides benchmark logs for evosax/EvoJAX strategies with all tasks in JAX "so that population evaluations can be parallelized on accelerators" but no throughput numbers surfaced — [PyPI neuroevobench](https://pypi.org/project/neuroevobench).
- Flow Lenia (a continuous CA, not a policy, but a comparable JAX workload): "about 255 μs per step on a Tesla T4 GPU (1 channel, 10 kernels, 128×128 world)" — [ar5iv 2212.07906](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.07906).
- Hamon et al. 2023: a 400×200 grid with up to 1,000 agents each running a 2,445-parameter conv+LSTM network for 1,000,000 timesteps completes "in about 20 minutes" on a single GPU in JAX — [ar5iv 2302.09334](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.09334).

### Inferences
- "When does ES on a CPU become practical?" The literature gives two anchor points: (a) Salimans: an 18-core machine solves 3D Humanoid (two 64-unit layers, ~10k params) in ~11 h with population-scale evaluations each requiring a full MuJoCo episode; (b) Tang: population 256 × 16 rollouts of CarRacing per generation with 3.7k params. The limiting factor is almost always environment rollout cost, not the ES update. For FLOCK-scale agents (likely ≤10k parameters) and a cheap 128×128 tile simulation, a population of 256–1,000 with episode lengths of a few thousand ticks is plausibly within tens of core-hours on a desktop; beyond ~100k parameters, evosax's own limitation note suggests ES becomes unreliable regardless of hardware. (Speculation anchored on the cited numbers.)
- ES/GA win over gradient RL specifically when: rewards are sparse/delayed (fox survival, lamb survival), policies include discrete non-differentiable components (communication tokens, patch selection), long horizons with recurrent nets, and when behavioural diversity across the population is itself valued (ES "wide variety of gaits"; GA-NS solves deceptive tasks).
- Gradient RL wins on sample efficiency for hard continuous control (Hopper/Walker 7–8× worse for ES), so for lifetime learning within an episode a gradient or Hebbian inner loop is cheaper than per-individual ES.

### Gaps
- No published CPU-vs-GPU evaluations-per-second table for evosax/EvoJAX was found; the only concrete numbers are the qualitative EvoJAX claims and the evosax V100S run times.
- Tang et al. hardware and wall-clock not in the extracted text.
- Original NEAT 2002 quantitative results (double pole balancing evaluations) were not retrieved; HyperNEAT author list (D'Ambrosio) not confirmed from retrieved sources.

## KQ2. Evolution + lifetime learning: Baldwin effect, evolved initial weights / meta-learned priors, DERL, Evolved Policy Gradients, evolved rewards / drives

### Takeaway
There is a consistent empirical line — Fernando et al. 2018, DERL 2021, Najarro & Risi 2020, EPG 2018, Singh/Lewis/Barto 2010, Niekum 2010 — showing that evolving the *starting point* (initial weights, hyperparameters, plasticity rules, reward/loss functions, or bodies) while leaving behaviour to lifetime learning produces faster learners and better transfer than evolving behaviour directly, with the Baldwinian (non-Lamarckian) variant winning when the task distribution is broad or changing.

### Cited Findings
- **Fernando et al. 2018, "Meta-Learning by the Baldwin Effect"**: evolution "shaping the hyperparameters and the initial parameters of deep learning algorithms" yields few-shot supervised and RL learners "on par to MAML" on sinusoid regression using only final fitness as signal; generational GA population 100 (sinusoid) and asynchronous steady-state GA population 500, tournament 10 (MuJoCo Cheetah). Lamarckian won on Goal-Velocity but on Goal-Direction "Baldwinian evolution performed best, and Lamarckian was clearly the weakest" (stuck in a backward-only optimum). Conclusion: Baldwin "is better when the task distribution is broad or changing quickly, while Lamarckian learning is better when the distribution is narrow"; evolved learning rates rose and discount factors fell, i.e. evolution strengthened learning rather than removing it — [arXiv:1806.07917](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07917); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.07917).
- An eLife reviewed preprint (2025) on "Evolution imposes an inductive bias that alters and accelerates learning dynamics" cites Fernando et al. and notes similar ground was covered by Keesing & Stork (1990) — [eLife reviewed preprint 111582](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/111582/reviews).
- **DERL (Gupta, Savarese, Ganguli, Fei-Fei 2021, Nature Comms)**: asynchronous tournament evolution (P = 576, T = 4) of UNIMAL morphologies, ~4,000 morphologies over ~10 generations, each learning a controller from scratch with PPO for 5M interactions; 1,152 CPUs (288 workers × 4 CPUs), "finishes in under 16 hours". Morphological Baldwin effect: learning time to reach the 75th-percentile fitness of the initial population "roughly halved within 10 generations"; an 8th-generation agent beat a 1st-generation agent ~2× at end of learning and reached the 1st-generation's final fitness in about 1/5 of the time; evolved morphologies "more physically stable and energy efficient"; 8 test tasks in 3 domains — [arXiv:2102.02202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02202); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.02202).
- **Evolved Policy Gradients (Houthooft et al. 2018)**: ES evolves a differentiable loss (temporal convolutions over experience) whose gradient trains the policy; learned faster than an off-the-shelf PG on randomized environments; "at test time, our learner optimizes only its learned loss function, and requires no explicit reward signal" — [arXiv:1802.04821](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04821v1); [NeurIPS 2018](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/7876acb66640bad41f1e1371ef30c180-Abstract.html).
- **Singh, Lewis, Barto, Sorg 2010, "Intrinsically Motivated RL: An Evolutionary Perspective"** (IEEE TAMD 2(2):70–82): defines an "optimal reward framework" in which a primary reward is judged by evolutionary fitness across environments; two experiments show "optimal primary reward signals may yield both emergent intrinsic and extrinsic motivation"; intrinsic vs extrinsic reward lies on a continuum of directness to fitness — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5471106); [PDF](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~baveja/Papers/IMRLIEEETAMDFinal.pdf).
- **Niekum, Barto, Spector 2010, "Genetic Programming for Reward Function Search"** (IEEE TAMD 2(2):83–90): GP over reward-generating programs replaces the brute-force discretized search of Singh et al. 2009; in a Hungry-Thirsty grid world with Q-learning the evolved shaping reward "outperform[s] the task reward"; identifies three problem classes where reward search helps: distributions of environments, nonstationary environments, and short agent lifetimes — [GP bibliography entry](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Niekum_2010_ieeeTAMD.html); [AAAI 2010 short version](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/7772).
- A 2021 follow-on, "Learning to Win, Lose and Cooperate through Reward Signal Evolution", continues the evolved-reward line in multi-agent settings — [arXiv:2105.08187](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08187) (abstract only retrieved).
- Hebbian evolved plasticity (see KQ1): plastic networks starting from random weights adapted to unseen damage "in under 100 timesteps" without a reward signal — [arXiv:2007.02686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02686).

### Inferences
- The strongest design pattern across these works is: outer loop = evolution over (initial weights, plasticity rules, reward/drive coefficients, morphology); inner loop = cheap lifetime learning (PPO, Hebbian, or a learned loss). DERL and Fernando both show the Baldwin effect appearing within ~10 generations, which is cheap enough to run at FLOCK scale.
- Evolved intrinsic drives (Singh/Barto; Niekum) are the most direct match to "evolved priors + learned lifetime behaviour": evolve a small vector of drive weights (hunger, fear-of-fox, affiliation, curiosity) per lineage, let lifetime learning optimise against those drives. The explicit result that reward search helps most under "nonstationary environments" and "short agent lifetimes" maps directly onto a months-long world with nightly predation and mortal lambs.
- Lamarckian inheritance (copying learned weights to lambs) is tempting for an installation but Fernando's Goal-Direction result warns it can lock lineages into local optima; Baldwinian inheritance of priors plus re-learning is the safer default, with Lamarckian used sparingly (e.g. only for communication conventions). (Speculation.)

### Gaps
- No work was found that combines evolved intrinsic drives with Hebbian lifetime plasticity in a non-episodic multi-agent world; the pieces exist separately.
- Quantitative Omniglot / few-shot classification numbers from Fernando et al. were not in the extracted text.

## KQ3. Open-ended ALife: what keeps long-running populations interesting and non-collapsing (Tierra, Avida, Polyworld, Sims, Lenia/Flow Lenia, open-endedness theory, POET, Neural MMO, Hamon 2023, minimal criterion, novelty search, QD)

### Takeaway
Classic ALife worlds reliably "stop producing anything interesting within days of launching" (Stanley/Lehman/Soros) because of narrow domains, bounded phenotype complexity, and fitness-driven convergence; the documented remedies are a non-trivial minimal criterion for reproduction (strictness tuned between stagnation and divergence), coevolution of problems with solutions (POET, MCC), explicit novelty/quality-diversity pressure (novelty search, MAP-Elites), and environmental dynamics (resource regrowth/climate gradients), while non-episodic neuroevolution runs show real risk of ecological collapse (3 of 5 seeds in Hamon et al.).

### Cited Findings

**Why systems stall**
- Stanley, Lehman & Soros 2017: Tierra, Avida, Polyworld, Geb and Evosphere are "a far cry from nature"; early methods "are destined to stop producing anything interesting within days of launching, because their problem domains are narrow"; conventional EAs discard "stepping stones" (precursors to wings) that do not improve fitness; "you can't get giraffes if you don't have trees" (solutions create new problems) — [O'Reilly Radar, Dec 2017](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/open-endedness-the-last-grand-challenge-youve-never-heard-of/).
- Soros & Stanley 2014 (Chromaria, ALIFE 2014): four necessary conditions — (1) individuals must meet a nontrivial minimal criterion (MC) to reproduce, (2) evolution should create novel opportunities to meet the MC, (3) individuals decide their own interactions, (4) phenotype complexity not bounded by representation; the world "stagnates when one of the four conditions is not met" — [UCF STARS](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2010/9117); conditions summarised in [arXiv:2406.04268](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04268) and [arXiv:2110.10320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.10320).
- Soros, Cheney & Stanley 2016: "the strictness of the MC can profoundly affect open-ended dynamics, ultimately deciding between complete stagnation (both with extreme strictness or complete relaxation) and orderly divergence" — [ALIFE 2016 PDF](https://www.uvm.edu/neurobotics/pubs/pdf/2016_SorosCheneyStanley_HowTheStrictnessOfTheMinimalCriterionImpactsOpenEndedEvolution_ALIFE.pdf).
- Hughes et al. 2024 formal definition: a system is open-ended to observer O iff artifacts are both *novel* (∀t, ∀T>t, ∃T′>T: E[ℓ(t,T′)] > E[ℓ(t,T)]) and *learnable* (for t′>t, E[ℓ(t′,T)] < E[ℓ(t,T)]); "interestingness" = the observer's choice of loss; AdA's novelty "plateaus after about one month of training, so it is finitely open-ended"; POET is "expect[ed] to plateau once agents solve all possible terrains"; the observer's loss must be fixed in advance or "the observer could declare any system open-ended" — [ar5iv 2406.04268](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.04268).

**Classic systems**
- Tierra (Ray, early 1990s): programs compete for CPU time and memory, "mutate, self-replicate and recombine"; "thousands of generations were compressed into hours" and ancestors diversified — [Cal Academy 1994](https://research.calacademy.org/calwild/1994summer/stories/horizons.htm); [Ray, Tierra 2010 notes](https://csmgeo.csm.jmu.edu/geollab/complexevolutionarysystems/Documents/Tierra2010.pdf).
- Avida (Ofria, Adami, Brown; first version 1993 at Caltech) gives each organism its own protected memory and virtual CPU — [Weblio Avida summary](https://www.weblio.jp/content/Avida). Lenski, Ofria, Pennock & Adami 2003 (Nature): populations evolved complex logic functions (EQU) built on simpler ones; first performers "differed from their non-performing parents by only one or two mutations"; some mutations harmful when they appeared later helped build complex features — [Lenski lab page](https://lenski.mmg.msu.edu/papers/nature2003/index.html); [Caltech record](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20150326-134627107). Chow, Wilke, Ofria, Lenski & Adami 2004 (Science) showed adaptive radiation from resource competition in Avida — cited in [Tierra 2010 notes](https://csmgeo.csm.jmu.edu/geollab/complexevolutionarysystems/Documents/Tierra2010.pdf).
- Polyworld (Yaeger 1994): agents with move/turn/eat/mate/attack/light/focus primitives under continuous control of a genome-specified ANN with Hebbian learning, vision, metabolism; "predation, mimicry, sexual reproduction, and even communication are all supported"; distinct species evolved and coexisted — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyworld); [Ars Electronica 1993](https://webarchive.ars.electronica.art/en/archives/festival_archive/festival_documentations/1993/yaege.html). Yaeger & Sporns: max population 300 agents, world seeded with 90 agents; when populations are not self-sustaining a steady-state GA with heuristic fitness keeps a minimum number of agents; a population cap with culling of least-fit agents produced "evolutionary slowdown"; they report "a consistent trend towards greater structural elaboration and adaptability, with a concomitant and statistically significant growth in neural complexity" — [Yaeger & Sporns PDF](https://shinyverse.org/larryy/YaegerSporns.EvoNeurComplex.pdf) (text extracted).
- Sims 1994 "Evolving Virtual Creatures": directed-graph genotypes for morphology and neural brains; fitness = swimming, walking, jumping, following (light); "population sizes were typically 300, and the survival ratio was 1/5"; "size 300, run for 100 generations, might take around three hours to complete on a 32 processor CM-5"; following behaviour "can be transformed into fleeing behavior" by negating the response — [SIGGRAPH 94 PDF](https://www.karlsims.com/papers/siggraph94.pdf) (text extracted).
- Lenia (Chan 2019, Complex Systems 28(3):251–286): continuous CA; "more than 400 species in 18 families" identified, many via interactive evolution; patterns are "geometric, metameric, fuzzy, resilient, adaptive, and rule-generic" — [arXiv:1812.05433](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05433); [Complex Systems](https://www.complex-systems.com/?p=3160).
- Flow Lenia (Plantec et al. 2022/23): mass conservation makes localized creatures "far more common" than in Lenia; update-rule parameters embedded as a local map so multiple species coexist (1024×1024, 144 creatures, 200k steps, mutations every 500 steps); OpenES with population 16 optimised directed motion/chemotaxis where original Lenia "found only exploding patterns"; the paper explicitly says "Demonstrating the emergence of intrinsic evolutionary processes has been left out of the scope" and does not test open-endedness — [ar5iv 2212.07906](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.07906).

**Modern open-ended / eco-evolutionary systems**
- POET (Wang, Lehman, Clune, Stanley 2019): 20 active environment–agent pairs; child environments kept only if the paired agent scores in [50, 300]; parents must score ≥ 200 to reproduce; up to 512 children per reproduction step; ES population 512, Adam lr 0.01→0.001, σ 0.1→0.01; controller 24 inputs, 2×40 hidden, 4 outputs; "each run used 256 parallel CPU cores and took about 10 days" (up to 25,200 iterations). Direct ES on POET-solved environments (5 runs, ≤16,000 steps) scored only 17.9, 39.6, 13.6, 24.0, 19.2 vs the 230 success threshold; mean iterations to solve: challenging 638 ± 133, very challenging 11,180 ± 343, extremely challenging 22,178 ± 368; ~49–54% of ~19,000 transfer attempts succeeded; without transfer no extremely challenging environment was solved (p < 2.2e-16) — [ar5iv 1901.01753](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01753).
- Neural MMO (Suarez et al. 2019): persistent world with "a large and variable number of agents"; larger populations encourage more skillful behaviour; agents with unshared weights "diverge into different niches to avoid competing" — [arXiv:1903.00784](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00784). NeurIPS 2022 challenge: 500 participants, >1,600 submissions — [arXiv:2311.03707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03707v1); NeurIPS 2023: top solution 4× baseline "within 8 hours of training on a single 4090 GPU" — [arXiv:2508.12524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12524v1).
- Hamon, Nisioti & Moulin-Frier 2023 (GECCO '23 Companion): 400×200 grid, 330 initial agents, max 1,000; each agent a 2,445-parameter conv(4,8)+LSTM(4)+dense(8) net; asexual local reproduction after energy above threshold for 140 steps, mutation σ = 0.02; death after 200 steps below threshold or age 650; 1,000,000-timestep runs in ~20 min on one GPU. Findings: Lotka-Volterra-like population/resource oscillations; "near tragedy of the commons" early; two coexisting strategies ("opportunistic travelers" and sustainable low-movement foragers; 9 of 50 tested agents were sustainable foragers); turning reproduction on raised consumption efficiency significantly; **"3 of 5 seeds collapsed after several generations, so hyperparameters needed tuning to keep the environment stable"**; a constant "sparse spontaneous growth" term (0.00005) keeps resources from vanishing, which the authors flag as "problematic in reset-free environments"; two seeds gave qualitatively different ecosystems due to resource-landscape differences — [arXiv:2302.09334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09334v1); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.09334).

**Remedies: novelty search and quality-diversity**
- Lehman & Stanley 2011 (Evolutionary Computation 19(2):189–223): in the deceptive maze, novelty search "evolves successful maze navigators … in significantly fewer evaluations"; on harder mazes objective search "almost always fails, while novelty search is successful in nearly every attempt"; also beats objective search on biped walking — [Lehman ECJ11 PDF](https://www.cs.swarthmore.edu/~meeden/cs81/f17/papers/lehman_ecj11.pdf); [GP bib](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Lehman_2011_EC.html). A Swarthmore student replication reported inconclusive results — [BillyMike.pdf](https://www.cs.swarthmore.edu/~meeden/cs81/f15/papers/BillyMike.pdf).
- MAP-Elites (Mouret & Clune 2015): returns a map of elites across user-chosen behaviour dimensions, "tends to find a better overall solution than state-of-the-art search algorithms", demonstrated on modular nets and soft robots — [arXiv:1504.04909](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909).
- Cully, Clune, Tarapore & Mouret 2015 (Nature): hexapod map of ~13,000 gaits in a 6-D behaviour space (5 bins/dim), built with 40 million evaluations taking "roughly two weeks on one multi-core computer"; arm map 200×100 = 20,000 cells with 20M evaluations; after damage the hexapod found compensatory gaits in ~66 s (IQR 24–134 s) and 8 trials (IQR 3–16), 33–77× faster than the reference gait; standard Bayesian optimisation in raw parameter space "does not find working controllers" — [ar5iv 1407.3501](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1407.3501); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14422).
- Cooperation/predator–prey ALife precedents: evolution of cooperative hunting in multi-layered societies — [arXiv:2005.11580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11580); evolved predator teams vs scripted prey on a torus grid comparing individual vs team fitness — [arXiv:1703.08577](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08577); Nitschke 2003 pursuit-evasion found a "multiple pools" approach best for role specialisation — [PDF](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~gnitschke/projects/papers/2003-Emergence%20of%20Cooperation%20in%20a%20Pursuit-Evasion%20Game.pdf).

### Inferences
- Collapse modes documented in the literature map onto three mechanisms: (i) ecological collapse (resource overexploitation → extinction; Hamon 3/5 seeds), (ii) evolutionary stagnation (MC too strict or too loose; fitness convergence; bounded representation), (iii) observer boredom (Hughes: novelty plateaus once the environment's problem space is exhausted; AdA ~1 month, POET once terrains are solved).
- Remedies with the best evidence: a steady-state GA floor that reseeds when population falls below a minimum (Polyworld), a sparse spontaneous resource regrowth term plus spatial climate gradients (Hamon), a minimal criterion tuned for "orderly divergence" (Soros 2016), QD archives to keep behavioural niches populated (MAP-Elites), and co-evolving the environment with the agents so problems grow with solutions (POET).
- Non-episodic, reset-free runs are the right ecological model for a months-long installation, but every reported such system needed hand-tuning to avoid collapse; a months-long run needs explicit safety rails (population floor, immigrant injection from an archive).

### Gaps
- Polyworld 1994 original paper text not retrieved (only Wikipedia/Ars Electronica/Yaeger–Sporns); Geb and Evosphere not examined.
- No 2023–2025 JAX predator–prey ALife study with quantitative collapse statistics beyond Hamon et al. was found before the search budget ran out; Biomaker CA (Randazzo & Mordvintsev 2023) was not retrieved.
- Hamon et al. main-text Discussion (future work on sexual reproduction/predators) not extracted; only appendix text was read.

## KQ4. ASAL (Kumar, Lu et al. 2024, Sakana AI): how VLMs score interestingness / open-endedness / target match; costs; limitations

### Takeaway
ASAL uses CLIP (and DINOv2 in an ablation) embeddings of rendered simulation frames to define three scalar objectives — target-prompt similarity, historical novelty ("open-endedness"), and set diversity ("illumination") — optimised with Sep-CMA-ES, a custom GA, Adam, or brute force; it recovers Game of Life in the top 5% of all 262,144 Life-like CA rules and finds novel Lenia/Boids forms, but reports no compute costs and does not measure alignment with human judgments.

### Cited Findings
- Authors/affiliations: Akarsh Kumar (MIT, Sakana AI), Chris Lu, Louis Kirsch, Yujin Tang, Kenneth Stanley, Phillip Isola, David Ha; project page asal.sakana.ai; code github.com/SakanaAI/asal — [arXiv:2412.17799v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799v2).
- Three mechanisms: "finding simulations that produce target phenomena, discovering simulations that generate temporally open-ended novelty, and illuminating an entire space of interestingly diverse simulations"; substrates: Boids, Particle Life, Game of Life, Lenia, Neural Cellular Automata — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17799).
- Models: CLIP for all supervised-target, open-endedness and illumination experiments; DINOv2 only in an illumination ablation on Lenia/Boids (vision-only, so cannot do text targets); "CLIP as slightly better than DINOv2, with both better than pixel-based representations" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799). The README exposes only `create_foundation_model('clip')`, embedding dimension 512 (which matches ViT-B CLIP variants; the specific checkpoint is not named in the extracted text) — [GitHub SakanaAI/asal](https://github.com/SakanaAI/asal).
- Metric definitions: (Eq. 2) supervised target = inner product between CLIP image embedding of the rendered state and CLIP text embedding of the prompt, averaged over prompted timesteps; (Eq. 3) open-endedness = minimise the maximum CLIP similarity between the frame at T and all earlier frames (nearest-neighbour historical novelty; "worked better than variance-based novelty"); (Eq. 4) illumination = minimise the max similarity between each simulation and its nearest other simulation — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799).
- Search budgets: Sep-CMA-ES population 16, 10,000 iterations (single-prompt targets on Lenia/Boids/Particle Life); Adam/TBPTT 1,000,000 iterations for NCA temporal targets; brute force over all 2^18 = 262,144 Life-like CA rules × 256 random initial states × 2,048 steps; illumination GA population 8,192, 32 parents mutated per iteration (σ 0.1), 100,000 iterations — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799).
- Substrate sizes: Boids 128 boids, 16 neighbours, 1,000 steps, substrate = weights of a shared NN; Particle Life 5,000 particles, 6 types; Lenia 45 dynamics dims + 3,072 initial-state dims, 256 steps; CA 64×64 grid — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799).
- Results: Conway's Game of Life ranks in the top 5% of Life-like CAs on the open-endedness metric; a Particle Life "caterpillar" appears only above a particle-count threshold; "many previously unseen" Lenia lifeforms (qualitative) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799).
- Limitations stated: optimisation failures usually due to substrate inexpressivity; NCA "too expressive" for open-endedness search so excluded; open-endedness search not run on Lenia/Boids because "novelty was hard to sustain in preliminary tests"; human alignment "argued from the FM's similarity to human representations, not measured"; frame-level image models only — "video-language FMs as future work" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799).
- Compute: not reported in paper or README; README: "Everything is implemented in the Jax framework, making everything end-to-end jittable and very fast", and GPU recommended — [GitHub SakanaAI/asal](https://github.com/SakanaAI/asal). Secondary coverage also found no hardware numbers — [comdig summary](https://comdig.unam.mx/2025/01/04/automating-the-search-for-artificial-life-with-foundation-models).

### Inferences
- The ASAL novelty score is a cheap, per-frame operation (one CLIP image encode + a max over stored embeddings), so it can run online on a rendered camera feed at a few Hz on a consumer GPU. (Speculation; no throughput figures were retrieved for CLIP — see gap.)
- ASAL's "illumination" objective is in effect a CLIP-space analogue of MAP-Elites' diversity, and its "open-endedness" score is a CLIP-space analogue of the Hughes et al. novelty criterion; both are directly reusable as *observer* metrics without any search at all.
- The paper's own warning that novelty was "hard to sustain" on Boids (a flocking substrate) is relevant: a sheep flock's CLIP embedding may saturate quickly, so a text-conditioned or event-conditioned score may be needed in addition to raw novelty.

### Gaps
- No compute, GPU type, or wall-clock figures for ASAL anywhere retrieved.
- Exact CLIP checkpoint not confirmed.
- No measured correlation between ASAL scores and human interestingness ratings.

## KQ5. Foundation models as curators, interest scorers, or evaluators (not brains): OMNI, OMNI-EPIC, Motif, Eureka, ELLM, Hughes et al.; VLM narration of game/sim events

### Takeaway
LLM/VLM-as-judge works measurably as a *curriculum filter* (OMNI: 14 vs 9 vs 3 interesting Crafter tasks learned for OMNI vs learning-progress vs uniform), as a *preference-based intrinsic reward* over captions (Motif: Llama-2-70B over 500k caption pairs; intrinsic-only agent beats score-trained agent, with documented misalignment-by-composition), and as a *reward-code writer* (Eureka: beats human rewards on 83% of 29 tasks, +52% normalised), but every study flags prompt sensitivity, Goodhart risk when the generator optimises against the judge, and VLM unreliability for fine-grained visual state (OMNI-EPIC: VLMs "not yet accurate enough" as success detectors; game-commentary VLMs hallucinate).

### Cited Findings
- **OMNI (Zhang et al. 2023)**: FM as "model of interestingness" (MoI); Crafter with 15 interesting tasks diluted by 90 "boring" numerical repeats and 1,023 extremely hard tasks; BabyAI expanded to 1,364 tasks; AI2-THOR infinite task space. Prompting: GPT-3 Davinci few-shot True/False on candidates given learned tasks (Crafter); GPT-4 "mentor" system prompt with reasoning then True/False (BabyAI); GPT-4 zero-shot proposing new tasks plus success-check code (AI2-THOR). Combination: learning-progress weights × 0.001 for tasks the MoI calls boring, then normalised. Median tasks learned (Uniform / LP / OMNI): Crafter 3 / 9 / 14; BabyAI 1 / 4 / 8; AI2-THOR 0 / 2 / 13; Crafter overall 82 vs 55 for LP; an oracle MoI performed almost the same as the FM. Costs: 24–60 h on one GPU; GPT-3 ≈ $0.02 per 1k tokens with caching. Limitations: Goodhart's law if the generator optimises against the MoI; synthetic "boring" tasks; malformed outputs regenerated — [ar5iv 2306.01711](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.01711); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01711).
- **OMNI-EPIC (Faldor, Zhang, Cully, Clune; ICLR 2025)**: FMs write environment + reward code (PyBullet), with a post-generation MoI that compares each new task to its nearest archive tasks and asks whether it is "novel, surprising, diverse, or worthwhile"; uninteresting tasks discarded; archive stores NL descriptions + code, retrieved via text-embedding-3-small; DreamerV3 as learner. Long run: 200 iterations without RL; short runs: one run completed 16 tasks, failed 6, 1 judged uninteresting; cell coverage and ANNECS-OMNI higher than controls (p < 0.05). "Current VLMs were not yet accurate enough" as success detectors; 50-person study: 72.7% agreement between humans and the success detector; limited by academic compute; no dollar cost reported — [ar5iv 2405.15568](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.15568); [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2405.15568); [ICLR 2025](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/29279).
- **Motif (Klissarov et al. 2023)**: Llama-2-70B-chat (chain-of-thought, no fine-tuning) gives preferences over pairs of NetHack message captions; 𝒟_pref = 5×10^5 pairs from ~10^7 observations, ~90% response rate; a 1-D char-conv reward model trained with cross-entropy (10 epochs, lr 1e-5); reward thresholded and normalised by episodic message counts; identical-message pairs (5–10%) auto-labelled "no preference". Agent trained on intrinsic reward alone "obtains a better game score" than one trained on score; combination best; steering prompts raised gold +106%, descents +17%, kills +150%; extrinsic-only agent kills its pet 99.4% ± 0.63% vs 33.4% ± 25.14% for Motif. "Misalignment by composition": on the oracle task the agent hallucinates (eats a yellow mold corpse) so the game registers a hallucinated oracle. LLM size matters (7B/13B/70B); prompt wording sensitivity "significant for narrowly defined tasks"; LLM bias toward longer messages; FLAN-T5-XXL hallucinated domain knowledge; using the LLM directly as policy "made no progress"; PPO at ~20k FPS on 20 cores + one V100 — [arXiv HTML 2310.00166](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00166); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00166).
- **Eureka (Ma et al. 2023, ICLR 2024)**: GPT-4 writes reward code; evolutionary search over reward candidates; across 29 environments and 10 robot morphologies beats human-engineered rewards on 83% of tasks with 52% average normalised improvement; first simulated Shadow Hand pen-spinning via curriculum; gradient-free in-context RLHF variant — [arXiv:2310.12931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12931).
- **ELLM (Du et al. 2023)**: LLM prompted with the agent's state description suggests goals; agent rewarded for achieving them; Crafter and Housekeep; more common-sense behaviours covered during pretraining and usually matched/improved downstream — [arXiv:2302.06692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06692).
- **Hughes et al. 2024**: FMs "capture human notions of interestingness", can act as "general mutation operators and assess the novelty and interestingness of data to decide what to explore"; proposes a "proxy observer" that "sits within the system and proactively guides it" toward what the true external observer will find novel and learnable; earlier proxies used TD-error — [ar5iv 2406.04268](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2406.04268).
- **VLM/LLM narration of game events for human viewers**: racing-game commentary benchmark (129,226 utterances, 1,389 races) found "the vision encoder had difficulty extracting useful information" while telemetry worked — [INLG 2021](https://aclweb.org/anthology/2021.inlg-1.11.pdf); a 2024 football-commentary thesis found the VLM "hallucinated less … but exhibited a higher overall error rate" than the LLM fed structured data — [U Twente thesis](https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/101002); a gameplay-narration prototype packs nine frames into a 3×3 image to infer motion and "still hallucinates game state at times" — [gamedev.net summary](https://gamedev.net/news/5119-content-based-video-narration-of-gameplay-with-vision-language-models/); a fine-tuned Llama-3.2-11B game-comment VLM's model card warns it "should not be relied on for factual game-state reporting" — [HF TK17250/game-comment-vlm](https://huggingface.co/TK17250/game-comment-vlm); a 2025 LoL esports study built a tri-modal (screenshot + JSON) dataset — [IJCNLP 2025 findings](https://preview.aclanthology.org/new-sigs/2025.findings-ijcnlp.112/); MIT tennis commentary pipelines CV-extracted metadata through a data-to-text LLM — [DSpace@MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/151608).

### Inferences
- Across the commentary literature the consistent pattern is: feed the FM *structured event data* (telemetry, JSON) and let it judge/narrate, rather than raw pixels; VLMs on raw frames are weakest exactly at fine-grained state. For a simulation that owns its own ground truth, there is no reason to make the FM infer state from pixels.
- The Motif recipe (LLM preferences over captions → small distilled reward model, run offline once) is the cheapest way to get a *persistent* interest scorer: a 70B-LLM pass over a few hundred thousand event-caption pairs is a one-off cost; the distilled model is then free at runtime.
- The Goodhart warnings (OMNI, Motif) apply with force if the *agents* are evolved against the FM judge; they apply far less if the judge only steers a camera. Steering the camera with the judge is close to the Hughes "proxy observer" role.

### Gaps
- No published system was found that uses a VLM/LLM specifically to direct a camera or select "what to show" in an ALife installation; the closest are OMNI's task filter and ASAL's frame scoring.
- No numeric NetHack score table was extracted from Motif (figures only).
- No work found applying FM interestingness judgments to a *population* of evolving agents rather than to tasks/environments.

## KQ6. Quality-diversity (MAP-Elites) as a tool for keeping a flock behaviourally diverse

### Takeaway
MAP-Elites and novelty search are the standard tools for keeping an evolving population spread across a behaviour space rather than converging; they are compute-heavy to fill (Cully: 40M evaluations, ~2 weeks on one multi-core machine for 13k hexapod gaits) but the resulting archive is a persistent reservoir of diverse elites that can be reinjected.

### Cited Findings
- MAP-Elites illuminates "a space defined by dimensions of variation" chosen by the user and "tends to find a better overall solution than state-of-the-art search algorithms" — [arXiv:1504.04909](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909).
- Hexapod repertoire ~13,000 gaits, 6-D behaviour space (duty factor per leg), 5 bins/dim, 40M evaluations, ~2 weeks on one multi-core computer; arm map 20,000 cells, 20M evaluations — [ar5iv 1407.3501](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1407.3501).
- Novelty search solves deceptive mazes where objective search "almost always fails" — [Lehman & Stanley 2011](https://www.cs.swarthmore.edu/~meeden/cs81/f17/papers/lehman_ecj11.pdf).
- Deep GA + novelty (archive p = 0.01, k = 25) solves Image Hard Maze where DQN/A3C/ES/GA fail — [arXiv:1712.06567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06567).
- Stanley/Lehman/Soros describe QD (NSLC, MAP-Elites) as combining "novelty with quality, so they seek the best solution in each niche" — [O'Reilly Radar 2017](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/open-endedness-the-last-grand-challenge-youve-never-heard-of/).
- ASAL's illumination objective (minimise max CLIP similarity to nearest neighbour, GA population 8,192) is a learned-descriptor QD variant — [arXiv HTML 2412.17799](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799).
- POET's environment population with minimal-criterion admission [50, 300] is a QD-style archive over environments; transfer succeeded in ~49–54% of ~19k attempts — [ar5iv 1901.01753](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.01753).
- Neural MMO: unshared-weight agents "diverge into different niches to avoid competing" — [arXiv:1903.00784](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00784).

### Inferences
- A MAP-Elites archive keyed on cheap behaviour descriptors (e.g. mean distance to flock centroid, time grazing vs vigilant, communication rate, fox-avoidance latency) can serve two roles at once: a diversity-preserving gene bank for reseeding after collapse, and a catalogue the camera can use to pick "a sheep that behaves unlike the ones shown recently". (Speculation.)
- Hand-chosen descriptors are cheap and interpretable; CLIP-space descriptors (ASAL-style) capture visual diversity but risk saturating on a visually uniform flock.

### Gaps
- No study found applying MAP-Elites to a continuously-running, non-episodic multi-agent population (all retrieved QD uses are episodic / offline).

## FLOCK 2.0 fit assessment (SEPARATE SECTION — my analysis and speculation, grounded in the citations above)

**Framing recap.** FLOCK 2.0: tens of sheep on a 128×128 tile meadow, each a small neural agent with learned behaviour and communication, lambs inheriting from parents, a nightly fox, a months-long continuous run, and a "documentary camera" choosing what to show.

### (1) Where evolutionary methods beat / complement gradient RL for these agents
- **Use evolution for the slow, non-differentiable, population-level layer; use lifetime learning for the fast layer.** Everything in KQ2 (Fernando 2018, DERL, Najarro & Risi, EPG, Singh/Barto, Niekum) points the same way: evolve priors (initial weights, Hebbian rule coefficients, drive/reward weights, maybe a morphology-like trait vector) and let each sheep learn within its life. DERL's ~10-generation Baldwin effect and Fernando's "Baldwinian wins when the task distribution is broad or changing" are direct evidence that lamb inheritance should be Baldwinian (inherit priors, re-learn) rather than Lamarckian (copy learned weights), with the Goal-Direction local-optimum failure as the cautionary case.
- **ES/GA is the natural fit for the FLOCK agent class.** FLOCK agents are small (likely ≤10k parameters, like Salimans' 2×64 MLP, Tang's 3.7k-param attention agent, or Hamon's 2.4k-param conv-LSTM), have sparse delayed rewards (surviving the night), long horizons, recurrent state, and discrete communication tokens — exactly the regime where Salimans et al. list ES advantages, and where Tang et al. chose CMA-ES for non-differentiable patch selection. Gradient RL's main advantage (sample efficiency on hard continuous control, 7–8× in Hopper/Walker) is not the binding constraint when the environment is a cheap tile sim.
- **CPU practicality.** With population 256–1,000 and ~10k parameters, the ES update is negligible; cost is dominated by rollouts. Salimans' 18-core / 11-hour Humanoid anchor and Hamon's 20-minute GPU run for 1,000 agents × 1M steps bracket the problem: a CPU-only pre-evolution on a workstation is feasible for the pre-training phase if the simulator is vectorised (NumPy/JAX); a $0.34–0.89/h 4090 makes it trivial. Beyond ~100k parameters ES becomes unreliable (evosax limitations), so keep the evolved portion small.
- **Recommended stack (speculation):** evosax (OpenAI-ES / Sep-CMA-ES / PGPE, all with the ask-tell API) or a Deep-GA with seed-list genomes (Such et al.) for the pre-training outer loop; Hebbian ABCD coefficients (Najarro & Risi; merged rules per Pedersen & Risi to shrink the genome to ~2k numbers) or a small gradient inner loop for lifetime learning; an evolved drive vector à la Singh/Barto/Niekum for the reward.

### (2) What open-ended ALife teaches about keeping a months-long population interesting and non-collapsing
- **Expect collapse and stagnation by default.** Hamon et al.'s 3-of-5-seed collapse in a reset-free neuroevolution world, Polyworld's need for a steady-state GA floor and the "evolutionary slowdown" from population caps, and Stanley/Lehman/Soros's "stop producing anything interesting within days" are the baseline expectation for FLOCK.
- **Concrete rails that the literature supports:**
  1. *Population floor with archive reseeding* (Polyworld's heuristic-fitness GA when not self-sustaining; QD archive as gene bank per MAP-Elites/Cully).
  2. *Resource dynamics with a spontaneous regrowth floor and spatial gradients* (Hamon's sparse spontaneous growth 0.00005 and latitudinal climate factor) so grass cannot be driven to zero permanently.
  3. *A minimal criterion for reproduction tuned for "orderly divergence"* (Soros 2016): e.g. a ewe must have survived N nights and reached an energy threshold; too strict → stagnation, too loose → stagnation.
  4. *Keep problems growing with solutions* (POET's [50, 300] admission band; Hughes' novelty-and-learnability): schedule the fox's competence, weather, or grazing geometry so difficulty tracks flock competence rather than staying fixed, which is the single mechanism with the strongest evidence against the "POET plateaus once all terrains are solved" failure.
  5. *Explicit diversity pressure* (novelty/QD) because reward-only selection converges; Neural MMO's unshared weights → niche divergence suggests per-lineage weights rather than one shared brain.
- **Boredom is an observer property.** Hughes' definition makes "interesting" = novel-and-learnable *to the observer*; AdA plateaued at ~1 month. For a months-long installation this argues for a camera/curator that measures its own novelty budget and for environmental events (seasons, fox cubs, new pastures) on a cadence shorter than the expected novelty plateau. (Speculation.)
- **Boids/flock caveat from ASAL:** novelty in CLIP space was "hard to sustain" on Boids, so a flock's visual novelty will saturate; interest must come from events and relationships (deaths, births, separations, calls), not from the flock's silhouette.

### (3) Can a foundation model be used as curator / interest scorer rather than brain?
- **Yes, and the evidence favours three concrete designs, in order of cost:**
  - *ASAL-style CLIP scorer on the rendered feed* — cheap per-frame novelty (max-similarity to history) and diversity scores, zero training; weakest on fine-grained state and likely to saturate on a flock; best used as one feature among several.
  - *Motif-style distilled preference model over event captions* — generate structured text captions from the simulator's ground truth ("ewe #12 left the flock at dusk with two lambs; fox 30 tiles east"), have a 70B open-weights LLM rank pairs once (Motif: 500k pairs), distil to a tiny reward model, and run it at zero marginal cost to rank candidate shots. The commentary literature's finding that structured data beats raw vision for VLMs supports captioning from ground truth rather than from pixels. Prompt steering raised targeted behaviours by +17% to +150% in Motif, which translates into a dial for the documentary's editorial stance.
  - *OMNI-style online LLM judge* — periodic calls ("given what has been shown in the last hour, which of these 10 candidate storylines is most interesting and learnable for a viewer?"), with learning-progress-like weights (OMNI multiplies boring tasks by 0.001). OMNI's result (14 vs 9 vs 3 tasks) is the best quantitative evidence that an FM filter adds value over a pure novelty/learning-progress heuristic. Cost at OMNI scale was 24–60 GPU-hours plus modest API spend.
- **Where not to use it:** as the agents' reward during evolution (Goodhart/misalignment-by-composition in OMNI and Motif), or as a pixel-level state estimator (OMNI-EPIC: VLM success detection only 72.7% aligned with humans; game-commentary VLMs hallucinate state). Using the FM only to steer the camera isolates the Goodhart risk to the camera, which is the lowest-stakes place for it.
- **Evaluation role:** Hughes' novelty/learnability criterion plus ASAL's historical-novelty score give an offline, observer-side metric to track whether the run is still open-ended; the Motif/OMNI judge gives a human-aligned "interestingness" metric. Both can be logged continuously and used to trigger environmental interventions when they flatten.

### Overall verdict (speculation)
FLOCK 2.0's requirements — tiny recurrent agents, sparse survival reward, inheritance, a non-episodic months-long world, and a human-facing camera — line up unusually well with the evolution-outer / learning-inner literature and with FM-as-proxy-observer rather than FM-as-brain. The risks with the most evidence behind them are ecological collapse and observer boredom, not optimisation difficulty; budget engineering effort accordingly (rails, minimal criterion, QD archive, difficulty scheduling, camera curator), and expect the one-off pre-training compute to be in the tens-of-dollars range on a rented 4090 at October 2026 prices.
