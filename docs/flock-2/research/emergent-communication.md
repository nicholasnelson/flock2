# Emergent Communication in MARL: Signalling Games, Failure Modes, Remedies (and fit for FLOCK 2.0)

Scope note: this file covers the general emergent-communication (EC) literature, discrete vs continuous channel theory, vocal/acoustic EC, costly signalling, spatial/partially-observable communication, evaluation, and sample complexity. Graphical/pictorial EC (drawing games) is covered by a separate researcher; iterated learning is covered by another researcher and only cross-referenced. Claims from the literature carry a link. Where I could only verify a paper's abstract (not the body) I say so. My own inference/speculation is confined to "Inferences" sub-sections and the final FLOCK section.

Research-process note: the most load-bearing sources (Lowe et al. 2019, Chaabouni et al. 2019, Li & Bowling 2019, Eloff et al. 2021/2023, Nunley 2026, Chaabouni et al. 2022, Eccles et al. 2019, Villanger & Alexandersen 2024, the 2026 collapse preprint, Lazaridou & Baroni 2020, the PLOS "Maynard Smith revisited" paper) were read from full text. Foundational 2016–2018 papers (Lazaridou 2017, Havrylov & Titov 2017, Foerster 2016, Mordatch & Abbeel 2018, CommNet/IC3Net/TarMAC/ATOC) are cited at abstract level from their arXiv pages and were not re-read in full this session.

## KQ1. Foundations: Lewis games, the canonical papers, and which training methods actually train reliably

### Takeaway
The canonical setup is a Lewis signalling/referential game (sender sees a target, emits discrete symbols, receiver picks target among distractors). The dominant trainers are REINFORCE (score-function) and (straight-through) Gumbel-Softmax; the literature is fairly consistent that Gumbel-Softmax/ST converges faster and more reliably when the loss is differentiable, that REINFORCE is high-variance and needs entropy bonus + baseline, and that the most robust discrete-channel recipe in recent large-scale work is "differentiable receiver + REINFORCE sender + entropy + KL-to-prior regularisation". DQN (value-based) has also been shown to beat REINFORCE for an acoustic channel. Fully continuous channels (DIAL-style) are the easiest to optimise but have their own collapse failure mode (KQ4).

### Cited Findings
- Lewis signalling game as the base model: "a modern Lewis signaling game" is the reference implementation in Facebook's EGG toolkit; EGG exposes both REINFORCE and Gumbel-Softmax via a common interface, and notes Gumbel-Softmax "only works when the game loss is differentiable" so its signalling game uses REINFORCE — [EGG README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/EGG) (repo is now archived/read-only).
- Lazaridou, Peysakhovich & Baroni (ICLR 2017): two agents play a referential game over natural images with single-symbol messages; communication succeeds, and grounding via an auxiliary supervised objective makes symbols more interpretable to humans — [arXiv:1612.07182](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07182) (abstract level).
- Havrylov & Titov (NeurIPS 2017): sequence-of-symbols referential game over images; the straight-through Gumbel-Softmax estimator was reported as "much faster to converge" than the REINFORCE approach and "results in more effective protocols" — [arXiv:1705.11192](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11192); the quotation is as surfaced in a search snippet of the paper, not re-read in full.
- Foerster et al. (NeurIPS 2016) introduced RIAL (communication as discrete RL action, REINFORCE-style/Q-learning) and DIAL (differentiable inter-agent learning: real-valued messages during centralised training with gradients flowing through the channel, discretised at execution, with channel noise to force discretisation) — [arXiv:1605.06676](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676) (abstract level).
- Mordatch & Abbeel (AAAI 2018): grounded compositional language emerges in a 2-D multi-agent environment where agents must move to landmarks; they use end-to-end differentiable training with Gumbel-Softmax on a discrete vocabulary and add a soft penalty on vocabulary size (a cost on using many symbols) to get compact, compositional protocols — [arXiv:1703.04908](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04908) (abstract level).
- Chaabouni et al. 2019 compared trainers directly: "two approaches are dominantly used: Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and REINFORCE... We also experimented with the approach of Schulman et al. [2015], combining REINFORCE and stochastic backpropagation... Preliminary experiments showed that the latter algorithm results in the fastest and most stable convergence" — [Chaabouni et al. 2019, arXiv:1905.12561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12561).
- The hybrid recipe (receiver updated by backprop, sender by REINFORCE with entropy regularisation and a baseline) is adopted "following previous work" because it "converges more robustly" — [Noukhovitch et al., AAMAS 2021 (PDF)](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2021/pdfs/p974.pdf).
- A 2025 paper (VQEL) summarises the field: "While Gumbel-Softmax often yields higher performance (Havrylov & Titov 2017; Chaabouni et al. 2020; Kharitonov et al. 2020), it relies on continuous approximations during training that deviate from strict discrete communication constraints" — [arXiv:2503.04940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04940).
- Carmeli, Meir & Belinkov (AAAI 2023) argue both REINFORCE and Gumbel-Softmax "result in poor performance compared to fully continuous communication" and propose quantised communication as a middle ground — [arXiv:2211.02412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.02412).
- Eloff et al. (continuous acoustic channel, see KQ4) found "DQN has significant performance gains and increased compositionality when compared to previous methods optimised using REINFORCE"; e.g. end-to-end acoustic agent without pretraining reached 0.611 train / 0.566 eval accuracy with REINFORCE vs 0.956 / 0.789 with DQN — [Eloff et al., arXiv:2111.02827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827).
- Lazaridou & Baroni's survey: "Tuning the weights of a neural network with an error signal that is back-propagated through a discrete bottleneck is a challenging technical problem"; options are REINFORCE or approximating discrete messages with continuous ones (Gumbel-Softmax) during training — [Lazaridou & Baroni 2020, arXiv:2006.02419](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02419v2).
- Stabilising discrete-channel RL at scale: without KL regularisation (β=0) training with 1024 candidates became unstable (mean train accuracy fell from 98.9% at 20 candidates to 76.0% with high variance); adding a KL term to a prior (β=0.5) gave 99.8% at 20 and 98.3% at 1024 candidates with low variance; the benefit is greatest when the entropy coefficient is low — [Chaabouni et al. 2022, "Emergent Communication at Scale" (ICLR 2022 full text)](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2022/emergent_communication_at_scale__d7f9d37e.txt); conference page: [ICLR 2022](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/spotlight/6460).
- Overview of EC as a tool for language-evolution research, historical context: [Moulin-Frier & Oudeyer 2020, arXiv:2002.08878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08878). This review notes most MARL EC "rel[ies] on an idealized communication channel where the signal produced by an agent is directly broadcasted to other agents" and that vocal control "is a classical robotic problem" (as summarised in search results).

### Inferences
- For a continuous-parameter bleat (pitch contour, duration, timbre), the speaker's output is naturally a continuous Gaussian policy; REINFORCE on continuous actions is standard but high-variance. If the receiver's loss is differentiable (e.g. receiver predicts a target), a DIAL-style fully differentiable channel (noise added) will be the easiest thing to make train at all; if the receiver's action is an RL action in the world (flee/graze), the sender must be trained with policy gradient or via a learned differentiable "listener-value" critic.
- The Eloff result (DQN >> REINFORCE for acoustic) is one of the few direct comparisons on a continuous channel and suggests value-based or actor-critic methods with replay should be tried before pure REINFORCE for sound.

### Gaps
- I did not find a systematic benchmark (same task, many seeds) comparing REINFORCE vs ST-Gumbel vs DIAL vs DQN across tasks; evidence is per-paper and partly anecdotal.

## KQ2. Failure modes

### Takeaway
The best-documented failure modes are: (1) "positive signalling without positive listening" — messages correlate with sender state yet have zero causal effect on the receiver (Lowe et al. 2019 show this quantitatively in matrix games); (2) agents ignoring the channel / communication failing to emerge at all without inductive bias (Eccles et al.: 0/50 runs succeed on Summing-MNIST without bias; 28% on Treasure Hunt); (3) anti-efficient encoding — frequent meanings get the longest messages, driven by a listener-side discriminability bias (Chaabouni 2019); (4) non-compositional, non-generalising protocols with compositionality not needed for generalisation (Chaabouni 2020) and topographic similarity not predicting generalisation at scale (Chaabouni 2022); (5) idiosyncratic/"private" codes that overfit the training partner and are opaque to humans (Lazaridou & Baroni 2020); (6) representational collapse on continuous channels (KQ4).

### Cited Findings
- Lowe, Foerster, Boureau, Pineau & Dauphin (AAMAS 2019) define positive signalling (messages statistically related to the sender's observation/action) and positive listening (messages influence the receiver's behaviour) and show "positive signaling ⇏ positive listening": in matrix games with a channel, agents' messages predicted their own next action (speaker consistency ≈ 0.20–0.54 depending on payoff size), yet causal influence of communication (CIC) over 1000 test games was within 2% of its minimum in 89.3% (2×2), 97.9% (4×4) and 99.9% (8×8) of games, i.e. changing the message had essentially no effect on the other agent — [Lowe et al. 2019 (AAMAS PDF)](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p693.pdf); arXiv: [1903.05168](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05168).
- Lowe et al.'s explanation: the message and action heads shared parameters, so messages leaked action information (speaker consistency fell to ~0.02–0.12 with a separate communication network) — [same source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p693.pdf).
- Lowe et al. also show that simply measuring reward gain from adding a channel "does not prove much" because the channel may just add capacity; entropy of the message policy "measures neither positive signaling nor positive listening"; instantaneous coordination "is not state-dependent" and "can miss many positive listening relationships" — [same source](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p693.pdf).
- Communication frequently fails to emerge at all in temporally-extended decentralised RL because of a "difficult joint exploration problem": with no bias, 0% of 50 Summing-MNIST runs and only 28% of 50 Treasure-Hunt runs reached "good" reward; Jaques-style social influence alone also gave 0% on Summing-MNIST — [Eccles et al. 2019, arXiv:1912.05676](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.05676v1).
- Anti-efficient encoding: "the most frequent inputs are associated to the longest messages"; messages "are skewed towards the maximum length threshold"; this is driven by a Listener-side bias — untrained Listeners' hidden representations are furthest apart for the emergent (long) messages, so they are "a priori easier for non-trained Listeners" — and "natural languages are not easy to process by Listeners. This suggests that the emergence of 'natural' languages in LSTM agents is unlikely, without imposing ad-hoc pressures" — [Chaabouni et al. 2019, arXiv:1905.12561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12561).
- Same paper: trained speakers develop a very skewed unigram distribution and a strong tendency to repeat the same symbol (P(s_r,s_r) > P(s_r)^2 in >97.5% of runs with max_len=30); agents "need much larger D [hidden size] than strictly necessary in order to converge"; mean 25 of 48 runs per configuration succeeded (>99% accuracy) — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12561).
- Compositionality vs generalisation: with large enough input spaces emergent languages generalise to novel composite concepts, but "we find no correlation between compositionality and generalization" and "compositionality does not arise from simple generalization pressure"; compositional languages are, however, easier to transmit to new learners even with different architectures — [Chaabouni et al. 2020, arXiv:2004.09124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09124) (abstract).
- At scale (ImageNet/CelebA), topographic similarity had non-significant Spearman correlation with generalisation (0.09 ImageNet, 0.08 CelebA, p>0.19) while ease-and-transfer-learning (ETL) correlated >0.90 — [Chaabouni et al. 2022](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2022/emergent_communication_at_scale__d7f9d37e.txt).
- "Natural language does not emerge naturally": in a multi-turn image dialog game, agents learned non-compositional, opaque codes unless constrained (e.g., no memory for the answer bot, small vocabulary) — [Kottur et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.08502](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08502) (abstract level).
- Lazaridou & Baroni survey failure catalogue: agents can "exchange information simply through the number of turns they take" regardless of content; under skewed input distributions frequent inputs get the longest messages; agents trained on pictures still "communicated about Gaussian noise", i.e. used shallow visual cues rather than concepts; agents form "very idiosyncratic conventions"; and because "the communication partners of agents during training and testing phases are the same", one cannot tell whether agents communicate in general or "overfit their interlocutors" — [Lazaridou & Baroni 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02419v2).
- Local optima in continuous channels: without a bias agents "get stuck in a local optimum where the same message is sent in two different states" — [Villanger & Alexandersen 2024, PMLR v233](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v233/villanger24a/villanger24a.pdf).
- Population size alone is not a cure: "little to no systematic benefit on emergent language properties when increasing the population size" (1, 10, 50 pairs; CelebA generalisation 89.00/91.06/90.69%; ImageNet 96.09/95.78/95.29%) — [Chaabouni et al. 2022](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2022/emergent_communication_at_scale__d7f9d37e.txt); Rita et al. find language properties "are not enhanced by population size alone" and that heterogeneity (e.g. different learning speeds) is what partially restores group-size effects — [Rita et al. 2022, arXiv:2204.12982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.12982).
- Review of what EC is missing relative to human language evolution (motivates most of the remedies in KQ3): [Galke, Ram & Raviv 2022, arXiv:2204.10590](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.10590); follow-up on learning and communication pressures: [Galke & Raviv 2024, arXiv:2403.14427](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14427).

### Inferences
- The Lowe result is the most important design warning for FLOCK: a flock whose bleats are correlated with internal state (and therefore look expressive to a viewer) can be entirely un-listened-to. Any "the sheep are talking" claim needs a causal (intervention) test, not a correlation.
- The Eccles numbers suggest decentralised, temporally extended settings (which FLOCK is) have a high base rate of total non-emergence; inductive biases or centralised training are near-mandatory.

### Gaps
- I did not find quantitative base rates for "protocol collapse to one symbol" in standard referential games; papers report success-run counts (e.g. Chaabouni 2019's 25/48) but not a taxonomy of how the failures failed.

## KQ3. Remedies

### Takeaway
Remedies with quantitative support: (a) explicit positive-signalling (entropy-shaped) and positive-listening (CIC-maximising) auxiliary losses raise emergence rate from 28% to 94% on Treasure Hunt and 0% to 100% on Summing-MNIST (Eccles 2019); (b) a per-symbol length cost α|m| flips anti-ZLA to ZLA encodings but slows convergence and reduces success rate (Chaabouni 2019), and combining a lazy speaker with an impatient listener gives near-optimal lengths (Rita 2020); (c) information-bottleneck objectives (VQ-VIB) yield human-like lexicon sizes and faster convergence (Tucker 2022); (d) periodically resetting listeners increases topographic similarity (0.59 vs 0.51) and ease-of-teaching (Li & Bowling 2019); (e) population + imitation + voting beats plain population (93.13% vs 90.69% CelebA) and KL regularisation stabilises RL (Chaabouni 2022); (f) partner heterogeneity rather than raw population size (Rita 2022); (g) channel noise and limited capacity force redundancy/compositionality (Eloff; Resnick). Iterated learning is the other major remedy, covered elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- Positive-signalling bias: minimise −E[λH(π̄_M) − (H(m_t|x_t) − H_target)^2], i.e. maximise marginal message entropy while pulling conditional entropy to a target (≈ log|A|/2), avoiding the "disconnected policy space" of direct MI maximisation. Positive-listening bias: listener minimises the negative L1 distance between its message-conditioned action distribution and an auxiliary message-free predictor (an L1 proxy for multi-step CIC; L1 was more stable than KL). Results: Summing-MNIST proportion of good runs none 0.00 / listening 0.88 / signalling 0.98 / both 1.00; Treasure Hunt none 0.28 (all-run reward 12.45±0.48) / signalling 0.84 / listening 0.64 / both 0.94 (15.14±0.33) — [Eccles et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.05676v1).
- Social-influence reward (reward agents for causally influencing others' actions) as an intrinsic motivation that yields emergent communication in sequential social dilemmas — [Jaques et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.08647](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08647) (abstract level); note Eccles found it insufficient alone on Summing-MNIST (0/50) — [Eccles et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.05676v1).
- Length penalty: loss L' = L(i, L(m)) + α|m| (handled by REINFORCE since it depends only on speaker params); with max_len=30, a=40, α=0.5 "the emergent messages clearly follow ZLA" but encoding is "not as efficient as ... natural language", and "when adding length regularization, we noted a slower convergence, with a smaller number of successful runs, that further diminishes when α increases" — [Chaabouni et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12561).
- LazImpa: speaker "made increasingly lazy, i.e. avoids long messages" and listener "impatient" (guesses as early as possible); "near-optimal and ZLA-compatible messages" emerge only when both modifications are present — [Rita, Chaabouni & Dupoux 2020, arXiv:2010.01878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01878) (abstract).
- Information bottleneck: VQ-VIB (vector-quantised variational IB) "compress[es] inputs into discrete signals embedded in a continuous space"; "encouraging informativeness leads to faster convergence rates and improved utility"; "penalizing complexity leads to human-like lexicon sizes while maintaining high utility"; VQ-VIB "achieving higher utility for any given complexity"; tested in MARL and colour reference games where agents "continuously adapt to changing communicative needs and align with human languages" — [Tucker et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2207.00088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00088); NeurIPS page: [poster 55058](https://nips.cc/virtual/2022/poster/55058); journal version in Open Mind 2025 — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/170586).
- Ease-of-teaching via listener resets: reset regime trains listener for 6k iterations then re-initialises, 50 listeners sequentially (300k iterations total) vs one listener for 300k; both reach ~97% communication success; topographic similarity rises to 0.59 under resets vs falling to 0.51 without; languages from the reset regime are on average easier to teach to new listeners and teaching speed keeps increasing with training; a compositional (topsim 1.0) language is learnt much faster than a permuted one (topsim 0.13) — [Li & Bowling, NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1906.02403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02403). (Explicit link to iterated learning: the paper says "A similar regime, iterated learning (Smith et al., 2003b), has been studied in the language evolution literature" — same source. Iterated learning proper is covered by the other researcher.)
- Scale + populations + imitation/voting: 1, 10, 50 pairs; listener resets every 51k steps; "imitation and voting gave more consistent gains" (50 pairs + imitation + voting: 93.13% CelebA generalisation vs 90.69% plain 50 pairs); KL regularisation (β=0.5) fixed instability at 1024 candidates — [Chaabouni et al. 2022](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2022/emergent_communication_at_scale__d7f9d37e.txt).
- Heterogeneous populations (agents with different learning speeds) partially restore the beneficial effect of group size on language structure — [Rita et al. 2022, arXiv:2204.12982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.12982) (also [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/on-the-role-of-population-heterogeneity-in-emergent-communication/)).
- Channel noise and capacity: an acoustic speaker over a noisy channel "learns redundancy to improve Listener coherency" and "develops more compositional communication protocols which implicitly compensates for transmission errors over a noisy channel" (topsim 0.707–0.747 acoustic vs 0.691 discrete with DQN) — [Eloff et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827). Capacity limits on the message channel are studied in [Resnick et al. 2020, "Capacity, bandwidth, and compositionality", arXiv:1910.11424](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11424) (abstract level; not re-read).
- Differentiable-channel trick (DIAL): train with real-valued messages and gradient flow, add noise during training so the protocol becomes robust to discretisation at execution — [Foerster et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676) (abstract level).
- Vocabulary-size cost and physical grounding produce compact, compositional, human-interpretable symbols in a 2-D landmark world — [Mordatch & Abbeel 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04908) (abstract level).
- Continuous-channel inductive bias (a "continuous positive signalling" loss = pairwise repulsion of messages within a batch on an n-torus, L_IB = (1/B^2)Σ max(−λ1 d + λ2, 0)) raised Continuous Sequence Guess from 0.780±0.034 (RL only) to 0.958±0.010; the discrete positive-signalling bias made things worse in one setting (0.551 vs 0.693 RL-only) while the continuous bias helped (0.803) — [Villanger & Alexandersen 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v233/villanger24a/villanger24a.pdf).
- Pragmatics (RSA-style recursive reasoning) and the "what's missing" agenda — [Galke, Ram & Raviv 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.10590). I did not fetch a dedicated RSA-in-EC paper this session (see Gaps).
- Partner-overfitting critique and the recommendation to evaluate with unseen partners: [Lazaridou & Baroni 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02419v2).
- Application-oriented review (what EC is for, hence what to measure), with the three-way split internal / task-driven / knowledge-driven uses — [Boldt & Mortensen, TMLR 2024, arXiv:2407.03302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03302v1).

### Inferences
- The two cheapest, best-evidenced levers for FLOCK are Eccles-style positive-signalling/listening auxiliary losses (they attack the "no emergence" failure directly) and a message cost (attacks anti-efficiency and makes "cheap vs costly channel" a real pressure). Resetting/rotating listeners (Li & Bowling) maps naturally onto a flock with births/deaths.
- Chaabouni 2019's finding that length cost slows convergence and reduces success is a warning that FLOCK's "drawing is costly" should be phased in (curriculum) rather than imposed from step zero.

### Gaps
- No single paper I found combines all remedies; interaction effects (e.g., length cost + resets) are unreported.
- I did not locate a quantitative RSA/pragmatics-in-EC result in this session.

## KQ4. Continuous channels, collapse, and vocal/acoustic emergent communication

### Takeaway
Continuous-vector channels are easier to optimise and usually score higher, but (i) ~25% of continuous seeds suffered representational collapse vs 0% discrete in a 2026 study; (ii) continuous channels let a receiver "dominate" a sender under conflict; (iii) a discrete bottleneck acts as a regulariser. The only substantial published RL work on a genuinely acoustic channel (Eloff et al.) uses a phone sequence → TTS (eSpeak) → room impulse response + background noise → mel-spectrogram listener, i.e. the continuous channel is the acoustic transmission, not a learned continuous articulatory parameter like pitch; it shows noise induces redundancy and higher compositionality. Vervet-style alarm-call emergence has been modelled with evolved CTRNN agents (Nunley 2026) and with tabular-Q "Maynard Smith revisited"; I found no deep-RL paper where agents learn pitch/duration/timbre parameters of a call.

### Cited Findings
- Continuous messages: 5/20 continuous seeds (25%) collapsed during training vs 0/20 discrete (Gumbel-Softmax) seeds; continuous had weaker compositional structure (selectivity 0.807 discrete vs 0.722 continuous, p<0.05) at comparable holdout accuracy; authors credit the discrete bottleneck as a regulariser — [arXiv:2604.03266 (2026 preprint, 4-agent collision dataset, V-JEPA 2 features)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03266v1). Single preprint; not yet replicated.
- Continuous protocols "generally outperform discrete ones, even when the target information is discrete", attributed to smoother optimisation and to one-hot discrete encodings growing linearly with alphabet size; but continuous agents get stuck sending "the same message in two different states" without a repulsion bias — [Villanger & Alexandersen 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v233/villanger24a/villanger24a.pdf).
- Under competition, continuous messages let the receiver dominate the sender whereas the discrete model stayed relatively fair; communication "can emerge in competitive settings" with amount tracking degree of cooperation, and "both agents must benefit from communication for it to emerge" — [Noukhovitch et al., AAMAS 2021](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2021/pdfs/p974.pdf) (continuous-vs-discrete claim is from a search snippet of this paper; the PDF fetch failed), abstract at [arXiv:2101.10276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10276).
- Discrete messages can match continuous performance with a much smaller vocabulary, but very large discrete bandwidths hurt learning — [Lin et al. 2021, arXiv:2102.12550](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.12550) (abstract level via search).
- Quantised (VQ) communication as a middle path between continuous and discrete — [Carmeli et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.02412).
- Spontaneous discretisation of continuous signals: agents with a continuous channel can spontaneously discretise and compositionalise signals — [Geffen Lan, Chemla & Steinert-Threlkeld 2020, arXiv:2005.00110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00110) (title/abstract level); biological/cognitive review of continuous vs discrete vocal signalling (loudness, pitch, duration, timbre as continuous properties of acoustic units) — [PMC8381766](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8381766/).
- Acoustic EC (Eloff, Räsänen, Engelbrecht, Pretorius, Kamper; arXiv 2021, v2 2023): Speaker GRU emits a phone sequence (default phone set {a,e,i,o,u}, max length L=5 or 8), eSpeak synthesises a waveform, channel f(w) = (w + n) * h with n background noise from Clotho and h a room impulse response from the AIR database (train rooms: booth, lecture, office; unseen: meeting, stairway); Listener is a CNN on mel-spectrograms; attribute task N=4, M=5 (625 combinations); agents trained with DQN; "All models are trained until convergence, which always occurs before 50 training epochs" — [Eloff et al., arXiv:2111.02827](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827).
- Eloff Table 1 (DQN; train rooms / eval rooms / topsim): Discrete 0.649/0.649/0.691; Acoustic E2E 0.956/0.789/0.519; Acoustic* (pretrained) E2E 0.986/0.958/0.707; Acoustic+PhoneRec 0.682/0.674/0.747; Acoustic*+PhoneRec 0.726/0.710/0.745; max SD 0.02 over 5 seeds. Table 2: with 10 dB SNR background noise in the unseen stairway room, Discrete 0.604 (L=5) / 0.544 (L=8) vs Acoustic*+PhoneRec 0.664 / 0.640; the discrete model's performance drops when allowed longer messages under noise (more chance a phone is lost), while the acoustic speaker exploits extra length for redundancy, up to "13.3% better relative performance" — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827).
- The acoustic speaker learns more repetition of bigrams than the discrete speaker, i.e. redundancy as noise compensation — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827).
- Alarm-call emergence (evolved agents, not RL): "minimal predator avoidance task" abstracting the vervet paradigm (predator targets one of two cover types; sentinel agent gets the warning; partner must rely on communication); 10×10 toroidal continuous space; CTRNN agents that hear their own calls; >2,000 evolutionary runs, 112 perfect-fitness agents (~5% of runs); three strategies cover 81%: safety calling 39% (call from safe cover), alarm indication 22% (call whenever predator present), self-regulatory calling 20% (depend on hearing own call to sustain escape); self-hearing dependency 47% among threat-time callers vs 10% among safety callers (p<1e-4); removing self-hearing drops self-regulatory callers to fitness 0.40 while safety callers stay at 0.90 — [Nunley 2026, arXiv:2606.02840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02840) (preprint, not peer-reviewed).
- Earlier evolved-CTRNN referential communication (Williams, Beer et al. 2008) and extensions to displaced communication are cited as the lineage of that work — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02840).
- Honest costly alarm/need signalling under MARL: see KQ5 (Sir Philip Sidney game result).
- Swarming/escape behaviours emerge from predator–prey survival pressure alone (no communication channel) — [arXiv:2308.12624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.12624) (title/abstract level via search).
- Implicit (non-channel) signalling: in pursuit tasks with curricula, agents exchanged "more than twice as much information (in bits)" through movement than baselines — [Grupen et al., AAMAS 2022](https://ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2022/pdfs/p553.pdf), arXiv [2106.11156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.11156) (search-snippet level).

### Inferences
- There is essentially no published deep-RL work in which agents learn a few continuous articulatory parameters (pitch contour, duration, timbre). The closest analogues are (a) continuous-vector message channels (which collapse/degenerate ~25% of the time and need repulsion/entropy-like biases) and (b) Eloff's phone-sequence-through-acoustics pipeline. FLOCK's bleat design is therefore novel-ish; expect the continuous-channel failure modes (collapse to one point, receiver dominance) rather than the discrete ones (anti-efficiency).
- Adding acoustic-style channel noise (random perturbation of pitch/duration scaled by distance) is the one well-supported lever for making a continuous bleat protocol robust and more "compositional" (Eloff; DIAL).

### Gaps
- No paper found with learned pitch/duration/timbre parameters in RL; "emergent vocalisation" searches returned only Eloff and the evolved-CTRNN line. The "Discretisation and continuity" review (PMC8381766) was not read in full.
- Nunley 2026's call parameterisation (whether calls are scalar on/off or continuous) was not extracted; the abstract describes "vocalizations" and a 3-output CTRNN but I could not confirm the signal dimensionality.

## KQ5. Cost, urgency, and honesty: costly signalling in emergent communication

### Takeaway
Classical theory (Zahavi handicap; Grafen) says costs stabilise honesty under conflict of interest; Lachmann, Számadó & Bergstrom (2001) argue that cost-free signals can be honest when lying is punished or interests overlap, which is why cheap human language works. The one MARL test I found (tabular Q-learning on the Sir Philip Sidney game) found no honest costly signalling under any tested parameters: agents converge on "proactive prosociality" (give without signal) or no-signal equilibria. In fully cooperative EC (the usual setting) cost acts as an efficiency/length pressure (Chaabouni 2019; Mordatch & Abbeel's vocabulary penalty), not an honesty device. I found no MARL experiment where cheap and costly channels coexisted and specialised.

### Cited Findings
- Lowe et al. explicitly scope out cost: they assume communication cost rc = 0 "and leave an examination of costly signaling (e.g. [16, 46]) to future work" — [Lowe et al. 2019](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p693.pdf).
- "Maynard Smith revisited" (PLOS Comput Biol 2025): Sir Philip Sidney game with Beneficiary (thirsty with prob p) and Donor; signal costs c = 0.25 (weak) and 0.75 (strong); inclusive-fitness rewards (own payoff + r × partner's); tabular Q-learning, Q-tables init 0, 500 rounds per dyad (first 50 random exploration), 1000 runs per parameter set, learning rates {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}, discount {0.1, 0.9}. Result: "honest costly signalling did not emerge under any tested parameters"; "the Beneficiary never prefers to signal when c > 0"; the game "provides no incentive for such behaviour"; agents instead learn proactive prosociality (Donor gives regardless) or keep/no-signal. Numbers: in Case 1 (S=0.2, V=0.2, r=0.5) Donor keeps the resource in ≥80.9% of iterations; LR 0.9 / DR 0.1 gives the highest rate of the expected strategy; in Case 2 B's optimal strategy learned only 46.1% of the time while D "always gives" 94.6% — [PLOS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013302](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013302); also [PMC12440204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440204/).
- Lachmann, Számadó & Bergstrom 2001, "Cost and conflict in animal signals and human language", PNAS 98(23):13189–13194 — [PNAS doi:10.1073/pnas.231216498](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.231216498). The fetch attempt in this session hit a wrong DOI/403, so the following is from my prior knowledge of the abstract and is NOT verified here: the paper argues that honest signalling does not require the signals themselves to be costly when the cost falls on dishonest signallers (e.g., punishment or verification of cheats), and that this explains how cheap conventional signals like human words can be honest. Treat as "needs verification".
- Length cost as an efficiency pressure (cooperative setting): α|m| regulariser yields ZLA-like codes at the price of slower convergence and fewer successful runs — [Chaabouni et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12561); lazy speaker + impatient listener yields near-optimal lengths — [Rita et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01878).
- Vocabulary-size penalty as cost in a grounded 2-D world — [Mordatch & Abbeel 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04908) (abstract level).
- Communication vs manipulation under partial conflict: communication emerges in partially competitive games in proportion to cooperation, and the authors distinguish "communication from manipulation" — [Noukhovitch et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10276).
- Complexity (information-rate) cost via IB trades off against utility and informativeness, producing human-like lexicon sizes — [Tucker et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00088).

### Inferences
- In a fully cooperative flock (shared reward), there is no incentive to lie, so costly-signalling-for-honesty is irrelevant; cost will function purely as a bandwidth/efficiency pressure. If FLOCK introduces individual rewards (e.g., competition for grass), honesty becomes an issue and the PLOS result suggests tabular/simple RL will not discover handicap-style honest signalling on its own.
- The "cheap sound ↔ urgency, costly drawing ↔ information" hypothesis has no direct MARL precedent that I could find; the closest theoretical support is the IB/complexity trade-off (Tucker) plus the ZLA/length-cost results: agents under a cost pressure allocate short/cheap signals to frequent, high-utility-per-bit events. Whether "urgent" maps to "frequent" or "high-utility" depends on FLOCK's reward design (speculation).

### Gaps
- No MARL paper with two channels of different cost that specialise. Not found in this session.
- Lachmann et al. 2001 content not verified (fetch failed).

## KQ6. Partial observability and spatial broadcast: CommNet, IC3Net, TarMAC, ATOC, grid-world grounding

### Takeaway
Architecturally, the broadcast-in-radius channel has standard precedents: CommNet (continuous averaged broadcast), IC3Net (gated per-agent decision whether to broadcast, works in competitive/mixed settings), TarMAC (targeted, attention-weighted continuous messages, multi-round), ATOC (attention-gated, local communication groups). Grid-world studies show emergent messages that spatially cluster the state space and refer to locations/directions, and that populations give basic compositional structure. These are all continuous-vector, differentiable-channel methods trained centrally; none use acoustic parameters.

### Cited Findings
- CommNet: agents communicate through a continuous vector channel, with messages averaged across agents and the whole system trained by backpropagation; applied to multi-agent tasks with variable numbers of agents — [Sukhbaatar, Szlam & Fergus 2016, arXiv:1605.07736](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07736) (abstract level).
- IC3Net: individualised controlled continuous communication; each agent learns a gating action deciding when to communicate (broadcast), enabling use in cooperative, competitive and mixed settings; tested on predator–prey, traffic junction, StarCraft — [Singh, Jain & Sukhbaatar 2019, arXiv:1812.09755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09755) (abstract level).
- TarMAC: targeted multi-agent communication using signature-based soft attention so senders can address specific receivers, with multi-round communication; continuous messages, centralised training — [Das et al. 2019, arXiv:1810.11187](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11187) (abstract level).
- ATOC: an attention unit decides when an agent should communicate and forms local communication groups; bidirectional LSTM channel; shown in cooperative navigation and predator–prey with large numbers of agents — [Jiang & Lu 2018, arXiv:1805.07733](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07733) (abstract level).
- Grounded gridworld communication: agents learning navigation "develop a communication protocol that is understandable to humans"; emergent signals "spatially cluster the state space" and refer to locations/directions ("left", "up", "upper left room"); "In many cases the solutions are optimal"; populations yield basic compositional structure — [Kajić, Aygün & Precup 2020, arXiv:2004.01097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01097) (abstract).
- Treasure Hunt (gridworld, 5×5 partial views, one of five symbols per step, 500-step episodes): without inductive bias only 28% of runs develop useful communication — [Eccles et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.05676v1).
- Mordatch & Abbeel's landmark world: physically grounded, compositional language with agents that must also act (move) — [arXiv:1703.04908](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04908) (abstract level).
- Alarm-style spatial task with cover types and sentinel/receiver roles (continuous 10×10 toroidal space) — [Nunley 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02840).
- Critique that idealised broadcast channels ignore the physics of signalling — [Moulin-Frier & Oudeyer 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08878).
- Social learning via emergent communication in MARL (agents learn from each other's messages, scaled populations) — [arXiv:2302.14276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14276) (title level via search; not read).

### Inferences
- A radius-limited broadcast with learned "whether to bleat" is essentially IC3Net's gate plus a distance mask; the literature trains these with centralised critics and differentiable channels. FLOCK would be departing from precedent by making the channel non-differentiable (physically rendered audio) unless the listener's input is the sender's raw parameters with noise (DIAL-style) rather than a rendered waveform.
- Neural MMO: I did not find evidence of learned communication in Neural MMO in this session (gap).

### Gaps
- No quantitative results extracted for CommNet/IC3Net/TarMAC/ATOC (abstract-level only). Neural MMO communication: not found.

## KQ7. Evaluation: measuring whether a protocol is meaningful

### Takeaway
Use a battery, not one number: (1) Δreward with/without channel — necessary but weak; (2) positive signalling metrics: speaker consistency (MI between message and sender action/obs), context independence; (3) positive listening metrics: instantaneous coordination (MI between one agent's message and the other's next action) and, preferably, causal influence of communication (CIC: intervene on the message, measure change in receiver's action distribution); (4) structure: topographic similarity (Spearman ρ between meaning-distance and message-distance), posdis/bosdis; (5) transfer: ease-of-learning / ease-and-transfer-learning to fresh listeners, which at scale predicts generalisation far better than topsim; (6) zero-shot human interpretability. Also test with unseen partners to detect interlocutor overfitting.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions and recommendations: positive signalling = messages statistically dependent on the sender's observation/action; positive listening = messages influence the receiver's behaviour; speaker consistency (SC) = MI between an agent's message and its own action; context independence (CI) measures concept→message alignment; instantaneous coordination (IC) = MI between one agent's message and the other's next action, "not state-dependent" so it "can miss many positive listening relationships"; message entropy is "useful for diagnosing policy behaviour, but not as a metric"; Δr "should always be measured"; CIC "should always be used to determine effect communication has on agent behaviour" — [Lowe et al. 2019, Table 4](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p693.pdf).
- One-step CIC algorithm: for each test game, iterate over all possible messages m_j, compute receiver action distribution p(a|m_j), and accumulate MI between message and action; naive CIC "will be computationally expensive" over all messages/agents — [same](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2019/pdfs/p693.pdf).
- Multi-step CIC(x_t) = H(a_t | x'_t) − H(a_t | x_t) where x'_t removes all messages from the trajectory (used by Eccles as a trainable bias) — [Eccles et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.05676v1).
- Topographic similarity = (negative) Spearman ρ between pairwise meaning distances and pairwise message (edit) distances; perfect compositional language → 1.0 — [Li & Bowling 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02403); same definition used by Eloff — [Eloff et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827).
- Topsim "does not tell us anything about the nature of the specific compositional processes" — [Lazaridou & Baroni 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02419v2). New disentanglement-inspired compositionality measures (posdis, bosdis) — [Chaabouni et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09124).
- At scale topsim does not predict generalisation (ρ≈0.08–0.09, n.s.) whereas ease-and-transfer-learning does (>0.90) — [Chaabouni et al. 2022](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2022/emergent_communication_at_scale__d7f9d37e.txt).
- Ease-of-teaching: train a fresh listener for a fixed budget (1000 iterations) and measure how fast success is reached; compositional languages are learned faster — [Li & Bowling 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02403).
- Selectivity (block-zeroing ablation: ratio of targeted vs non-targeted property disruption when a message block is zeroed) as a compositionality probe for continuous/discrete messages — [arXiv:2604.03266](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03266v1).
- Human-likeness benchmark (XferBench: transfer of an emergent corpus to human-language tasks) — mentioned in [Boldt & Mortensen 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03302v1) context (search-level; not read).
- Evaluate with partners not seen in training — [Lazaridou & Baroni 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02419v2).

### Inferences
- For an art installation, the single most defensible test is a live CIC-style intervention: mute or scramble a bleat and measure whether neighbours' movement distribution changes. This is cheap for a few continuous parameters (sample perturbations) and is what the literature says is the only metric that establishes listening.

### Gaps
- No standard numeric threshold for "meaningful" CIC or topsim exists; values are relative.

## KQ8. Practical sample complexity

### Takeaway
Published referential games converge in tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand gradient steps/episodes with batch sizes in the hundreds–thousands; e.g. 6k iterations for ~97% success in a simple attribute game (Li & Bowling), <50 epochs over 625 inputs for the acoustic attribute game (Eloff), 300k–900k steps at batch 1024 for image-scale games (Chaabouni 2022), and 500-step episodes × 50 runs for Treasure Hunt with many runs failing entirely (Eccles). Adding length cost or decentralised multi-step RL increases steps and reduces success rate.

### Cited Findings
- "after around 6k iterations agents can achieve high communication success rate"; final ~97% at evaluation after 300k iterations; listener reset every 6k iterations — [Li & Bowling 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02403).
- "All models are trained until convergence, which always occurs before 50 training epochs" on a 625-combination attribute space with hidden size 256 GRUs; 5 seeds, max SD 0.02 — [Eloff et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02827).
- Max 300k steps (CelebA) / 900k (ImageNet), batch 1024, LR 1e-4, 10 seeds; ETL 10k steps at LR 1e-3, batch 4096; listener resets every 51k steps — [Chaabouni et al. 2022](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2022/emergent_communication_at_scale__d7f9d37e.txt).
- 1000 inputs with power-law frequencies; success = >99% accuracy; mean 25/48 runs succeeded; length regularisation gives "slower convergence, with a smaller number of successful runs" — [Chaabouni et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12561).
- 50 runs per setting; 500-step episodes; without biases 0% (Summing-MNIST) and 28% (Treasure Hunt) of runs succeed — [Eccles et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.05676v1).
- Evolutionary alternative: >2,000 runs for 112 perfect-fitness agents (~5%) — [Nunley 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02840).
- Tabular Q-learning in a 2-player signalling game: 500 rounds per dyad, 1000 repeats — [PLOS 2025](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013302).
- EGG's MNIST tutorial example uses `--n_epochs=50` and warns it "might take a while to run" — [EGG README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/EGG).
- Toy continuous-channel games: 30 runs each, noted "unstable learning in Negotiation" — [Villanger & Alexandersen 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v233/villanger24a/villanger24a.pdf).

### Inferences
- A FLOCK-scale referential sub-game (tens of meanings, few continuous parameters) should be in the 10^4–10^5 interaction regime if trained offline/centrally; embedding it inside a live, decentralised, multi-step environment pushes it toward the Eccles regime (many seeds fail outright) unless auxiliary biases are used. Pre-training the channel in a simple referential game and then dropping it into the world (Eloff's "Acoustic*" pretraining raised eval accuracy from 0.789 to 0.958) is the most evidence-backed shortcut.

### Gaps
- Wall-clock figures are rarely reported; none found for the canonical games.

## FLOCK 2.0 FIT ASSESSMENT (separate section; contains my inference and speculation)

What the literature supports directly
1. Communication can be learned from scratch in referential and grounded settings; it is routinely done with tens of agents (CommNet/IC3Net/ATOC) — but almost always with centralised training, differentiable channels and shared reward.
2. The "sheep ignore the channel" and "sheep signal but nobody listens" outcomes are the default, not the exception (Lowe; Eccles 28%/0% base rates). FLOCK needs (a) centralised training or Eccles-style positive-signalling/listening auxiliary losses, and (b) an intervention-based CIC test wired into the installation's telemetry.
3. Continuous bleat parameters put FLOCK in the continuous-channel regime: easier optimisation but ~25% collapse rate per seed and "same message for different states" local optima; remedies are a message-space repulsion/entropy term (Villanger) and channel noise (DIAL; Eloff). Rendering the bleat to audio and listening via a spectrogram (Eloff-style) is possible but currently needs DQN-class training and pretraining to work well; a cheaper route is for listeners to receive the sender's parameters + distance-scaled noise, with audio rendered only for the audience.
4. Cost/urgency: there is no MARL precedent for two coexisting channels of different cost specialising. Theoretical support for the designer's hypothesis is indirect: (i) length/complexity costs push frequent/high-value meanings onto short signals (ZLA results; IB trade-off), (ii) honesty-via-cost is irrelevant under fully shared reward and did not emerge under RL even when the game nominally supported it (PLOS 2025). So "cheap sound = urgency" would have to be engineered through reward timing (e.g., predator events that are rare, time-critical and high-reward) rather than through signal cost per se. This is a genuinely open question and a legitimate novel contribution if FLOCK measures it.
5. Interpretability for a human audience: the literature is pessimistic about emergent codes being human-readable (Kottur; Lazaridou & Baroni). The levers that make protocols more structured/teachable are listener resets (Li & Bowling), heterogeneity (Rita), iterated learning (other researcher), and cost. For sound, "interpretable" probably means "consistent and context-aligned" (speaker consistency / context independence visualised live), not semantically decodable.
6. Sample complexity: pre-train bleat and drawing channels in offline referential games (10^4–10^5 interactions), then fine-tune in-world with resets as sheep are born/die. Expect multi-seed failure; keep the best seeds.

Risks / where FLOCK departs from precedent
- Non-differentiable, physically rendered acoustic channel with few continuous parameters: no published deep-RL result; expect to borrow from Eloff (noise, redundancy) and continuous-channel bias work.
- Two-channel cost specialisation: unstudied; design the experiment so the null result (both channels used interchangeably, or one channel ignored) is detectable via CIC per channel.
- Decentralised, open-population, mixed-horizon training: the hardest regime in the literature.
