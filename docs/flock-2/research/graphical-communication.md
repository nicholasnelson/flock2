# Emergent graphical / pictorial communication, iconicity, and fit for FLOCK 2.0

Research notes compiled 2026-10-22. Every literature claim carries a link. Items marked **[speculation]** or in "Inferences" sections are my own reasoning, not sourced claims. The FLOCK 2.0 fit assessment is the final section and is clearly labelled.

Context recap (from the brief): FLOCK 2.0 sheep see an egocentric 16x16, 16-colour view (~1 px/tile of a 128x128 meadow) and can produce a 16x16, 16-colour image, either shown transiently (speech bubble) or stamped persistently on the ground where it is later seen through the normal view. Hypothesis under test: if received drawings go through the SAME visual encoder as the world view, iconic drawings should be easier to decode than arbitrary codes, biasing emergence toward iconic, human-readable marks.

## KQ1. Mihai & Hare 2021, "Learning to Draw: Emergent Communication through Sketching" (NeurIPS 2021) and the differentiable rasteriser

### Takeaway
Agents communicate photos to a receiver by emitting 20 straight-line strokes through a differentiable rasteriser, with a frozen ImageNet VGG16 as the visual front-end on both sides; the game loss alone produces drawings that are "indecipherable to a human", and iconicity only appears when an explicit perceptual (feature-matching) loss against the photo is added. The receiver applies the same frozen VGG16 to sketch and photos, which is the closest published analogue of FLOCK's "shared encoder" condition, and it did NOT by itself produce iconic drawings.

### Cited Findings
- Game: referential game where "one agent draws a sketch of a given photo and the second agent guesses, based on the drawing, the corresponding photo from a set of images"; the drawing procedure is differentiable so training is end-to-end; with "appropriate inductive biases" drawings become human-interpretable — [arXiv:2106.02067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02067); [NeurIPS 2021 proceedings](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/39d0a8908fbe6c18039ea8227f827023-Paper.pdf)
- Datasets and distractors: STL-10 at 96x96 (main), Caltech-101 at 128x128, CelebA at 112 px; 99 distractors in the "original" game, 9 in the object-oriented (OO) games; human study uses K=9 — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Game variants: *original* (target = sender's photo), *OO-same* (target = sender's photo, all candidates from disjoint classes), *OO-different* (receiver's target is a *different* photo of the same class, forcing class-level rather than instance-level drawing) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Drawing action space: default 20 straight constant-width black lines on a white canvas the same size as the input; each line is 4 numbers (start/end coordinates in [-1,1]); ablations at 5/10/20/30/40/50 lines; alternative point-cloud rasteriser (10/50/100 points). **No Bezier curves** in the NeurIPS paper. Rasteriser: squared-distance-transform per segment with sigma^2 = 5e-4, soft-or composition, inverted to black-on-white — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Encoders: sender = frozen VGG16 truncated at relu5_3 (ImageNet or Stylized-ImageNet weights) -> linear 64-d (1024-d "wide") -> 3-layer MLP -> line parameters. Receiver = frozen VGG16 applied independently to the sketch and to each candidate photo, then 2-layer MLP; score = dot product between sketch and photo features. The paper does not explicitly state whether the two agents share VGG weights, but both are frozen VGG16 — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Loss: multi-class hinge game loss plus an optional *perceptual loss* = squared differences of unit-normalised VGG features at relu1_2 ... relu5_3 between sketch and photo (style-transfer-style content loss); Adam 1e-4, 250 epochs — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Agent communication success: STL-10 original 71.8% (game loss only) vs 69.6% (+perceptual); OO-same 95.5% vs 96.0%; OO-different 82.7% vs 81.1%; i.e. the iconicity-inducing loss costs almost nothing in game success — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Line-count ablation (original game): 20/10/5 lines -> 69.6/69.5/73.4%; 30/40/50 -> 71.1/70.0/69.2% — success is nearly flat in stroke budget — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Capacity ablation (Caltech-101, OO-diff, 101 classes): baseline 64-d bottleneck 50.5% vs 1024-d "wide" 65.0% — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Human evaluation (6 people per group, 150 games, chance 10%): game-loss-only sketches -> humans 8.3% (at chance) while agents 100%; +perceptual loss, 20 lines -> humans 38.3% (class-level 55.6%); +perceptual, 50 lines -> 37.2% (47.8% class); OO-diff +perceptual 20 lines 23.9%, 50 lines 38.9% — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Drivers of interpretability: perceptual loss; later VGG blocks (3-5) matter most (blocks 1-2 alone give unrecognisable drawings; relu3_3 alone fails to converge); shape-biased Stylized-ImageNet backbone yields more shape-like sketches (CelebA: head pose, hairstyle, glasses); OO games push toward class-level symbols; more lines allow shorter strokes and more recognisable objects — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Failure modes: game-loss-only sketches "indecipherable to a human"; sketches "tend to cover the object's overall spatial extent rather than depict it"; 10-point point-cloud agents communicate (75%) but drawings are meaningless to humans; forcing all sketches toward an arbitrary dog image still allows 69.2% communication (i.e. the channel can carry hidden codes humans cannot read); OOD generalisation is poor (CelebA-trained on STL-10: 15.8-30%) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Differentiable rasteriser paper: "Differentiable Drawing and Sketching" (Mihai & Hare, arXiv:2103.16194) reformulates rasterising a pixel in terms of the primitive's distance transform and relaxes it so primitive parameters (points, lines, curves) are learnable; applications: optimising sketches against photographs and unsupervised raster-to-vector auto-encoders of handwritten digits — [arXiv:2103.16194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16194)
- Follow-up: "Physically Embodied Deep Image Optimisation" (Mihai & Hare 2022) uses the same rasteriser to optimise strokes then exports G-code to a drawing robot — [arXiv:2201.08142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.08142)
- Companion study on encoder bias: "The emergence of visual semantics through communication games" (Mihai & Hare 2021): supervised-ImageNet pretrained extractors "inductively bias the emergent communication channel to become more semantically aligned"; random-frozen and learned-from-scratch extractors align *less* with semantics yet play the game *better* (comm. rate 0.90 pretrained vs 0.93 random vs 0.94 learned; top-5 target-class count 1.86 vs 1.69 vs 1.50), suggesting hash-like codes; SimCLR-style augmentation on both sides raises semantics but lowers success (learned model: 0.72 rate) — [ar5iv 2101.10253](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2101.10253)

### Inferences
- The strongest single lesson for FLOCK: a frozen, shared, pretrained visual encoder on the receiver side was *not* enough to make drawings iconic; what made them iconic was a direct feature-space similarity loss between the drawing and the referent. The receiver's dot-product scoring can be satisfied by any code the sender can reliably emit, so without the extra term the channel drifts to "cover the spatial extent" codes.
- Agent success was insensitive to stroke budget (5-50 lines), which suggests the receiver needed only tens of bits even with 100 candidates (log2(100) ~ 6.6 bits per decision).
- The 64-d -> 1024-d bottleneck ablation (+14.5 points on 101 classes) shows that the pre-rasteriser latent, not the canvas, was the binding constraint.

### Gaps
- No public code repository for the NeurIPS paper was found in searches; implementation details come from the paper text only.
- The paper does not state whether sender and receiver VGG weights are literally the same tensor, only that both are frozen VGG16.

## KQ2. Qiu et al. 2022, "Emergent Graphical Conventions in a Visual Communication Game" (NeurIPS 2022)

### Takeaway
A sender draws up to 7 steps of 5 strokes each via a pretrained differentiable stroke renderer seeded by a pretrained photo-to-sketch module; the receiver (pretrained VGG16 + linear heads) can guess early or wait. With co-adaptation and a discount factor penalising late answers, sketches shrink from iconic to abbreviated conventions (rabbit -> ear, giraffe -> neck) while keeping category separability, and agents revert to longer, more iconic drawings for unfamiliar referents.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract: two neural agents; sender sketches on a canvas; a new RL method "evolves the agents jointly toward successful communication and abstract graphical conventions"; three properties defined: iconicity, symbolicity, semanticity; co-evolved agents "switch between conventionalized and iconic communication based on their familiarity with referents" — [arXiv:2111.14210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14210); [NeurIPS 2022 page](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/550ff553efc2c58410f277c667d12786-Abstract.html)
- Data: Sketchy dataset, 40 categories x 10 images (chosen because the sketching module handles them); 30 training categories; test on unseen instances of trained categories and 10 unseen categories; receiver sees M=3 distractors + target (4 options) — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Action space: canvas blank each round; each step the sender emits 5 strokes, each a continuous parameter vector in (0,1); maximum T=7 steps; canvas resolution not stated — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Sender pipeline: a pretrained edge-to-sketch module (Kampelmuhler & Pinz) produces a target pixel sketch; a sketching module based on Huang et al. 2019 "Learning to Paint" draws stroke by stroke with a pretrained differentiable neural renderer; gradients flow through the renderer to the sender — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Receiver: pretrained VGG16 (4096-d) + two linear heads (query/key); softmax over similarity to candidates plus a "wait" option — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Reward: shared, +gamma^t for a correct guess, -gamma^t for incorrect, gamma = 0.85 (0.95 in ablations); **no explicit stroke-count penalty** — communication cost is only implicit via discounting of late answers — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Algorithm: multi-agent policy-gradient surrogate with a learned value function and eligibility-trace (TD(lambda)-style) returns; baseline REINFORCE converges more slowly and is high-variance at gamma=0.95; Adam 1e-4, 30k iterations, batch 64, 5 seeds, ~20 GPU-hours per run; 2,000-iteration sender warm-up — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Settings: *complete* (early decision allowed, both updated), *max-step* (receiver decides only at step 7), *sender-fixed*, *one-step* (single drawing step), *retrieve* (unevolved sketches) — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Metrics: iconicity = accuracy on unseen instances/classes (proxy for visual resemblance); symbolicity = accuracy of a VGG16 fine-tuned to classify evolved sketches into 30 categories; semanticity = correlation between word2vec category distances and VGG feature distances of sketches — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Results: complete 98.1% seen / 70.4% unseen-instance / 39.4% unseen-class with avg steps 1.03 / 2.36 / 3.76; sender-fixed 99.6 / 71.8 / 45.4 with 2.41 / 3.83 / 4.75 steps; max-step 86.3 / 67.9 / 38.4 (7 steps); one-step 22.9 / 14.1 / 9.6; retrieve 99.5 / 76.8 / 48.0 (7 steps) — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Average step count first rises then falls in complete and sender-fixed (compared by the authors to Lewis's "systematic reduction"); complete uses fewer steps than sender-fixed, read as evidence that mutual adaptation drives abstraction — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Qualitative: "the drawer gradually simplifies the drawing but keeps the most salient parts of the target concept"; "the sender is inclined to use the first several strokes to depict the most salient parts"; rabbit sketches converge on the ear, giraffe on the neck — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.14210)
- Semanticity table: 0.43 complete, 0.36 sender-fixed, 0.41 max-step, 0.31 one-step, 0.55 retrieve; the text claims complete "better retained" semantics than retrieve, which the table contradicts (internal inconsistency) — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Early-decision failure mode: max-step (no early guessing) performs worse than complete, attributed to the sender not learning each stroke's marginal value; one-step fails to communicate at all; controls show no consistent conventions — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Limitations stated: sender relies on two pretrained components (photo-to-sketch converter and stroke module) and "an ideal sender would not need the pixel-level conversion"; generalisation only tested on 10 unseen categories — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Project page listed in the PDF: sites.google.com/view/emergent-graphical-conventions; no GitHub repo surfaced in searches — [Yixin Zhu publication page](https://yzhu.io/publication/teaming2022neurips/)

### Inferences
- Iconicity in Qiu et al. is *inherited* from the pretrained photo-to-sketch module, not discovered: the sender starts from an iconic sketch and learns which strokes to keep. Symbolisation (fewer strokes) is driven purely by temporal discounting, not by a reward term on ink. This is a clean demonstration that a cheap "time/step cost" is sufficient pressure for abbreviation.
- The "revert to iconic for unfamiliar referents" result depends on the receiver being able to score sketch-vs-photo similarity with a general-purpose encoder (VGG16). That is the mechanism FLOCK hopes to get from a shared encoder, but here the encoder is a strong ImageNet model, not a tiny from-scratch encoder.

### Gaps
- Canvas resolution and exact stroke parameterisation (Bezier control points, width, colour) are not stated in the HTML text I retrieved; the neural renderer is the Huang et al. 2019 one (see KQ6).
- I could not fetch the project page to confirm code availability.

## KQ3. Human graphical conventions: Fan, Hawkins et al.; what human data says about iconicity-to-symbol drift

### Takeaway
Human Pictionary-style reference games show rapid simplification (fewer strokes, faster, more efficient) across repetitions, but drawings do *not* become arbitrary: they keep the visually most diagnostic features and simplify by pragmatic selection of what distinguishes the target from its distractors; efficiency gains are partly specific to the particular partner.

### Cited Findings
- Fan, Hawkins, Wu & Goodman (Computational Brain & Behavior 2020; arXiv 2019): pairs played a drawing reference game; sketchers in easier different-category contexts "reached high recognition accuracy while using less effort"; two faculties posited: *visual abstraction* (linking object to drawing) and *pragmatic inference* (choosing what distinguishes target from distractors); a sketcher model combining a deep CNN with a probabilistic program fits human data and beats lesioned variants — [arXiv:1903.04448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04448); [Springer DOI 10.1007/s42113-019-00058-7](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s42113-019-00058-7)
- Hawkins, Sano, Goodman & Fan (Nature Communications 2023; arXiv 2021): 69 dyads (+65-dyad replication), ShapeNet chairs in two sets of 8 visually similar chairs, 4 candidates per trial; 4 "repeated" objects drawn once per block over 6 blocks, 4 "control" objects only pre/post; 30 s on a 300x300 canvas, no erasing; viewers can answer when confident; accuracy and speed bonuses — [arXiv HTML 2109.13861](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.13861); [Nature Comms DOI](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v14y2023i1d10.1038_s41467-023-37737-w.html)
- Results: communicative efficiency rose across repetitions (b=0.5, p<0.001); first-repetition accuracy 76% vs 25% chance; strokes fell steadily (b=-0.216, p<0.001); repeated objects improved more than control objects pre->post (+14.5% vs +7.1%, interaction p=0.002); successive drawings of the same object became more similar within a dyad (CNN features, b=0.53); drawings of the same object from *different* dyads grew less similar (b=-1.4); viewers yoked to one sketcher improved more than those shown shuffled drawings (+15.8% vs +5.6%); repeated objects became *more diagnostic as they became sparser* (b=-0.05, p<0.001, crowdsourced part annotations) — [arXiv HTML 2109.13861](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.13861)
- Conclusion: drawings "did not drift toward 'arbitrariness,' as a pure convention-based account would predict, but preserved visually diagnostic features"; visual resemblance is the foundation and shared interaction history increasingly determines meaning — [arXiv abstract 2109.13861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13861v1)

### Inferences
- For FLOCK the relevant human regularity is: *simplification plus diagnosticity*, not symbolisation into arbitrary glyphs, when (a) the referents are visually similar, (b) there is per-trial feedback, and (c) time/effort costs exist. Qiu et al.'s rabbit-ear result is the computational replication of this.
- The partner-specificity result (yoked vs shuffled) warns that with tens of sheep and churn in who meets whom, conventions may fragment into dyad-specific dialects unless marks are broadcast (persistent ground stamps seen by many are a natural community-wide channel; cf. Fay 2010 on communities in KQ5).

### Gaps
- The Nature Communications page could not be fetched directly (login redirect); figures come from the arXiv HTML version of the same study.
- Fan et al. 2020 full numbers (effort/stroke counts) were not retrieved; only the abstract-level claims.

## KQ4. Other emergent visual communication where the message IS an image (or image-like) fed to a visual encoder

### Takeaway
Beyond the two sketching papers, the closest work is (a) the 2025 "Signification Game" (agents paint Bezier splines on a canvas and process signals with the same perception network as the environment), (b) Evtimova et al. 2018 (binary-vector messages, bandwidth matters for generalisation), and (c) pixel-input referential games (Lazaridou 2018; Dessì 2021) which show that from-scratch CNN agents happily exploit low-level pixel statistics and degenerate "hash" protocols unless augmentation forces semantic robustness.

### Cited Findings
- **Signification Game (arXiv 2502.01568, 2025):** decentralised-POMDP iterated multi-agent RL where agents "paint parameterized curves on a greyscale canvas, a signaling space meant to resemble early proto-writing"; Part I senders output parameters for two Bezier curves rendered with Gaussian noise (MNIST, 5 agents); Part II uses three splines on a background matched to the mean CIFAR-100 image (10 classes, 10 agents); sender outputs a Gaussian over spline parameters; both policies trained with PPO; canvas resolution not stated — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)
- Shared perception in that paper: "In Part I, agents process environment observations and signals with the same perception network, and cannot distinguish between them" (pretraining status not stated); in Part II agents *can* tell pictographs from observations and apply an inferential "mirror cognition" model only to pictographs — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)
- Results: deceptive/adversarial signalling yields *iconic replicas* (near-copies of stimuli), "only possible when the signaling space overlaps the environment stimuli"; cooperative signalling "starts with iconic signals that drift into abstract symbols", attributed to size/curvature penalties and to fewer agents interacting with the environment directly; purely behaviourist agents "learn slowly and send symbols that do not resemble observations"; inferential agents communicate "roughly half of all referents almost immediately"; failure mode "signification gaps": referents whose stimuli are complex relative to the signalling medium cannot be communicated — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)
- Iconicity probe in that paper: a CNN classifier trained on MNIST/CIFAR-100 applied to the pictograph; high output entropy = more abstract sign — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)
- **Evtimova, Drozdov, Kiela & Cho (ICLR 2018), multi-modal multi-step referential game:** messages are d-dimensional binary vectors from factorised Bernoulli distributions (argmax at test), adaptive conversation up to 10 turns, trained with cross-entropy + REINFORCE with learned baselines, RMSProp 1e-4; in-domain accuracy@6 96.6%, accuracy@1 86.0%; in-domain accuracy did not change with d but out-of-domain accuracy correlated strongly with d ("higher communication bandwidth improves generalization"); 32-d messages reached up to 45% accuracy@7 out-of-domain — [ar5iv 1705.10369](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1705.10369); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10369)
- **Lazaridou, Hermann, Tuyls & Clark (ICLR 2018), symbolic and pixel input:** REINFORCE with entropy regularisation; symbolic study: alphabet capped at 100 (effective 10/17/40 symbols used for max length 2/5/10), 5 candidates, train acc 92.0/98.2/98.5%, novel-object test 74.2-81.6%; pixel study: speaker and listener each an 8-layer ConvNet trained only from the reward; game A (19 distractors) 93.7% train / 93.6% test; "structured (compositional) language is most likely to emerge when agents perceive the world as being structured"; lower ConvNet layers of the two agents are highly aligned (rho 0.97-0.91 near the pixels) and object *position* is linearly decodable at 85.9-95.3% while shape is 41.2-90.2% — [ar5iv 1804.03984](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.03984); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03984v1)
- **Dessì, Kharitonov & Baroni (NeurIPS 2021), interpretable communication from scratch:** ResNet-50 sender/receiver trained from scratch on ILSVRC-2012, 127 distractors, single symbol from a 2048-symbol vocabulary via Gumbel-Softmax; "+shared" = one CNN shared by sender and receiver; without augmentation accuracy is 91-93% but the Gaussian-blob sanity check shows the protocol relies on low-level pixel patterns (84.7% "success" on noise blobs with shared CNN); with SimCLR-style augmentation accuracy is 81.5-82.2% in-distribution, 72-74% OOD, and the noise check falls to chance (0.8%) — [arXiv HTML 2106.04258](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04258); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04258)
- **Heterogeneous visual encoders (arXiv 2605.11695, 2026):** two agents with different frozen visual encoders exchange discrete captions in a Metropolis-Hastings captioning game on MS-COCO; "all cross-agent metrics decline as encoder mismatch increases"; moderate heterogeneity gives fewer shared sequences but keeps per-sequence visual specificity; stronger heterogeneity gives fewer, coarser, more asymmetric sequences — [arXiv:2605.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11695)
- **Emergent Quantized Communication (Carmeli et al., arXiv 2211.02412):** Appendix A.2 argues the referential game has a trivial solution under continuous messages if "the sender and receiver use the same encoder" and the channel can carry the encoded target in one message ("the sender can simply send the encoded target to the receiver"); results: continuous and quantised channels reach ~1.0 on the object game even at 10,000 candidates while Gumbel-Softmax collapses (0.152 recurrent, 0.008 instant); image game at 100 candidates: continuous-instant 0.962, quantised-instant 0.962, Gumbel-Softmax-instant 0.132; with long enough words, quantised *binary* alphabets perform almost as well as continuous — [arXiv HTML 2211.02412](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02412)

### Inferences
- The Signification Game is the only work I found that explicitly implements FLOCK's "same encoder for world and message" assumption; its reported pattern (iconic start, drift toward abstract symbols under size/curvature penalties; iconic replicas when signal space overlaps stimuli) supports the *direction* of FLOCK's hypothesis but the paper gives few numbers and no ablation isolating the shared-encoder factor.
- Dessì et al. and Lazaridou et al. together show that from-scratch CNN agents, left to themselves, will coordinate on low-level pixel statistics (position, texture, noise) rather than semantics. For FLOCK, which trains tiny encoders from scratch, this is the default outcome to expect unless augmentation or an auxiliary objective pushes the encoder toward object-level features.
- The Carmeli et al. argument cuts both ways for FLOCK: a shared encoder plus a high-capacity continuous channel makes communication *trivial* (sender just transmits its embedding) and therefore not iconic; a 16-colour pixel grid is a quantised channel, so the trivial solution is blocked only to the extent the quantisation and the receiver's conv front-end make embedding-copying impossible.

### Gaps
- I found no paper where the message is a raw small pixel image (e.g. 16x16) produced by a pixel-level Bernoulli/categorical head and decoded by the receiver's world-perception CNN. "Emergent communication through pixels", "CommunicatingDrawings", and "Shape Bias" as named in the brief did not resolve to identifiable papers in my searches; the shape-bias content lives inside Mihai & Hare 2021 (Stylized-ImageNet experiment).

## KQ5. Iconicity theory: Garrod et al. 2007, Fay et al. 2010/2014, Theisen et al. 2010

### Takeaway
The experimental-semiotics literature holds that icons become symbols through *interaction with feedback*, not through repetition alone: with feedback drawings simplify and lose iconicity; without feedback they get more complex and stay iconic; communities converge on shared conventions that keep enough residual iconicity to be legible to outsiders, whereas isolated pairs develop idiosyncratic local systems.

### Cited Findings
- Garrod, Fay, Lee, Oberlander & MacLeod (Cognitive Science 31(6), 961-987, 2007): "It is argued that icons evolve into symbols as a consequence of the systematic shift in the locus of information" from the sign itself to users' memory of how it has been used, supported by an interactive grounding process — [Semantic Scholar TLDR](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/03640210701703659?fields=title,abstract,tldr,year,citationCount); [Wiley DOI](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1080/03640210701703659)
- Task: whiteboard drawing to communicate predetermined concepts (Art gallery, Drama, Arnold Schwarzenegger, Television, Parliament ...), no speech or text, roles alternate; "simple repetition of drawings was insufficient to produce simplification and abstraction. This occurred only when there was a feedback from the addressee"; example: "Parliament" goes from an iconic debating chamber to "a simplified symbolic form (two lines plus a circle)" — [Fay et al. 2008, PMC2607338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607338/)
- Secondary summaries: refinement "only occurred when partners could interact graphically, even if that only meant ticking a drawing to confirm comprehension"; in the no-feedback control "drawings became more complex and kept their iconic character"; complexity scored by perimetric complexity ((inside + outside perimeter)^2 / ink area); passive observers identified meanings less accurately than interacting partners — [search summary citing Fay 2018 and others](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cogs.12600); [Fay et al. 2008 PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607338/)
- Information-theoretic rationale (Fay et al. 2008 discussion): icons work by resemblance so their complexity tracks the referent's physical complexity; symbols need only enough structure to be distinguished from other symbols, so they can be simpler; hence "it makes sense for icons (or indices) to evolve into symbols" — [PMC2607338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607338/)
- Fay, Garrod, Roberts & Swoboda (Cognitive Science 2010), "The Interactive Evolution of Human Communication Systems": "Interaction was critical to the creation of shared sign systems"; isolated pairs developed local systems, 8-person communities developed distinct global ones — [Semantic Scholar TLDR](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1111/j.1551-6709.2009.01090.x?fields=title,abstract,tldr,year); [Wiley DOI](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2009.01090.x)
- Fay et al. 2008 ("fitness and functionality of culturally evolved communication systems"): community signs after 7 rounds were identified by naive overseers more accurately than isolated-pair signs (simple effect F(1,30)=14.68, d=1.27) and decoded better (F(1,30)=11.17, d=1.08); round-1 (iconic) images were detected better than round-7 at 10-30 ms exposures (F(1,15)=10.99); community signs "retain more residual iconicity" — [PMC2607338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607338/)
- Garrod, Fay, Rogers, Walker & Swoboda (Interaction Studies 2010), "Can iterated learning explain the emergence of graphical symbols?" — exists in the Glasgow repository but full text not retrievable — [Glasgow eprints 45926](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/45926)
- Fay, Ellison & Garrod (Pragmatics & Cognition 2014), "Iconicity: from sign to system": "Iconic signs tend to become simplified and symbolic as the communication system matures, and the process is driven by repeated interactive use of the signs" — [jbe-platform](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/pc.22.2.05fay)
- Fay, Garrod, Lee & Oberlander (CogSci 2003), "Understanding Interactive Graphical Communication": partners' representations converge and are refined, and through interaction "lose their iconicity, taking on a more symbolic form" — [Edinburgh research portal](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/understanding-interactive-graphical-communication/)
- Theisen, Oberlander & Kirby (Interaction Studies 11(1), 14-32, 2010), "Systematicity and arbitrariness in novel communication systems": participants invented signs from scratch for items sharing semantic features; "systematic reuse of arbitrary signal elements arose through interaction" — [Edinburgh research portal](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/systematicity-and-arbitrariness-in-novel-communication-systems/); [Benjamins](https://benjamins.com/catalog/bct.45.02the)
- Tamariz, Roberts, Martinez & Santiago (Cognitive Science 2018), "The Interactive Origin of Iconicity": the iconicity effect appeared in the communication condition but not the reproduction condition — [HW research portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/the-interactive-origin-of-iconicity/)

### Inferences
- Two conditions recur as necessary for the icon-to-symbol shift: a *feedback signal* from the addressee and *repeated encounters of the same referent with the same partners*. FLOCK's reward structure needs an analogue of the addressee's tick (e.g. a shared reward when a receiver acts correctly on a mark) or symbolisation will not have a gradient to follow.
- The Fay 2008/2010 community results suggest persistent ground marks seen by many sheep (a community channel) should produce more globally shared, more residually iconic conventions than transient bubbles between pairs.

### Gaps
- Full text of Garrod 2007 and Garrod 2010 were paywalled/blocked (Wiley 403, ResearchGate 403); numbers come from Fay et al. 2008 (open access) and secondary summaries. Exact complexity-reduction percentages from the 2007 paper were not obtained.

## KQ6. Action spaces that train: pixels vs strokes vs stamps vs latent decoders; RL vs differentiable channels

### Takeaway
Published drawing agents use either (a) a differentiable analytic rasteriser (Mihai & Hare), (b) a learned neural renderer with policy gradients or DDPG through it (Learning to Paint; Qiu et al.), or (c) RL against a non-differentiable painter (SPIRAL, Signification Game with PPO). The emergent-communication literature consistently reports REINFORCE as slower/higher-variance than relaxed or quantised differentiable channels, and no paper was found that trains a pixel-level categorical drawing head for communication.

### Cited Findings
- SPIRAL (Ganin et al., ICML 2018): "an adversarially trained agent that generates a program which is executed by a graphics engine"; pen strokes for MNIST/Omniglot and brush strokes for CelebA via libmypaint; discriminator output as RL reward "optimizes pixel error between renderings and data better than optimizing pixel error directly"; no stroke supervision needed — [arXiv:1804.01118](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01118); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ganin18a.html)
- Learning to Paint (Huang, Heng & Zhou, ICCV 2019): "employing a neural renderer in model-based Deep Reinforcement Learning", agents "learn to determine the position and color of each stroke", "hundreds of strokes"; code at hzwer/ICCV2019-LearningToPaint — [arXiv:1903.04411](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04411)
- StrokeNet (Zheng et al., ICLR 2019): trains an agent against a learned differentiable approximation of the painting environment; "able to learn to write characters such as MNIST digits faster than reinforcement learning approaches"; motivation: non-differentiable environments "leading to slow convergence and massive computation" — [ICLR 2019 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2019/poster/1098); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2019/zheng2019iclr-strokenet)
- Differentiable Drawing and Sketching: rasterisation reformulated via the primitive's distance transform, relaxed to be learnable; argued to allow "a potentially more principled or flexible approach to modelling the drawing process or the loss" vs RL — [arXiv:2103.16194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16194); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.16194)
- Survey: "Stroke-based Rendering: From Heuristics to Deep Learning" (2022) groups methods into heuristics, stroke optimisation with differentiable vector graphics / neural rendering, and deep RL agents — [arXiv:2302.00595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00595v1)
- Qiu et al.: REINFORCE baseline "converges more slowly and is high-variance at gamma=0.95"; their eligibility-trace policy-gradient with gradients through the differentiable renderer was needed — [arXiv HTML 2111.14210v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Carmeli et al.: REINFORCE and Gumbel-Softmax "both ... result in poor performance compared to fully continuous communication"; quantised (straight-through style) messages train end-to-end with near-continuous performance; cites Havrylov & Titov finding Gumbel-Softmax converges faster than REINFORCE — [arXiv:2211.02412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02412); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02412)
- Lazaridou 2018 and Evtimova 2018 both trained discrete/binary messages with REINFORCE + entropy regularisation + learned baselines and reached >90% in-domain accuracy with few candidates — [ar5iv 1804.03984](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.03984); [ar5iv 1705.10369](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1705.10369)
- Signification Game: sender outputs a Gaussian over spline parameters, rendered (non-differentiably, with Gaussian noise) and trained with PPO; receivers CNN + head; both work, but behaviourist agents "learn slowly" — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)
- Mihai & Hare 2021: point-cloud rasteriser (10 points) communicates at 75% but drawings are not meaningful to humans; line rasteriser with perceptual loss gives human-readable sketches — [arXiv HTML 2106.02067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)

### Inferences
- **[speculation]** Pixel-level 16-way categorical heads over 256 cells are a 1024-bit discrete channel; REINFORCE over that is a 256-dimensional joint action with enormous variance; straight-through / Gumbel-Softmax per pixel, or a quantised continuous channel (Carmeli-style), is the only option in the literature that reliably trains at that size. Nothing in the surveyed work trains a pixel-categorical drawing head for communication, so this is untested.
- Stroke/stamp parameterisations (Mihai & Hare lines: 4 floats per line; Qiu: 5 continuous strokes per step) reduce the action to tens of continuous numbers, train through differentiable rasterisers, and come with an inherent "ink" prior that human data (KQ3/KQ5) and Qiu et al. show is the substrate on which simplification operates. At 16x16 with 16 colours an analytic distance-field rasteriser (Mihai & Hare style) is trivially cheap.
- **[speculation]** A VQ-VAE / learned-decoder latent message is the easiest to train (it is a continuous or quantised vector channel) but is the case Carmeli et al. identify as "trivial" and non-iconic when encoder is shared; it would need an explicit iconicity term (perceptual loss) to produce readable marks.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head published comparison of pixel-categorical vs stroke vs latent action spaces for emergent *communication* was found. StrokeNet's "faster than RL" claim is against a learned renderer, not against straight-through pixels.

## KQ7. Information capacity of a 16x16, 16-colour message; robustness to downsampling

### Takeaway
No published measurement of channel capacity for emergent image messages exists in the sources found; the nearest quantitative anchors are Evtimova's 32-bit binary messages (96.6% at 6 candidates, 45% OOD), Lazaridou's 10-symbol vocab x length-10 messages (~33 bits max, 98.5% at 5 candidates), Mihai & Hare's 20 lines (success at 100 candidates ~70%) and Carmeli's finding that binary alphabets with long words approach continuous performance. Nothing was found on robustness of learned image messages to being viewed downsampled (4x4 or 8x8 within a larger field).

### Cited Findings
- Evtimova et al.: d-dimensional binary messages; out-of-domain accuracy "correlated strongly with d"; 32-d messages up to 45% accuracy@7 OOD; in-domain accuracy insensitive to d — [ar5iv 1705.10369](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1705.10369)
- Lazaridou et al. 2018: effective alphabet 10/17/40 symbols for max length 2/5/10; train acc 92.0/98.2/98.5% with 5 candidates — [ar5iv 1804.03984](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.03984)
- Dessì et al.: a single symbol from 2048 (11 bits) suffices for 82% accuracy among 128 ImageNet candidates with augmentation — [arXiv HTML 2106.04258](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04258)
- Carmeli et al.: Gumbel-Softmax with alphabet 10, word length 10, 6 words (~20 bits by my calculation) collapses at 10,000 candidates (0.152) whereas quantised channels of alphabet 10 x length 100 x 6 words (~1,993 bits) reach 1.0; "with long enough words, quantized communication with binary alphabets performs almost as well as continuous communication" — [arXiv HTML 2211.02412](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02412)
- Mihai & Hare: agent success with 99 distractors is flat from 5 to 50 lines (69-73%); capacity bottleneck was the 64-d vs 1024-d latent, not the canvas — [arXiv HTML 2106.02067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Qiu et al.: 4-way choice solved in ~1 step (5 strokes) for seen referents, 2.4-3.8 steps for unseen — [arXiv HTML 2111.14210v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Noise: a 2025 IJCNLP/AACL study of symbolic referential games with erasure/deletion channels found protocols did not become more redundant under noise and certain noise levels pushed senders toward more compositional messages (symbolic messages only, not images) — [ACL Anthology 2025.ijcnlp-long.168](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.ijcnlp-long.168)
- Signification Game: "signification gaps" occur when referent stimuli are complex relative to the signalling medium (2-3 splines) — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)

### Inferences
- **[my calculation]** Raw channel: 256 cells x log2(16) = 1,024 bits per 16x16 mark. If a mark is seen at 1 px/tile inside a 16x16 view it occupies the *entire* view at full resolution; if instead it is rendered onto one tile it is seen as a single 4-bit pixel (16 distinguishable messages); at 4x4 tiles ~64 bits raw and 8x8 ~256 bits raw, before any decoding loss.
- **[my estimate from the anchors above]** Usable capacity with small from-scratch encoders and co-trained sender/receiver is tens of bits, not hundreds: 32-bit binary vectors already saturate in-domain referential accuracy (Evtimova), 11 bits suffice for 128-way ImageNet discrimination with a strong encoder (Dessì), and ~20 bits of Gumbel-Softmax messages collapse at very large candidate sets (Carmeli). For FLOCK, a repertoire of roughly 2^5-2^8 reliably distinguishable marks is a realistic target; the 1,024-bit canvas is massively over-provisioned for any plausible referent set (predator here, food there, direction, identity).
- **[speculation]** Iconic marks are *less* bit-efficient than codes (Fay 2008's argument: icons' complexity tracks the referent), so over-provisioning the canvas is a feature: there is slack for resemblance.

### Gaps
- No publication measured channel capacity (bits) of image-shaped emergent messages produced and decoded by small networks under noise or downsampling. No work on multi-scale perception of learned marks (seeing a mark at 4x4 or 8x8 inside a larger view) was found. These would have to be measured empirically in FLOCK.

## KQ8. Persistent vs transient messages: stigmergy / environment-mediated communication in MARL

### Takeaway
Stigmergic MARL (digital pheromones on a grid) works and scales to small teams, but every paper reports a bootstrapping problem: agents must act well to leave useful marks and must trust marks to act well, so early marks are noise and agents learn to ignore them; curricula that seed trustworthy marks are needed. No paper isolates whether persistence per se speeds learning relative to transient messages.

### Cited Findings
- ForMIC (Shaw, Wong & Sartoretti, 2020): 2D grid foraging; agents carrying food leave pheromone p = alpha^(t-h) with alpha = 0.97, cells take the max across agents, global decay beta = 0.99 per step; agents observe an 11x11x8 tensor with pheromone as one channel; A3C-style training with VGG-style conv + LSTM; ablations relative to the full model: no pheromones 82.8%, no pheromone curriculum 75.9%, no non-learning agents 72.6%; learning stigmergic policies is "highly nontrivial" due to the circular dependency; "early in training, agents often place pheromones haphazardly and learn to ignore them"; higher beta gives more persistent trails that signal distant food, and decay lets misleading trails fade — [arXiv HTML 2006.08152](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.08152); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08152)
- Stigmergic Independent RL (Xu, Li, Zhao & Zhang, 2019-2021): digital pheromone as an indirect communication bridge among independent learners, with a conflict-avoidance module and federated training; task: agents form a target shape; "extensive simulations demonstrate the effectiveness" (no figures in abstract) — [arXiv:1911.12504](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12504)
- Stigmergic Multi-Agent DRL with virtual pheromones (2025): DQN-based, coordinates up to 5 agents in deterministic and 8 in stochastic pellet-retrieval environments; authors note stigmergy in MARL "remains under-explored" — [arXiv:2510.03592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03592v1)
- Learned pheromone communication thesis (Mesa simulation, Q-learning): agents learn when to release a pheromone as a help request and when to respond — [unitesi.unimore](https://unitesi.unimore.it/handle/20.500.14251/3695)
- Human community data (KQ5): persistent exposure of many participants to the same signs produced globally shared conventions with more residual iconicity than isolated pairs — [PMC2607338](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607338/)

### Inferences
- **[speculation]** Persistence adds a credit-assignment delay (the reader may arrive many steps later) and a staleness problem (marks outlive their referent); ForMIC handles both with exponential decay and a curriculum of pre-placed trustworthy marks. For FLOCK, a decaying ground stamp plus an initial phase with hand-placed "ground truth" marks (e.g. a grass icon near grass) is the literature-backed way to bootstrap reading before writing.
- **[speculation]** Transient bubbles have simpler credit assignment (receiver reward within a few steps) and are the setting all the sketch papers use; a staged curriculum (bubbles first, stamps later, same drawing head) is consistent with everything found.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of persistent vs transient messages for learning speed or protocol quality was found in MARL.

## KQ9. Shared-encoder hypothesis: does reusing the perception encoder for message decoding bias toward iconicity?

### Takeaway
**There is no published direct test.** The closest evidence is indirect and mixed: (i) Mihai & Hare used the same frozen VGG16 for photos and sketches and still got non-iconic drawings until a perceptual loss was added; (ii) the Signification Game used one perception network for stimuli and signals and reports iconic beginnings that drift to abstraction, but without an ablation; (iii) Carmeli et al. warn that a shared encoder plus high-capacity channel makes communication trivially non-iconic; (iv) encoder *mismatch* hurts cross-agent communication.

### Cited Findings
- Mihai & Hare: receiver applies a frozen VGG16 to sketch and candidate photos and scores by dot product; game loss alone gives drawings "indecipherable to a human" (humans 8.3% vs chance 10%); adding a feature-space perceptual loss raises human accuracy to 38% — [arXiv HTML 2106.02067v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Qiu et al.: receiver scores sketches against photos with a pretrained VGG16 and the evolved conventions remain classifiable and revert to longer (more iconic) drawings on unseen classes — [arXiv HTML 2111.14210v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Signification Game Part I: "agents process environment observations and signals with the same perception network, and cannot distinguish between them"; iconic replicas arise under deception when the signal space overlaps stimuli; cooperative signals start iconic and drift to abstract — [arXiv HTML 2502.01568v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)
- Carmeli et al. Appendix A.2: if sender and receiver "use the same encoder" and the channel carries the encoded target, "the sender can simply send the encoded target to the receiver" and perfect, non-semantic performance follows — [arXiv HTML 2211.02412](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02412)
- Dessì et al.: sharing the CNN between sender and receiver ("+shared") raised accuracy slightly (92.8 vs 91.2 without aug; 82.2 vs 81.5 with aug) but without augmentation the shared model "succeeded" on Gaussian noise blobs at 84.7%, i.e. a degenerate low-level protocol — [arXiv HTML 2106.04258](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04258)
- Heterogeneous-encoder study: cross-agent metrics "decline as encoder mismatch increases" — [arXiv:2605.11695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11695)
- Mihai & Hare (visual semantics): a pretrained extractor biases the channel toward semantics; learned-from-scratch extractors give better game success and less semantic content — [ar5iv 2101.10253](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2101.10253)

### Inferences
- The literature supports a weaker version of the hypothesis: a shared encoder makes iconic marks *decodable for free* (the receiver already has features that respond to the referent), and encoder mismatch is harmful. It does *not* show that sharing makes iconicity *emerge*; where iconicity emerged it was injected (perceptual loss, pretrained sketch module, entropy-of-classifier reward in the Signification Game).
- **[speculation]** A testable FLOCK prediction follows: with shared encoder, a sender that stamps a *copy of its own view* (or of the referent's patch) should get above-chance decoding at step zero of training, which gives an iconic basin of attraction. Whether gradient descent stays in that basin or drifts to a cheaper code is exactly what Qiu/Hawkins (simplification keeps diagnostic features) vs Mihai & Hare game-loss-only (drifts to unreadable codes) disagree on; the difference between them is the presence of a resemblance signal in the objective (Qiu's pretrained sketch init, Hawkins' human perception).

### Gaps
- No ablation anywhere compares "shared encoder" vs "separate message encoder" on an iconicity metric. This is a genuine open experiment and FLOCK could contribute it.

## FLOCK 2.0 FIT ASSESSMENT (clearly labelled; this section is my own analysis built on the cited findings above)

### Takeaway
The hypothesis is plausible but under-determined by the literature: shared perception lowers the *cost of decoding* iconic marks, but every system that actually produced readable drawings also had an explicit resemblance signal in the objective. FLOCK should (1) treat the shared encoder as necessary but not sufficient and add a cheap resemblance term or iconic initialisation, (2) use a stroke/stamp action space rendered by a differentiable distance-field rasteriser rather than pixel-level categorical output, (3) budget for tens of bits of usable capacity and design the referent set accordingly, and (4) solve the scale problem by rendering marks onto a small tile footprint (e.g. 4x4 or 8x8 tiles) with a receiver that sees them at the same scale the sender's own view sees the world.

### Cited Findings (anchors reused from above)
- Shared frozen encoder without a resemblance loss gave human-unreadable drawings; adding feature-matching made them readable at ~no cost to agent success — [Mihai & Hare 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.02067v2)
- Stroke-count/step pressure plus co-adaptation produced simplification that keeps diagnostic parts; one-step drawing failed to communicate — [Qiu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.14210v4)
- Humans simplify but keep diagnostic features; conventions are partly partner-specific — [Hawkins et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.13861)
- Icons become symbols only with addressee feedback; communities keep more residual iconicity than isolated pairs — [Fay et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607338/)
- From-scratch CNN agents exploit low-level pixel statistics unless augmented — [Dessì et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.04258); [Lazaridou et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1804.03984)
- Continuous/quantised channels train far better than REINFORCE or Gumbel-Softmax; shared encoder + big channel is trivially solved non-iconically — [Carmeli et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.02412)
- Stigmergic marks need decay and a curriculum because early marks are noise — [ForMIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.08152)
- Same-network perception of signals and stimuli yields iconic starts drifting to abstract symbols under size/curvature penalties — [Signification Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01568v3)

### Inferences (FLOCK design answers)
**Hypothesis verdict.** *Supported in direction, unproven in mechanism.* Expect: (a) marks copied from the sender's own view will be decodable immediately through the shared encoder (iconic basin), (b) without a resemblance term, training pressure will move marks toward whatever the tiny encoder finds easiest (likely position/colour-blob codes, per Lazaridou/Dessì), (c) adding a small perceptual loss between the mark and the referent patch in the shared encoder's feature space (Mihai & Hare's recipe) or an "ink/stroke" cost with temporal discounting (Qiu's recipe) is what will keep marks readable. A clean ablation (shared encoder vs separate message encoder, with and without perceptual loss) would be a publishable contribution since none exists.

**Design question 1: mark scale.** A 16x16 stamp at 1 px/tile occupies the receiver's entire 16x16 view, so the receiver cannot see the mark *and* its context, and the sender's "draw what you see" iconic solution is a full-view copy. Options consistent with the literature: (i) render the 16x16 drawing into a 4x4- or 8x8-tile footprint (the receiver sees it downsampled, like a glyph on the ground), which also caps usable capacity at roughly 64-256 raw bits and matches the "tens of bits" regime that actually trains; (ii) keep bubbles at full 16x16 (transient, seen as a separate frame through the same encoder) and stamps at reduced footprint. Multi-scale legibility of learned marks is unstudied; it should be measured by training with a random-rescale augmentation on the message path (Dessì-style augmentation applied to the mark), which the literature shows is what prevents low-level-statistics hacks.

**Design question 2: action space.** Rank by what the literature shows trains: (1) **differentiable stroke/stamp rasteriser** (Mihai & Hare lines or a stamp library with learnable position/colour) — small continuous action, end-to-end gradients through to the receiver, free "ink" prior that supports simplification; at 16x16 the distance-field rasteriser is trivial to implement. (2) **Quantised latent decoder** (VQ/straight-through vector -> small decoder -> 16x16x16 logits) — trains easily but is the Carmeli "trivial channel" case and will not be iconic without a perceptual loss. (3) **Pixel-level categorical head with REINFORCE** — no precedent for communication; 256 joint discrete actions is a variance disaster; only viable with straight-through/Gumbel per pixel plus strong entropy regularisation. Recommend (1) for the installation, with the 16-colour palette applied as a learnable per-stroke colour index (softmax over 16) and a per-stroke "ink" cost.

**Design question 3: information capacity.** Raw capacity is 1,024 bits; realistic decodable capacity with small co-trained networks is on the order of 5-8 bits per mark (32-256 distinguishable messages), by analogy to Evtimova (32-bit binary vectors saturate), Dessì (11 bits for 128-way with a strong encoder), and Mihai & Hare (success flat across 5-50 lines). Design the referent set to be small (handful of concepts x handful of directions/identities) so that iconicity, not compression, is the binding constraint; the spare canvas capacity is what makes resemblance affordable. Expect downsampled 4x4 views to carry at most ~16 raw distinguishable messages after quantisation to 16 colours per cell; 8x8 is the smallest footprint where "a picture of a wolf" vs "a picture of grass" is plausibly distinguishable through a 1 px/tile encoder.

**Persistence.** Use decaying stamps (ForMIC-style exponential decay) and a curriculum: train bubbles (immediate feedback, Qiu/Mihai regime) before enabling stamps; seed early training with hand-authored iconic ground marks so readers learn to trust marks before writers learn to make them. Persistent community-visible marks are the setting human data predicts will keep the most residual iconicity.

### Gaps (what FLOCK would have to measure itself)
- Shared vs separate encoder ablation on an iconicity metric (none published).
- Capacity and legibility of learned 16x16 marks viewed at 4x4/8x8 (none published).
- Pixel-categorical drawing heads for communication (none published).
- Persistent vs transient message comparison for learning speed (none published).
