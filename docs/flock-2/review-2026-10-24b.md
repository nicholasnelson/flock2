## Summary

The method is now sound: there is a fixed evaluation with baselines, two seeds per change, and a
privileged upper bound that shows the meadow is learnable. Good. The session's conclusion, that
the fault is in learning from imagination, is plausible but not yet established. The logs point
to a more specific and cheaper-to-test cause first: **the critic cannot represent the value scale
this reward produces, so it is still drifting after 900 iterations and the advantages the actor
trains on are mostly critic error.** This is a port discrepancy from DreamerV3 (the critic lost
its symlog two-hot output) made fatal by a dense reward of about −1 every step. Two one-line
changes (centre the reward, or give the critic the same two-hot symlog head as the reward head)
test it. I recommend doing that and two cheap diagnostics (latent probes, the imagination gap)
before building PPO on the world-model state.

## 1. The critic is scale-limited and has not converged (the main finding)

**Evidence in the logs** (`agent_fix_s0_log.jsonl`, `agent_fix_s1_log.jsonl`, `agent_birth3_log.jsonl`):

| iteration (seed 0) | `ac_return` | `ac_critic_loss` | `ac_abs_adv` | `ac_ret_scale` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 303 (AC just started) | −10.6 | 69 | 10.0 | 14.6 |
| 603 | −163 | 28 | 6.1 | 15.2 |
| 900 | −246 | 48 | 7.1 | 29.7 |
| 900 (seed 1) | −276 | 30 | 5.6 | 22.8 |

The reward is about −1.08 per step, so with γ = 0.997 the true value of almost any state is about
−1.08 / (1 − 0.997) ≈ **−360**. A 32-step λ-return bootstraps 91 % of its value (0.997³²) from
the slow critic, so `ac_return` tracks the critic, and the critic is still on its way from 0 to
−360 after 600 iterations × 64 = 38,000 updates. It is not close to converged in any run.

**Why it is so slow.** `ac.Head` is a plain MLP with a zero-initialised linear output and an MSE
loss, trained by Adam at 3e-5. The hidden layer is LayerNorm + SiLU, so its activations are O(1);
to output −300 the final layer's 256 weights must sum to about −300, i.e. grow to O(1) each, and
Adam moves a weight by at most ~3e-5 per update. That is ~30,000+ updates just to reach the
right scale, which is what the logs show. The regulariser toward the slow critic
(`0.5 (v − slow)²`) slows it further by design.

**Why it matters.** The actor's signal is `rets − values`. A critic drifting by hundreds, with an
RMS error of 5–8 (√critic_loss), swamps what an action can change: a good bite at a deficit of
0.5 is worth ~0.012 per step (`drives.py`), so a 32-step action difference is worth a few tenths
of return at most. The actor is being trained on advantages that are 10–30× larger than any real
action effect and consist mostly of critic lag. That fits the behaviour: entropy falls from 2.2
to 0.7 as the policy commits to something (trot+right at iteration 603, drink at 900), and the
"something" changes from run to run and from seed to seed, which is what following noise looks
like. It also explains why birth3's `ret_scale` stayed small (~15) while birth2's exploded: the
scale depends on where the critic happens to be in its drift, not on the policy.

**DreamerV3 does not have this problem** because its critic, like its reward head, outputs a
255-bin two-hot distribution over symlog values (Hafner et al. 2023, §3 "Critic learning"):
symlog(−360) ≈ −5.9 is an ordinary target, and the output scale never has to be learned through
weight magnitudes. The port kept the two-hot symlog head for reward but gave the critic a scalar
MSE head. This is the one place the implementation departs from the paper in a way that matters
for this reward.

**Fixes, cheapest first; any one should do, and (a) is one line:**

- **(a) Centre the reward.** Subtract a running mean of the extrinsic reward before computing
  returns (Naik, Wan, Tomar & Sutton, "Reward Centering", 2024, arXiv:2405.09999). With γ < 1
  and no termination, subtracting a constant from every reward shifts all values by the same
  amount and leaves the optimal policy unchanged. Values collapse from −360 to around 0; the
  critic has nothing to drift toward. (Centre the *extrinsic* reward only; curiosity is already
  near zero-mean relative to its scale.)
- **(b) Two-hot symlog critic**, as DreamerV3: reuse `wm.twohot`/`BINS`, train with
  cross-entropy, read the value as symexp of the expected bin. This is the faithful port.
- **(c) Lower γ** to 0.99 or 0.98. Values become −100 or −50, still large. Helps SNR but only
  partially; do (a) or (b) regardless.

After the fix, check `ac_return` has settled within ~50 iterations of AC start and that
`ac_critic_loss` is well below `ac_abs_adv`². Only then do the seed-0/seed-1 numbers mean
anything about imagination.

## 2. The bandit test does not exercise the part that is broken

`test_the_actor_critic_learns_a_bandit` has a per-step reward of 0 or 1 with no delayed
consequences: the critic's job there is trivial and the returns are O(10), not O(300). It proves
REINFORCE is wired up; it says nothing about the critic, λ-returns or bootstrapping at this
scale. Add a second toy: the same bandit but with a constant **−1 added to every reward** (so
values are ≈ −333) and a two-step dependency (the rewarded arm only pays if a different arm was
chosen the step before). The current code should fail or learn far slower on it; the fixed code
should pass. That test is the regression guard for § 1.

## 3. Two diagnostics that are cheaper than the proposed next rung, and more decisive

The session proposes PPO on the world-model state next. I would do it, but after two things that
take minutes and separate "representation" from "imagination" more cleanly:

- **Linear probes of the model state.** Fit a logistic/linear probe from `feat_of(h, z)` (the
  posterior state on real data) to the privileged features in `flock/meadow/privileged.py`:
  near-water flag, grass ≥ 1 underfoot, night flag, egocentric direction and distance to water
  and cover. If `near_water` cannot be read from the state, no policy on that state (imagined or
  PPO) can learn to drink in the right place, and the fix is in the world model (the pixel loss
  is dominated by sky and uniform ground; `wm_pixel_acc` 0.96 says little about a one-tile
  water edge). If it can, the representation is cleared and PPO-on-state is a fair test. Save
  the probe accuracies alongside the eval JSON; they are the right per-run health check for M1
  onward.
- **The imagination gap.** From the same replay start states, roll the learned actor 32 steps in
  the *real* simulator and in imagination, and compare the mean return (and the mean predicted
  vs. real reward of the drink action specifically). If imagined return ≫ real, the actor is
  exploiting model error, which is the session's hypothesis stated as a measurement. If they
  agree and the policy is still bad, the problem is the optimiser (§ 1). This is standard
  model-based RL practice (model-error compounding; Janner et al. 2019, arXiv:1906.08253).

Then **PPO on the world-model state** (the session's option 1) is the right next rung, with two
cautions: the policy input is 896 numbers of which most are categorical samples, so use the
posterior *mean* of z rather than a sample for the policy input, and give it the same 300-update
budget as the privileged PPO before judging it. Option 2 (PPO from pixels) is a worse test
because it changes two things at once (no model, new encoder).

## 4. The upper bound is a lower bound on the upper bound

- PPO's final entropy is 1.99 of a maximum 2.20 nats: its action mix is still nearly uniform
  (rest 19 %, left/right 15 % each). `pg` ≈ −2e-4 and `approx_kl` ≈ 1e-4 say the policy has
  stopped moving under entropy coefficient 0.01, not that it has converged to a good policy. A
  greedy (argmax) evaluation of the same checkpoint, and a run with the entropy coefficient
  annealed to 0.001, would likely raise the bound considerably. Worth 10 minutes: it tells us
  how much headroom there is above the scripted flock.
- "PPO beats the scripted flock": −0.39 vs −0.41 per step is a 5 % difference on 8 meadows with
  no error bars. The depletion figures (9 % vs 20 %) are more convincing. Please add a
  per-meadow standard error to `m1_eval.py` output (8 meadows → 8 means → SE); otherwise
  differences of a few hundredths will be over-read later.
- PPO's warmth is 0.34 at dawn 3 and it rests near cover 0.5 % of the time despite being handed
  the cover vector. Night shelter is a long-horizon plan (walk up to 18 tiles, then rest) and
  the reward for it is deferred and small per step. This is the first concrete sign that
  warmth, as tuned, is nearly unlearnable even with perfect information; expect the same for
  the sheep. Consider raising the night warmth loss in the open, or lowering `COVER_DIST`'s
  effect gap, so that shelter is worth planning for.
