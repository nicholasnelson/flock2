[`review-2026-10-29-staged.md`](review-2026-10-29-staged.md). Result files are in

**Paused here, waiting on the owner's decision (§ 5).** Nothing is running.

## 1. In one paragraph

Meadow v2 works as a world. The scripted flock and PPO on privileged numbers survive it with no
deaths. Sheep that learn from their own eyes (the vision hybrid: a DreamerV3-style world model plus PPO
on its state) learn to graze and partly to drink. But 43–79 % of them die of thirst, mostly on day 2.
Their eyes read water's direction only within about 8 tiles, and **no arm has remembered water once it
leaves view**. That includes arm R, where a memory rotated by the sheep's own turns was built in. The
latest review argues that **this world does not demand that kind of memory**: a memory-free policy with
full perception solves it, and the deaths are "lost the pond a day ago" deaths that a 30-step memory
could not prevent. The owner proposes rebuilding in stages: a simple brain in an easy world first, then
adding challenges to the world and network modules to meet them ("forced evolution"). The review
supports this, with guards.

## 3. What we learned

**Established by our own measurements:**
- **The eyes see water near, not far.** The encoder's embedding reads water bearing at R² 0.63–0.74
  within 4 tiles, 0.29–0.35 at 4–8, and below 0 beyond 8. The decoder redraws about 80 % of water
  pixels within 4 tiles by day, about 40 % at 4–8, and none beyond 8.
- **The world model's state carries less than the eyes** (bearing 0.2–0.37 against 0.65): branch B of
  the diagnosis gate, "the state is the limit".
- **Night water was a world problem, now fixed.** With water reflecting the sky, night water within
  4 tiles is redrawn 73 % of the time, against 3–18 % before.
- **Multi-step prediction helps the state a little** (bearing +0.15 over N0). KL weight, replay length,
  longer horizons and longer sequences did not produce memory.
- **The rung-3 spatial model failed** at this budget (its decoder redraws almost no water).
- **The D3 memory probe is valid.** An ideal memory (the last-seen bearing turned by the heading change
  since) scores 1.00, and noise as wide as the model's state scores about −0.25. Every model's state
  scores at or below that noise floor.
- **Turns alone explain where water went after it leaves view:** within 30 steps, the last-seen
  bearing corrected for turns gives the current bearing almost exactly.
- **The built-in rotated ring (arm R) was used for the present, not the past.** It holds the bearing of
  visible water (0.41) but nothing after water leaves view.
- **Survival improved with reading the eyes directly** (embed arm: 43 % died against 76 %), the
  largest single gain so far.
- **The sheep do not huddle** (1–5 % of night rest), and they die on day 2.

**Judgement:**
- The ring's write rewrote every bin from the current view each step; nothing tied a bin to a
  direction, so there was no reason to keep the bins behind the sheep. Fixing that would mean
  building in more geometry. Retire the ring.
- Five arms aimed at a capacity (a 30-step bearing memory) that the world was never shown to pay for.
  Pre-registration stopped us moving the goalposts, but not from aiming at the wrong goal.

## 4. The latest review (Fable 5.1, 2026-10-29)

[`review-2026-10-29-staged.md`](review-2026-10-29-staged.md), 35 references, each link opened.

**Does the meadow demand memory? Mostly no, with a twist** *(the review's judgement, on our numbers)*:
- A memory-free policy with full perception (privileged PPO) never dies, so the meadow has a
  memory-free solution.
- The vision sheep can only use water within about 8 tiles, in a 48 × 48 meadow where they must re-find
  a pond every 1.6 days (a full hydration level lasts 3,750 steps).
- The deaths come on day 2, after the sheep drank once and grazed out of sight. A 30-step memory would
  not save them. What would: seeing water from far, a search that covers ground, staying within sight
  of water, or remembering a place for hours.
- Memory did not appear because neither the reward nor the reconstruction loss pays for it.

**The staged "forced evolution" approach is sound and is the field's own direction** (POET, PAIRED,
ACCEL; hide-and-seek; blind navigation agents that build maps when the task demands it), with three
guards:
- a **demand check** at each stage: the previous stage's brain must fail on the new world, or the
  pressure isn't one;
- a **route-around list** written with each criterion: cheap strategies that satisfy the pressure
  without the capacity, each with a measurement;
- **every earlier evaluation kept as a regression test**, against forgetting and loss of plasticity.

It also notes that "start small" is a bet, not a law: Elman's result was later reversed on more natural
tasks.

**Hand-design the module slots; search only small things.** Evolving the meadow brain is out of reach on
one GPU (about 27 minutes of rendering per generation). Evolution fits the grid world, a dozen discrete
design choices, or the world's own parameters. Add modules progressive-network style: keep the old
network, add the new beside it, freeze the old until the regression tests hold.

**Other points:**
- The "thirsty sheep sees water" and information-gap measures use a 16-tile range; the eyes work to
  about 8, so recompute both at 8.
- Report the per-day death curve and time to first drink (free).
- Letting the reward train the encoder is as honest as letting pixel reconstruction do it; record the
  choice.
- Warmth cannot kill, so huddling will never be forced.
- Write the bar for "interesting and meaningful" communication into stage 3's criterion now: causal,
  referential on new worlds, beats the "I'm here" beacon and plain following, works with the sender
  out of view.

## 5. The proposed plan from here (not yet adopted)

Each stage: pre-registered criteria, three seeds for claims (one seed to screen), a demand check, a
route-around list, and every earlier stage's evaluation re-run.

| Stage | World change (a fact of the world) | Brain | Pass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0. Does the world need memory?** (half a day; no learning sheep) | None. PPO on privileged numbers with water hidden beyond 4, 8 or 16 tiles or behind the sheep; at 8 tiles, add an ideal 30-step memory, then a 1,000-step one | — | Decides stage 2's pressure: if the 8-tile sheep survive (≤ 20 % die), memory isn't needed at today's eyesight; if the 30-step memory rescues them, it was; if only the 1,000-step one does, the need is place memory |
| Stage 0, free extra | — | — | From existing evaluation runs: distance to water and time since water was last in view at each death; per-day deaths |
| **1. The reactive sheep** | Willows on each pond's bank, so water can be seen from far | Vision encoder + PPO, no world model, no recurrence. Arms: willows vs none; reactive vs the current best hybrid | ≤ 20 % die, reward ≥ 2.0, three seeds. If it fails, the problem is perception from pixels, and the encoder is the next change |
| **2a. First memory pressure** | **Mud (owner's design, spec § 21): no grass or regrowth within a mud radius ramped 0 → 8+ tiles, trampling for paths**, so sheep must leave sight of water to eat | Add a GRU beside the stage-1 network | ≤ 20 % die and D3 ≥ 0.3 (wide-noise control below 0.1), stage 1 still passes; demand check: the stage-1 brain loses ≥ 40 % |
| **2b. First signal** (in parallel) | A scripted fox at night; an alarm channel heard within earshot | Track B's signal channel, run in the grid first | Hearing changes listeners' movement and survival (swap test); beats a scripted "fox!" beacon |
| 3. Calling about water | **World v3 (spec § 22): a hex lattice and terrain machinery, built together here (height may start flat).** Then a pond dries at random, hills or night hide the willows, so some sheep know what others don't | The 2b channel, now carrying what 2a's memory holds | Track B's criteria, and the bar in § 4 |
| 4. Marks | Information that outlives the caller (a dry pond's schedule, a rich patch) | M0's drawing medium | A sheep that never met the marker changes route and outcome because of the mark |
| 5. Generations | Lambs inherit weights and learn by watching | — | Later |

**Main risks:** sheep find a way around a pressure (stay near water, follow the fence, follow others);
forgetting as stages stack; stage 1 failing (then perception, not memory, is the problem); an early
crude strategy blocking a better one.

**Reused:** meadow v2 and its scripted checks; `m2_eval.py` and `m2_diag.py` (the validated D3 probe);
`m2_ppo.py`; the `m2_hybrid.py` loop with the world model made optional; Track B for the channel. The
spatial model and the ring are shelved.

**Coordinator's recommendation:** run stage 0 first. It is cheap and decides whether stage 2a's
pressure is the right one before anything is built.
