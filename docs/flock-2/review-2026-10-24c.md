# Review 3: the grid learner, the critic and the meadow's spatial blind spot (2026-10-24, late)

*Third outside review. It is based on reading the code, docs, commit messages and result files
(`docs/flock-2/m1-results/`, `docs/flock-2/grid-results/`, `runs/grid/`). I ran no training or
evaluation; the only computation was small arithmetic on logged numbers. Labels: **Established**
means a published result or a number read straight from a file in this repository.
**Judgement** means my own interpretation. I checked that every link resolves to the work named
(arXiv API, Crossref or the official page) on 2026-10-24.*

## Summary

1. **The grid diagnosis ("the model is fine, the optimiser is at fault") is half right.** The
   optimiser is clearly failing. But the imagination-gap test cannot show that the model is fine
   for *choosing actions*. The 0.95 and 0.92 correlations pool over worlds whose drive levels
   differ, so they mostly measure how well the model tracks state value. Inside a single state,
   the model's errors between actions are about as large as the true differences between actions
   (§1.1).
2. **The critic is still the weak link, now in a new form.** With reward centring at γ = 0.997,
   any error in the running mean r̄ is multiplied by 1/(1 − γ) = 333. The logs show centred
   returns averaging +10 to +16 in the slow runs and +73 to +87 in the "fast" runs. If centring
   were working they would sit near 0. The faster sweep collapses sooner (entropy 0.39 by
   iteration 75), as you would expect if more updates follow a biased critic faster (§1.2).
3. **The grid is easier than the meadow but not "perfect perception".** The 8 × 8 window covers
   about a third of the 14 × 14 interior, so the pond is often out of view and must be
   remembered. The scripted baseline (−0.10) knows the whole map, so it is not a reachable
   target for a learner that can only see its window (§1.4).
4. **The cheapest decisive experiment has not been run:** `ac.py` trained on the *true* grid
   simulator, at γ = 0.997 vs 0.98, with three seeds. The grid is a pure JAX function, so this
   rung is now a few hours of work. It separates "actor-critic" from "world model" completely
   (§3, S1).
5. **The meadow's missing direction-to-water** is the problem that matters most for the project,
   because "where the water is" is exactly what sheep would later communicate. Before changing
   the architecture, check whether the decoder draws water in the right place (§3, S5).

## 1. Assessment

### 1.1 The grid imagination-gap test does not test action-conditional accuracy (judgement, from `scripts/grid_gap.py` and `runs/grid/eval/gap_plain_s0.json`)

`one_step_corr` is `corrcoef(pred1.ravel(), real1.ravel())` over 7 actions × 64 worlds. Reward is
−D, which depends mostly on the world's current levels, so most of the variance being correlated
is *between worlds*. The per-action means tell a different story:

| action | predicted | real |
| --- | --- | --- |
| north / west | −0.566 | −0.523 |
| south | **−0.591** | −0.523 |
| eat | −0.587 | −0.562 |
| drink | −0.581 | −0.553 |

In reality all moves are identical. The model rates "south" 0.025 worse than "north", which is
the same size as the true penalty for eating or drinking in the wrong place (0.03–0.04). The
32-step comparison (−20.96 vs −20.76, r = 0.92) follows one policy only, and that policy had
collapsed (60 % west). It therefore says nothing about counterfactual actions, and its start
states sit mostly against the west wall. An actor in imagination learns from *differences
between actions in the same state*, so that is what needs measuring:

- the correlation *within each state*, across the 7 actions, of predicted vs real n-step return
  (with n = 1, 8 and 32, using a fixed "do a, then follow a random policy" rollout);
- the sign of the predicted advantage in the states that decide the task: beside water (drink
  vs anything else) and on grass ≥ 1 while hungry (eat vs anything else).

Until this is done, "the fault is the optimiser, not the model" should read "the model tracks
state value well; whether it ranks actions correctly is untested."

### 1.2 The critic: centring at γ = 0.997 leaves an amplifier (established numbers, judged mechanism)

**Established (logs):**

- In `plain_s0`, `ac_return` swings from −3.1 to +11.4 and r̄ moves from −0.87 to −1.14 and
  back to −0.98.
- `ac_critic_loss` rises from 0.016 at the start of AC training to about 8–10 and never settles.
- In `sweep_fast` and `sweep_ret_fast` (64 AC updates per iteration, lr 1e-4), `ac_return` is
  68–87 at iterations 175–250, and entropy is about 0.4.

**Judgement.** With centred rewards, the mean differential value across visited states should
be close to zero. A mean of +80 means the values carry a common offset that the critic is
chasing. A small mismatch between r̄ (an EMA over imagined 32-step windows from replay starts)
and the average reward the critic implicitly extrapolates beyond the window produces exactly
this: an error ε in r̄ becomes a value offset of ε/(1 − γ) = 333ε, so ε = 0.25 is enough for +80.
Every change of policy also moves r̄, and each change of 0.1 shifts every true value by 33.
Meanwhile 91 % of each 32-step λ-return is bootstrapped (0.997³² ≈ 0.91), so the actor's
advantages are mostly the critic's lag.

Naik et al. say simple centring is straightforward on-policy and propose a "slightly more
sophisticated method for the off-policy setting"
([Reward Centering, arXiv:2405.09999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09999); the off-policy variant
estimates r̄ from TD errors, as in differential TD,
[Wan, Naik & Sutton, arXiv:2006.16318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16318)). Imagination from
replay start states under a changing policy is closer to the off-policy case than the on-policy
one (judgement).

Centring was the right fix for review 2's problem, the critic drifting toward −333. But at this γ
it leaves an amplifier in place. **The grid does not need γ = 0.997** (judgement): water falls
0.006 per step and a sip gives 0.1 (one sip every ~17 steps); food falls 0.004 and a bite gives
0.08 (one every ~20 steps); any tile is at most ~25 moves away. An effective horizon of 50
(γ = 0.98) covers the whole plan, cuts the amplifier from 333 to 50, and cuts the bootstrapped
share of a 32-step return from 0.91 to 0.52.

### 1.3 Departures from current DreamerV3 that matter here (established from the official code, judged relevance)

The official `configs.yaml` and `agent.py` at
[github.com/danijar/dreamerv3](https://github.com/danijar/dreamerv3) (the Nature version is
[Hafner et al. 2025, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08744-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08744-2);
preprint [arXiv:2301.04104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)) set, among other things:

- `imag_length: 15` and `horizon: 333` (that is, γ = 0.997);
- `train_ratio: 32`;
- `opt: lr 4e-5, agc 0.3`;
- `imag_loss: slowtar: False, actent: 3e-4, slowreg: 1.0`;
- `repval_loss: True` with loss scale `repval: 0.3`.

Against `flock/ac.py`:

- **No replay value loss.** DreamerV3 also trains the critic on λ-returns computed from *real
  replayed rewards* (bootstrapped from the imagination return). This is the one component that
  anchors the critic to real reward rather than to the reward head and r̄. Adding it is the most
  faithful single fix for §1.2 (judgement).
- **Returns are bootstrapped from the slow critic, but the baseline uses the fast critic.** The
  current default config sets `slowtar: False`, so both come from the same critic. In `ac.py` the
  fast/slow gap enters every advantage as extra noise. It is small, but free to remove.
- **Train ratio.** In the grid, one iteration collects 4,096 environment steps and replays
  16 × 16 × 32 = 8,192 frames: a ratio of 2, against DreamerV3's 32. In total that is ~5,600
  actor-critic updates at lr 3e-5 over the whole run. `tests/test_ac.py` itself shows the actor
  needs ~300 updates at this rate to move a trivial bandit from 0.11 to 0.42. The fast sweep
  raises updates and learning rate *together with* the biased critic, so its failure does not
  show that more updates are useless (judgement).
- **The entropy bonus is tiny relative to noisy normalised advantages.** With S ≈ 12 and
  |advantage| ≈ 3, the normalised signal is ~0.25 and mostly critic error. An entropy weight of
  3e-4 cannot stop collapse onto noise. `sweep_ent` (3e-3) drinking better fits this, but it is
  one seed (§1.5).

### 1.4 The grid's reward and observability (established from `flock/grid/world.py`; judgement on consequences)

- **Partial observability.** The view is 8 × 8 with the sheep at (4, 4), so it sees 4 cells up and
  left and 3 down and right. The pond is 2–3 × 2–3 tiles in a 14 × 14 interior and is often out
  of view, and worlds restart every 400 steps. A learner must explore and then remember where the
  pond is. `flock/grid/scripted.py` uses the whole map (`_targets` reads `s.water` everywhere). So
  the gap between −0.10 and −1.12 includes the cost of searching, which no non-privileged policy
  can avoid. This does not explain −1.19 (worse than random), but it means "scripted" is not the
  target.
- **With n = 4, a sheep with one need empty can barely see the other need.** At food 0.1 and
  water 0.5, ∂D/∂(water deficit) = 0.30 and ∂D/∂(food deficit) = 1.72. At food 0, a sip is worth
  ~0.024 per step and a bite ~0.155. This is the intended "follow the worst deficit". But in
  training, the logged water level sits at 0.10–0.15 for the whole run, so most start states are
  thirsty *and* hungry, and the actor is learning in the region where the weaker need's signal is
  smallest. A one-run diagnostic with n = m = 2 (separable D = Σd², which breaks Keramati &
  Gutkin's n > m but only as a test) would show whether this coupling contributes to the
  one-need specialisations.
- **The floor at 0 is a plateau.** Levels clip at 0 and nothing dies, so once a need is empty,
  neglecting it costs nothing more, and a single bite or sip helps only for ~20 steps before the
  level is back at 0. The drives docstring argues that drive reduction "is flat once levels
  bottom out". −D is also flat in the neglected need at the floor (judgement). This is fine for
  the installation but makes recovery from the bottom a hard credit-assignment problem.
- **Reward alignment is correct** (checked): `wm.loss_fn` trains state t+1 on the reward of
  step t, and `ac.imagine` reads `world.reward(feats[1:])`.

### 1.5 Single-seed sweep readings are probably noise (judgement)

`plain_s0`'s own training log collapses in turn onto west (iteration 102), north (152–177),
south (227) and west again (400), with entropy swinging between 0.5 and 1.5. A learner that
specialises differently from moment to moment *within one run* will specialise differently
*between* runs. "Higher entropy learns drinking but neglects food; return-spread scaling eats
but does not drink" are each one seed, and both fit "follows noise toward whichever need it
stumbles on". I would not draw knob conclusions from them.

### 1.6 What is sound

- The sanity ladder worked as intended. Privileged PPO beat the scripted flock. The hybrid (PPO
  on the latent) beat random (−0.79/−0.75 vs −1.02). The probes predicted the hybrid's exact
  failure (no water), and it happened. That is good science.
- Building a tiny grid world to isolate the learner was the right move.
- Evaluations now have fixed seeds, standard errors and greedy variants.
- `wm.Config` lets one implementation serve both worlds, so grid findings carry over.

### 1.7 Overlooked items

- `CURRENT-DESIGN.md` is already stale. It does not mention reward centring, the optional
  two-hot critic, the probes, the hybrid result or the grid world. The `m1-meadow-README.md`
  narrative stops at "After the review", and the results since then exist only in commit
  messages. The design page and decision log were meant to prevent exactly this.
- Grid runs are slow for their size. `plain_s0` took 1,293 s for 400 iterations. Infancy
  iterations take ~1 s; actor-critic iterations take several seconds for 16 updates on a 0.5 M
  model. I did not profile this. A likely cause is per-call Python overhead in `nnx.jit` and the
  host-side replay loop. Scanning the 16 updates inside one jitted function (`nnx.split`/`merge`)
  could make a seed take minutes, which is what makes three seeds affordable.
- Four grid runs now share the GPU, against HANDOFF's "one GPU job at a time" (a CUDA crash in
  M0). Worth noting if a run dies.

## 2. Relevant research (2023–2026 focus)

**World-model agents.**
- **DreamerV3** (Nature 2025, links above) remains the reference for discrete-action
  imagination. Its robustness depends on the full package: two-hot critic, return
  normalisation, replay value loss, AGC and a train ratio of 32+.
- **TD-MPC2** ([arXiv:2310.16828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)) learns a decoder-free latent
  model for continuous control and plans with it.
- **STORM** ([arXiv:2310.09615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09615)), **IRIS**
  ([arXiv:2209.00588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00588)), **Δ-IRIS**
  ([arXiv:2406.19320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19320)) and **DIAMOND**
  ([arXiv:2405.12399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12399)) replace the RSSM with transformers or
  diffusion. They mainly buy sample efficiency or visual fidelity on Atari.
- **Judgement:** none of these addresses this project's bottleneck, a critic on a dense,
  continuing, negative reward. Switching architecture now would add variables without removing
  the failing one. Treat them as a dead end for M1.
- **R2I** ([arXiv:2403.04253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04253)) replaces the GRU with state-space
  models to improve long-term memory in world models. It is relevant if the grid's
  remember-the-pond demand or the meadow's out-of-view water turns out to be the limit.

**Rebalancing what the latent encodes.**
- **HarmonyDream** ([arXiv:2310.00344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00344)) reports that the
  observation loss dominates world-model training and rebalances it against reward modelling.
  This is directly relevant to water being a few pixels in a 256 × 64 strip.
- **MuDreamer** ([arXiv:2405.15083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15083)) learns without
  reconstruction by predicting value and actions.
- **Masked World Models** ([arXiv:2206.14244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14244)) separate visual
  representation learning from dynamics learning.

**Spatial and egocentric memory.**
- **Neural Map** ([arXiv:1702.08360](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08360)) and **EgoMap**
  ([arXiv:2002.02286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02286)) give agents structured spatial memory.
  EgoMap projects first-person features into a map that moves with the agent and shows gains in
  3D partially observed tasks.
- **Judgement:** the panorama is already a ring of directions. An azimuth-indexed state, where
  turning is a circular shift, is the cheapest version of this idea.

**Reward centring and average reward.** Naik et al. 2024 and Wan et al. 2021 (above). The
abstract of the former says the benefit grows as γ → 1, and that standard methods do much worse
when rewards are shifted by a constant.

**Homeostatic RL in deep agents.**
- Yoshida, Daikoku, Nagai & Kuniyoshi, "Emergence of integrated behaviors through direct
  optimization for homeostasis", *Neural Networks* 2024
  ([doi:10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106379](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106379)). I verified
  the title and venue only, not the content.
- Yoshida, Sprekeler & Gutkin, "Linking Homeostasis to Reinforcement Learning" (2025
  perspective, [arXiv:2507.04998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04998)).
- Horibe & Yoshida, "Emergence of Implicit World Models from Mortal Agents"
  ([arXiv:2411.12304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12304)), which adds death to homeostatic
  meta-RL.
- Yoshida & Man, "Empathic Coupling of Homeostatic States for Intrinsic Prosociality"
  ([arXiv:2412.12103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12103)), relevant to the social drive and to
  M5.
- **Judgement:** this line is the closest published relative of FLOCK's design and is worth a
  focused read for their reward form and discounting choices. I have not verified their
  hyperparameters.

**Exploration robust to noise.**
- Ensemble disagreement: [Pathak et al., arXiv:1906.04161](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04161);
  Plan2Explore, [arXiv:2005.05960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05960).
- **BYOL-Explore** ([arXiv:2206.08332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08332)).
- **Curiosity in Hindsight** ([arXiv:2211.10515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10515)), which
  explicitly targets stochastic environments.
- Learning progress: [Kim et al. 2020, arXiv:2007.07853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07853), and
  in a 2025 LLM-agent form, MAGELLAN
  ([arXiv:2502.07709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07709)).
- **Judgement:** keep curiosity off until the grid learner works without it.

**Replay ratio and plasticity.**
- **Primacy bias** ([arXiv:2205.07802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07802)) and **BBF**
  ([arXiv:2305.19452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19452)) show that high update ratios need
  periodic resets.
- **Loss of plasticity** (Dohare et al., *Nature* 2024,
  [doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7)) shows that
  standard deep learning gradually stops learning in continual settings, and proposes
  continual backpropagation (re-initialising little-used units). M7 already plans remedies of
  this kind.

**Emergent communication with world models, partial observability.**
- Surveys: [Lazaridou & Baroni, arXiv:2006.02419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02419);
  [Boldt & Mortensen 2024, arXiv:2407.03302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03302).
- Nomura, Aoki & Taniguchi, "Decentralized Collective World Model for Emergent Communication and
  Coordination" ([arXiv:2504.03353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03353)).
- Hill et al., "Communicating Plans, Not Percepts"
  ([arXiv:2508.02912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02912)). Per its abstract, messages learned end
  to end worked in simple grid settings, while sharing world-model plan summaries scaled better
  as complexity grew.
- The Metropolis–Hastings naming game
  ([arXiv:2205.12392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12392)).

**Animal-likeness over long runs.** Behaviour can be split into reusable sub-second "syllables"
with measurable transition statistics: MoSeq
([Wiltschko et al., *Neuron* 2015, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2015.11.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.11.031))
and Keypoint-MoSeq
([*Nature Methods* 2024, doi:10.1038/s41592-024-02318-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02318-2)).
**Judgement:** bout-length and transition statistics of this kind are a measurable stand-in for
"looks like an animal". The current agents fail them badly (single-action collapse), and even
privileged PPO is nearly uniform per step (entropy 1.99 of 2.20). On open-endedness as a goal for
long-running systems, see [Hughes et al., arXiv:2406.04268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04268).

## 3. Prioritised suggestions

Costs are GPU wall-clock time with one job running, plus coding time. "Worked" criteria should
be written down *before* each run.

**S1. Grid: train `ac.py` on the true simulator (highest priority).** Replace `world.transition`
and `prior` in the imagination rollout with `Wd.step` on cloned true states. Give actor and
critic fully observed features: a one-hot of the whole map, position and levels. Rewards are
real and there is no reward head. Grid: γ ∈ {0.997, 0.98} × 3 seeds, with no other change.
- *Cost:* half a day of coding plus ~1–2 h of runs (less after S7).
- *Shows:* whether the actor-critic recipe can learn this task at all, and whether γ is the
  culprit.
- *Worked if:* at γ = 0.98 every seed beats random on `grid_eval` by more than 5 SE and keeps
  both levels above 0.3; mean centred value at start states stays within ±5.
- If it fails at both γ, compare against PPO on the same features (`m1_ppo.py`'s pattern): if
  PPO succeeds, the fault is in `ac.py`'s update rule.

**S2. Grid: fix the critic in the learned-model agent.**
- Add DreamerV3's replay value loss (λ-returns on the real replayed rewards, weight 0.3).
- Use the same critic for the bootstrap targets and the baseline.
- Run at the γ that S1 picks.
- Log two health checks: the mean centred value (should be ≈ 0), and the correlation between
  predicted value and the real discounted, centred return on held-out real sequences.
- *Cost:* half a day plus 3 seeds.
- *Worked if:* the value correlation exceeds 0.7 and the return mean stays bounded; evaluation
  as in S1. Only after this, re-try the train ratio: 64 AC updates with the actor at 3e-5 and
  the critic at 1e-4.

**S3. Grid: an action-conditional gap test (§1.1).** Measure within-state correlation across
actions, and the sign of the predicted advantage in the decisive states.
- *Cost:* 1–2 h, a light evaluation-only job.
- *Worked if:* drink is ranked first beside water and eat first on grass when hungry in more
  than 80 % of those states. If not, the world model does need work (reward-head weight;
  HarmonyDream-style rebalancing).

**S4. Grid: separate memory from learning.** Add a `--view 16` variant (the whole map visible)
and a probe of the pond's position from the latent when the pond is out of view.
- *Cost:* an hour plus runs.
- *Shows:* whether search and memory, rather than optimisation, cap the score. It also gives the
  learner a fair target: PPO with an LSTM on the 8 × 8 view.

**S5. Meadow: find where the direction to water is lost.**
- First, a diagnostic: decode the posterior state and measure, per column, whether water pixels
  are reconstructed in the right azimuth (IoU of water columns, for frames with water in view).
  If the decoder places water correctly, the information is in the state but hard to read, and a
  probe or policy needs more capacity. If it does not, the loss is ignoring it.
- In the second case, try in order:
  - class-balanced reconstruction (weight water and grass ≥ 1 pixels by inverse frequency,
    capped);
  - an azimuth-structured state: keep the encoder's 8 bottom columns as 8 sector embeddings, and
    make turning a circular shift of the sector features in the prior.
- *Cost:* the diagnostic is ~2 h of CPU-light evaluation; each fix is about a day plus a
  world-model-only run.
- *Worked if:* the MLP probe's R² for water-in-view direction (now −0.9 forward / −0.2 left)
  rises above 0.5, and then the hybrid's dawn water level rises from 0.04 toward PPO's 0.80.
- Keep privileged labels out of the training loss (the owner's principle); use them only in
  probes.

**S6. Methodology.**
- (a) No more single-seed knob sweeps. Three seeds or none, and one change per comparison.
- (b) Pre-register each run's success criterion in the README before launching it.
- (c) Bring `CURRENT-DESIGN.md` and the M1 README up to date with centring, probes, hybrid and
  grid, and record in the decision log "grid world adopted as test bed".
- (d) Report advantage-sign tests and value health checks with every agent evaluation, as the
  probes now are.

**S7. Engineering: make a grid seed take minutes.** Profile `grid_agent.py`, move the update
loops into one jitted scan, and run one GPU job at a time.
- *Cost:* half a day.
- *Pays for:* S1–S4.

**Dead ends for now (judgement):**
- more entropy, normalisation or learning-rate sweeps at γ = 0.997 before the critic is fixed;
- transformer or diffusion world models;
- reconstruction-free world models (they remove the decoder, which is S5's main diagnostic);
- any curiosity variant in the meadow before the grid learner reaches at least half of the
  scripted score with all seeds;
- the two-hot critic on its own (already shown not to learn the offset bandit without centring).

## 4. Risks to the larger goals

- **Communication needs something worth saying that the speaker can represent.** The most
  useful message in this meadow is "water is over there". The world model currently cannot
  represent that direction. If S5 fails, M3/M4 communication will have no grounded content
  beyond levels and night, all of which each sheep already feels. The 2025 world-model
  communication work above suggests that learned messages scale poorly with task complexity
  unless they are tied to the model's plans (established for their grid tasks; for FLOCK this is
  judgement).
- **M0's channel worked with nine designer interventions on a supervised referential game.** In
  RL the benefit of a message is delayed and noisy, and gets the same weak critic signal this
  review is about. Expect communication to be harder than M0 suggested. Make the critic robust
  first.
- **Long-running learning.** Continuing tasks with γ near 1 and online updates are exactly where
  centring bias, plasticity loss and policy collapse show up. The plan's fallback, a frozen
  policy with offline updates, should be treated as the default for the first installation, not
  the fallback (judgement).
- **Animal-likeness.** Every learned policy so far is either near-uniform per step or collapsed
  onto one action. Neither looks like a sheep. Add bout-length and transition statistics to the
  M1 criteria now, so that "learned" and "lifelike" are measured together. The privileged PPO
  sheep's dithering suggests a small switching cost or action persistence may be needed even
  after the learner works.
- **Schedule.** The project has spent most of M1 on the learner. If S1 and S2 do not produce a
  grid learner within about two days of GPU time, consider an interim installation path: PPO on
  the world model's latent (the hybrid, already better than random) with S5's spatial fix. That
  keeps "learned, not scripted" while the imagination learner matures.
